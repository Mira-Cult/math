# The fifth term of the greedy $B_h$ sequence

*Mira Cult*

A set of nonnegative integers is a $B_h$ set if every sum of $h$ of its
elements, with repetition allowed, determines the multiset of summands.
Start with 0 and repeatedly adjoin the least larger integer that preserves the
$B_h$ property. Write $\gamma_k(h)$ for the $k$-th positive element of the resulting
sequence. The first term is $\gamma_1(h)=1$.

[Kevin O'Bryant, *Bounds for Greedy Bₕ-Sets*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v32i2p39)
conjectured the full six-residue formula below, including the exceptional value at $h=4$, in Conjecture 9 (Conjecture 4.1 of the [preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10910v3)). We prove it and give a separate analytic proof of the leading term $h^4/3+O(h^3)$.

## Exact formula

Write $h=6k+r$ with $0\le r<6$. Set $H=h+1$, $B=h^2+h+1$ and
$C=h^2\lfloor(h+3)/2\rfloor+\lfloor3h/2\rfloor+1$. Except at $h=4$,

```math
\gamma_5(h)=cC+qB+sH+t,
```

where the coefficients are as follows. (For the proof at large $h$ these are
its canonical positional digits; the all-orders formula asserts the resulting
integer value.)

| $r$ | $c$ | $q$ | $s$ | $t$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | $4k$ | $k$ | $k$ | $k+1$ |
| 1 | $4k$ | $k+1$ | $k$ | $5k+2$ |
| 2 | $4k+1$ | $k+1$ | $k$ | $k+1$ |
| 3 | $4k+2$ | $k$ | $k+1$ | $5k+3$ |
| 4 | $4k+3$ | $k$ | $k+1$ | $k+2$ |
| 5 | $4k+3$ | $k+1$ | $k+1$ | $5k+5$ |

At $h=4$, the exact value is 153, as already tabulated by O'Bryant.
The least eventual quasiperiod is six. In particular the coefficient of
$h^4$ is $1/3$ in every residue class, so the result gives more than the
conjectured leading term.

## Proofs

- [Body-centered cubic covering](asymptotic-proof.md) gives an analytic proof of the leading term without a computation.
- [Exact formula for every order](exact-proof.md) reduces the range $h\ge60$ to 18 unbounded linear-integer decision problems, all unsatisfiable, then handles $1\le h<60$ by exact finite enumeration. It includes the least-period argument and replay instructions.
- [A solver-free derivation of the eventual formula](eventual-proof.md) explains why the same six rows arise from the geometry of the near-extremal lattice holes. It does not supply the numerical cutoff 60.

The exact all-orders argument is computer-assisted; the solver queries are
fully generated by the included source, but no formal proof trace is supplied.
The analytic asymptotic does not depend on those queries.
