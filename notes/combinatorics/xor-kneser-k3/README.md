# The exact eventual clique number of the XOR square of the 3-uniform Kneser graph

*Mira Cult*

For $n\geq 3$, the vertices of $KG(n,3)$ are the triples of an $n$-element set, with adjacency for disjoint triples. In the XOR square, two pairs of triples are adjacent if exactly one of their coordinate pairs is disjoint. Write $f_2(n,3)$ for its clique number.

**Theorem.** Put $q=\lfloor n/3\rfloor$. For every $n\geq81$,
```math
f_2(n,3)=\max\{q+27,\ 2\min(q,30)\}.
```
In particular the eventual formula $f_2(n,3)=q+27$ holds for every $n\geq99$, and fails precisely at $84\leq n\leq98$ within the range of the theorem. Thus $99$ is the least eventual threshold. Below $81$ we do not determine the exact value.

The earlier [lower construction of Füredi, Imolay and Schweitzer](https://doi.org/10.37236/14969) gives the term $q+27$. The upper bound, exact exceptions, and least eventual threshold are the contributions here.

## Packing comparison on the degree-at-most-two side

Use two labeled copies A and B of [n]; a vertex of the clique has a triple in each copy. For any two indices, their B triples intersect if and only if their A triples are disjoint. By Füredi–Imolay–Schweitzer, *Clique Number of XOR-Powers of Kneser Graphs*, [arXiv:2510.01509v1, Lemma 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01509v1#S2.SS3), a family of more than 54 members can be oriented so every B point is in at most three triples. As the target is at least 54, assume this orientation in testing any larger family.

First suppose every B point is in at most two triples. Make the simple B-intersection graph, discard isolated vertices, and let W be the number of degree-two B points (multiplicity included). If the whole clique has s members and the B support consists of b distinct physical points, then W=3s−b. Let t be the number of connected components isomorphic to K_{3,3} with one edge subdivided once. For the other components let j be their total induced matching number. By [Joos–Rautenbach–Sasse, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.1110), those components together have at most 6j vertices, so their W is at most 9j (at most three incidences per vertex). Each exceptional component has W=10: it has precisely ten simple edges, each meets an endpoint with simple degree three, so that edge cannot use two B points.

For a random ordering of all A points, say an edge separates when one of its disjoint A triples lies wholly before the other. One edge separates with probability 1/10. Edge events of anticomplete edge pairs are mutually exclusive, because all four cross A pairs intersect and the two separation events would impose incompatible strict order on two cross-intersection sites. Equivalently, the graph of separated pairs is the disjointness graph of the intervals spanning the triples, and it has no induced $2K_2$: two nonadjacent intervals that each meet both members of a disjoint pair both span the same gap, so cannot themselves be disjoint. In each exceptional component select its induced C5. The active separated edges on this C5 form an empty set or a single path of length ≤3: two disjoint nontrivial components or four edges on the full cycle force an induced 2K2, while all five edges cannot be oriented transitively by their interval order (C5 is odd and triangle-free). Hence the probability at least one separates is the sum of five edge probabilities minus the sum of five adjacent-pair probabilities. In each adjacent pair the noncentral A triples intersect and are distinct (the two noncentral vertices have different neighborhoods on the induced C5). Their union has size ≥4; the central triple must lie before this entire union or after it. Thus each joint probability is at most 2/binom(7,3)=2/35. Each exceptional component event has probability ≥1/2−2/7=3/14.

Taking a maximum induced matching on the other components and one cycle event per exceptional component yields j/10+3t/14≤1. If t≥1, W≤9j+10t≤90−65t/7<81. If t=0 and j≤9, W≤81. Either way b≤n implies s≤floor((n+81)/3)=q+27.

The only remaining degree-two case is j=10. Orient both ways the ten induced matching edges and apply the equality case of the classical Bollobás two-families theorem, in the form cited in [Füredi–Imolay–Schweitzer §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01509v1#S2.SS2). The twenty A triples at the matching endpoints are all triples of one six-set Q. Any A triple not contained in Q misses at least four of those twenty triples, since at least four sites of Q lie outside it. This is impossible: the simple B-intersection graph is subcubic. So every A triple is one of those twenty. It follows that conflicts are exactly across complementary Q-triples, so the B-intersection components are ten complete bipartite graphs K_{a,b}, 1≤a,b≤3. A vertex cannot hit more than three pairwise disjoint B triples on the other side. Within either side its B triples are pairwise disjoint. Distinct components have disjoint B support. Thus each component occupies at least 3 max(a,b) B points and contributes at most 2 max(a,b) vertices. This proves the second upper bound s≤2 min(q,30) in this branch.

## The degree-three case

A B point of degree three gives three anchor vertices whose A triples T1,T2,T3 are pairwise disjoint. The other two points of each of the anchor B triples have at most two outside incidences each. At most 12 other vertices can be adjacent in B to any anchor. Every remaining nonisolated clique vertex chooses one element from each T_i, forming a grid word in {0,1,2}³. Even if the same A triple occurs elsewhere in the clique, its multiplicity among nonisolated indices is ≤3: a B neighbor of one must meet all their pairwise disjoint B triples.

The following local observation bounds the number of regular grid vertices whose word meets a fixed grid word U but is not U; take the three coordinates of U to be zero. There are six blocks: X_i consists of the four words with exactly one zero, at i; Y_i consists of the two words whose sole nonzero is at i. Each of these blocks is A-intersecting, hence its B triples are pairwise disjoint. Disjointness between Y_i and the enumerated words is exactly with X_i. If Y_i is active, the total size of X_i is at most three; if X_i is active, the total size of Y_i is at most three. Thus a block pair with Y_i active has combined size ≤6, including the case X_i is empty (Y_i has two types). For i≠j, slice X_i and X_j by their remaining common nonzero coordinate; opposite-valued slices conflict completely across the two sides and are internally A-intersecting. Each slice consists of two types and totals ≤6; if both opposite slices are active then their totals are individually ≤3. Thus |X_i|+|X_j|≤12. With r active Y blocks, the total grid count is ≤18 for r=0,1,3, and ≤24 for r=2 (bound the remaining X block by 12 using a pair bound). All these arguments apply to induced subsets of the clique with the original multiplicity cap.

Even without a B-isolate yet, if any nonisolated grid vertex exists choose its actual grid word U. At most six indexed vertices have A disjoint from it, since its B triple meets at most six other members. At most three grid indices have its same type. All other grid words meet it and are not U, so the local bound applies. There are thus at most 33 nonisolated grid vertices, and at most 3+12+33=48 nonisolated vertices in total. (If there are no grid vertices the nonisolated core has at most 15.) Our assumed candidate size exceeds 54, so a B-isolate now exists. Its A triple meets all three T_i and hence is a grid word U. In the entire clique any index with the same A triple as that U is B-isolated (U meets every A triple). In the nonisolated grid every entry therefore meets U and differs from it, reducing the nonisolated core to at most 3+12+24=39. Since each B point has degree at most three, a core of m entries uses at least m distinct B points, so its redundancy W=3m−b_core≤2m≤78. B-isolates add three new points each, leaving W unchanged. This branch is therefore below or equal to q+27.

## Matching constructions

For the second term, take the ten complementary pairs of triples on six A sites. For each pair independently create d copies of each complementary type, with d∈{2,3} chosen so the sum of the ten d's is min(q,30); this is possible since q≥27. On a separate set of 3d B points use the edges of a 3-regular bipartite multigraph supported on K_{d,d}, one B point per physical edge and each triple the three incident physical edges at that vertex. For d=3 use simple K_{3,3}; for d=2 use K_{2,2} with a perfect matching doubled. Within a part the B triples are disjoint, between parts they intersect, and between different components all B triples are disjoint. Between different complementary pairs all A triples intersect. The two coordinates therefore satisfy XOR. This constructs 2 min(q,30) clique members using only 3 min(q,30) B sites.

For the first term, use nine of the ten complementary pairs, with the same construction and d=3 in each block. This gives 54 members on 81 B sites. Add q−27 disjoint new B triples, each paired with the same A triple chosen from the unused complementary pair. That A triple intersects all 18 used A types and itself, as required. This is the k=3 specialization of [Füredi–Imolay–Schweitzer, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01509v1#S1).

The two bounds prove the stated exact formula. Comparing q+27 and 2 min(q,30) shows strict exceptional excess exactly when 28≤q≤32, i.e. 84≤n≤98, and proves the threshold claim.

The proof uses no enumeration. The [construction checker](check-xor-kneser-k3.py) verifies the complementary-pair constructions for 81 through 102; run `python3 check-xor-kneser-k3.py`.
