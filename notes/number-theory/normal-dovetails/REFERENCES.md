# References and comparison with earlier work

## Published analytic dependency

1. **Juan Arias de Reyna**, *Explicit van der Corput's d-th derivative estimate*, arXiv:2407.02094v1, 2 July 2024. [Versioned primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.02094v1). Theorem 11, equation (15), and Remark 14, equation (19), supply the growing-order derivative estimate. The exact weaker contract used here, with constants replaced by 12, is reproduced in the main manuscript, Appendix A. The primary constants are below 11 uniformly in the derivative order. This order-uniformity is essential.

## Earlier normal-number constructions

2. **Verónica Becher, Pablo Ariel Heiber and Theodore A. Slaman**, *A computable absolutely normal Liouville number*, manuscript dated 30 January 2014. [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://math.berkeley.edu/~slaman/papers/liouville.pdf). The theorem is stronger in its base scope than the elementary fixed-base normal Liouville example reproduced here. That example is background, not a novelty claim.

3. **Manfred G. Madritsch, Jörg M. Thuswaldner and Robert F. Tichy**, *Normality of numbers generated by the values of entire functions*, Journal of Number Theory **128** (2008), 1127–1145. [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://madritsch.perso.math.cnrs.fr/downloads/papers/entireFinal.pdf). Theorems 1–2 concern integer-part concatenation from transcendental entire functions of logarithmic order $1<\alpha<4/3$, sampled at natural numbers or primes. The input tends to infinity. The present manuscript instead copies growing fractional prefixes at shrinking inputs and uses D-finite coefficient windows. The two models and their hypotheses must not be conflated.

These comparisons are a targeted literature check, not an exhaustive novelty survey. No claim that the coefficient-window theorem is historically first is made.
