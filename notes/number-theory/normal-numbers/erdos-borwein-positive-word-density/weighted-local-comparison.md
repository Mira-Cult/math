# Fixed-order weighted joint bank atoms on a certified EB incidence

Fix a>7/12, small θ as in the certified adaptive half-marker theorem, Q≥1, an upper bound R for the number of forced primes, and an error tolerance ε>0. For each incidence cofactor b≤N^θ, prescribed j0=ω(b)+1, use its actual log-weighted prime interval p∈(X−Y,X] and t=loglog X=T+O_a(1). For any Q distinct shifts j_i≠j0 of size O(T), let U_i count eligible distinct prime divisors up to a terminal bank cutoff X^c of the stripped form (b/g_i)p+(j_i−j0)/g_i, g_i=gcd(b,j_i−j0). Fixed c>0 may depend on a,Q,R,ε. For nonnegative auxiliary forced tests Z as specified below, there is a comparator weighted mass z=Σ forced weights /φ(force modulus), and

    E_p[1(U_1=a_1,...,U_Q=a_Q) Z]
       = z (2πt)^(-Q/2) exp[−Σ_i(a_i−t)^2/(2t)]
         + O(ε z t^(-Q/2)) + O(exp(−κt)),

uniformly on any prescribed fixed central box |a_i−t|≤C sqrt(t). The model same bound for the all-atom comparison has comparator point probabilities in place of the displayed Gaussian. This is a uniform arbitrary fixed accuracy, not an automatic little-o with c fixed.

**Admissible forced weights.** Z is a positive sum of indicators of one reduced prime class modulo each force modulus, optionally with repetitions or nonnegative coefficients. A force modulus has ≤R distinct primes (fixed), total exponent as a power of X below a sufficiently small fixed share of the short-BV level. After adjoining a complete mixed factorial-test modulus, the weighted multiplicity of recovered forced choices must be at most exp(O(t)); the usual bounded-order forced distinct-prime and clipped prime-power test products satisfy this (for power tests the maximal exponent identifies the prime powers up to O((log X)^R) same-prime decompositions and finitely many bank overlaps). Incompatible or nonreduced classes that cannot capture p in the shell are discarded. The lemma does not cover tests that prescribe complete remaining factorization or odd-divisor residue.

**Proof: comparator.** At each prime q give p mod q uniform measure on the reduced classes. Increases for all Q coordinates at q use this same class; they need not be independent. The comparator is independent over q. For q>C T, q∤b, every shift class is reduced and eligible and all Q classes are distinct. Any force prime fixes its entire vector increment after reading its given class mod q, and there are at most R such primes. The omitted reciprocal mass from primes dividing b or a shift is O(log T) uniformly. For q>T^B with sufficiently large fixed B, replacing the categorical increment by independent Bernoulli trials for each eligible label costs total TV O_Q(T^-B), including after force conditioning. Each resulting coordinate has mean t+O(log T) and variance t+O(log T) in the first bank ending at X^(1/T^8); later banks together change these by O(log T). Below T^B the sum of all shifts' increments has an exponential moment exp(O_Q(loglog T)): each q contributes at most Q, hits at least one reduced class with probability O_Q(1/(q−1)), and forces add at most QR. Thus the vector of small-prime increments is O(t^1/4) except with probability exp(−c t^1/4), uniformly. The independent coordinate Gaussian lattice LLT for the high-prime part (Fourier inversion with variance t+O(log T) and third absolute moments O(t)) gives the product formula above with relative o(1) at central atoms after averaging this small part. The force comparator denominator is φ(m) for its congruence modulo m. At every stage even before this Gaussian substitution, the uniform max atom is O_Q(t^-Q/2), by integrating the joint CF: at distinct-label q the squared modulus is ≤1−c(q−1)^−1 Σ_i sin²(u_i/2), charging pairs of each occupied label against the empty label. The first bank alone supplies the needed reciprocal mass ≍t.

**Proof: weighted arithmetic comparison.** Use the short-BV input as sourced in [marked tail](marked-tail.md), with arbitrary fixed inverse-log savings and a positive exponent ρ after prime-power removal. Use r_1=ceil(C_Q t) and later reversed-geometric banks with r_i=ceil(C_Q L_(i−1)), L_0=T, L_i=log L_(i−1), stopping at the last L_(i−1)≥H0 (fixed large). Divide all later shares by a sufficiently large constant depending on a,Q so the entire Q-coordinate mixed modulus exponent is <ρ/2. The terminal share is then fixed and its last r_i is bounded in terms of H0 and Q, so x_terminal=X^c for exponents in a fixed compact positive range; to require a single fixed c choose the minimum terminal exponent and truncate terminally there. Enlarging H0 if necessary ensures this remains above the preceding endpoint uniformly, since the preceding log cutoff index grows exponentially in r_last; alternatively allow the terminal positive exponent within that compact range. The bank means, per label, are O_Q(L_(i−1)), while the first is ∼t. An assignment to a given bank modulus chooses a nonempty subset of at most Q labels per prime, costing exp(O_Q(t)) across all degrees; force weights add only the allowed exp(O(t)) multiplicity. BV pays the absolute errors uniformly, using the maximum AP error when different assignments choose different classes.

For a fixed prime force, keep its independent-by-prime comparator, with at most R deterministic increments. At stage i compare true weighted exact combined atom a to its comparator, for all further-bank binomial weights H. The inductive error is ε_i h t^-Q/2+δ_i, where h is the effective comparator weight of H (aggregate over forces). Start with multivariate binomial Taylor up to r_1−1 in every first-bank coordinate. For a nonempty subset of Taylor endpoints, write Z_i for the number of primes hitting any coordinate in that bank. Total coordinate count is ≤Q Z_i, and a fixed tilt s>1 bounds a degree-k positive factorial coefficient by s^-k E(1+s)^(Q Z_i) ≤s^-k exp(O_(Q,s)(μ_i+R)), where μ_i bounds the largest coordinate mean. For example the product of endpoint binomials is bounded by the relevant total coefficient times its multinomial assignment cost, absorbable by choosing s large. Hence each endpoint product with at least one degree r_i is exp(−C r_i) after selecting the large fixed multiplier in r_i. The first-bank weighted remainder is h exp(−c t), while arbitrary fixed BV saving pays coefficients exp(O_Q(t)) and all future factorial bounds exp(O_Q(log T loglog T)). This supplies ε_1,δ_1 exponentially small in t.

Add each later bank by Q-dimensional Fourier inversion at a fixed earlier atom a−b. For each Taylor coefficient apply the preceding weighted estimate with that coefficient's nonnegative binomial weight appended to H. The sum of lower-coefficient inflation is exp(O_Q(μ_i+R)) from the same generating function, and the additive error inflation is O_Q(3^(Q r_i)). Interior b in [0,r_i)^Q costs r_i^Q possibilities. For any exterior coordinates weight the earlier atom by the corresponding binomials at order r_i, divide by their values at the exterior counts and sum their reciprocals; each costs r_i/(r_i−1), not a full lattice support. Nonexterior coordinates then cost at most r_i^Q. Thus ε_i≤C_Q r_i^Q[exp(O_Q(μ_i+R))ε_(i−1)+(1+ε_(i−1))exp(−C r_i)], and δ_i gains at most C_Q r_i^Q 3^(Q r_i). For fixed Q,R, choosing H0 large guarantees factor-ten successive degree separation and absorbs all fixed constants, yielding ε_i≤exp(−r_i) and δ_i=exp(−κt) since Σ_(i≥2)r_i=O_Q(log T). At forced primes the comparator is conditional on the forced residues, independent across banks; sum the relative terms with their positive 1/φ weights and pay the absolute BV errors collectively. Take H0 large enough for ε_d≤ε (after constants), then the comparator Gaussian gives the announced estimate.

**Exactly what this funds.** For finitely many fixed-width bank strips on central offsets, factorial incidence moments through any fixed order Q can now use true joint atoms with arbitrary fixed relative accuracy (cluster size O(sqrt t) exactly cancels each lattice factor); this remains true with the admissible bounded-order forced weights. It furnishes the marked Bonferroni input for *bank* avoidance. A final conditional isolation theorem would still need a summable, quantitatively appropriate positive far-angle majorant under those marked tests and the exact complement itself is not a bounded-force weight. No true-completion independence, full phase separation, or word frequency follows.
