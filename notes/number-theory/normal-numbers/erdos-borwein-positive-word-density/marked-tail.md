# Size-biased actual phase truncation at certified adaptive markers in short windows

Fix 7/12<a<1. Choose θ0>0 small enough that (a−θ0)/(1−θ0)>a1>7/12 and a−θ0>1/2. For fixed 0<θ≤θ0 let F(n) count the certified squarefree adaptive half markers from [adaptive marker count](adaptive-marker-count.md), i.e. incidences b≤N^θ squarefree, j0=ω(b)+1≤C T, n=bp−j0, T=loglog N. Use ordinary ambient expectation on [N,N+H], N^a≤H≤N. Uniformly in those intervals, for M≥1, fixed h≠0 and J=ceil(C' T) with C'≥max(C,10), one has

    E_n Σ_(certified j0) Σ_(0≤j≤J, j≠j0, D_j>M) |e(h d(n+j)/2^(j+1))−1|
       ≪_(a,h) θ/(1+M) + o(1),

where D_j=j+1−v₂(d(n+j)). The far real tail after J is also o(θ) in this incidence mean, for fixed θ. Therefore the marked incidence law itself has complementary phase pruning error O_(a,h)(1/M)+o(1), since E_n F≍θ. No restriction to n with multiple markers is needed; on F=1 the same bound follows by domination, and P(F=1)≳_a θ for sufficiently small fixed θ.

**Short-prime input, fixed incidence.** For given b the actual p interval has endpoint X≍N/b, length Y=H/b≥X^a1, and Y≫sqrt N at fixed power margin. Use the short-interval Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem of [Huxley–Iwaniec](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300006069), in the uniform translated-interval formulation (equation (2) and the Huxley–Iwaniec exponent ranges in [Perelli–Pintz–Salerno](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASNSP_1984_4_11_4_529_0.pdf)). At any fixed exponent a1>7/12 it gives some positive power modulus range and arbitrary fixed logarithmic saving. Exact log p weights are used through the moment argument: the theorem is stated for von Mangoldt weights; removing prime powers for each modulus costs O(sqrt X log²X), even using a full range bound. Shrink the admissible modulus exponent ρ>0 so ρ+1/2<a1. Over at most X^ρ moduli this total is O(X^(ρ+1/2) log²X)=o(Y/log^A X) for every fixed A. Dividing by actual unrestricted prime log mass ≍Y preserves arbitrary savings, with the denominator normalization paid by the modulus-one case. The theorem's interval-length exponent can be selected on the actual scale Y (if desired use its shorter fixed exponent range strictly below a1 and partition the interval into pieces of comparable lengths); its maximum over lengths and translated intervals gives the same bound by summing, with no short final piece required.

For j≠j0 write g=gcd(b,j−j0), A=b/g, c=(j−j0)/g. The affine form Ap+c is positive on the prime window, even if c<0. For any tested modulus its divisibility has one class when all tested primes are coprime to A; nonunit classes cannot capture a prime in the window because the test moduli are below X^ρ. If a test prime divides A the condition is impossible since gcd(A,c)=1. Omitting these primes changes the bank mean by O(log T) uniformly, by Mertens up to (log N)^2 and the size bound on b above that. The first model bank mean equals t_b+O(log T), t_b=loglog X=T+O_a(1), and variance ≍T uniformly.

**Uniform local envelope on this incidence.** Apply the existing weighted reversed-geometric-share proof of [integrated deficit estimate](integrated-deficit.md) to this form with all shares reduced by a fixed a-dependent factor, so the bank product moduli and the at-most-four additional forced power tests have exponent below ρ. Here the terminal bank exponent remains a fixed positive constant (choose the fixed large stopping height H0 before N→∞); its center shifts by a fixed constant only. Choose the upper-prime forced-test endpoint and repeated-power cutoff X^η with 4η plus bank share <ρ. The remaining primes >X^η contribute O_a(1) total multiplicity deterministically since Ap+c≪N and X≥N^(1−θ0). For repeated increments at q between X^(1/T^1/4) and X^η, interval BT on q² (shrink η if necessary) gives exponentially tiny event probability O(exp(−c log X/T^1/4)); their multiplicity bound is O_a(T^1/4). For q below that cutoff, discard powers greater than X^η by testing the largest power below X^η, which is ≥X^η/q: BT and summing q≤X^(1/T^1/4) gives probability X^(−η+o(1)), and the total multiplicity's worst fourth moment O((log N)^4) is harmless. These repeat estimates cover all non-deterministic supplementary tests. Equivalently any optional check on prime squares beyond X^.2 is paid by O(Y X^−.2+sqrt N) raw integer candidates, divided by ≍Y/log X. The moment assignment and exponential-coefficient budgets are unaltered because shrinking shares only shifts the later means by bounded constants, and short-interval BV has arbitrary fixed inverse-log savings. The same local weighted atom envelope therefore holds:

    Pr_b(V_j=v, R_j+G≥r) ≪_a (1+G)^4 f(v)/[sqrt(T)(1+r²)],

for relevant v=O(T), f(v)=(1+(v−T)²/T)^−1, G=Ω(g), and R_j the stripped repeat count. This uses the direct comparison of the genuine valuation to the stripped distinct-prime baseline; no Lipschitz claim for two divisor valuations. The true odd part has log₂ at most 2(R_j+G). The bounded center and shift from t_b to T are absorbed by the envelope, and the exponentially small absolute error is negligible at every v=O(T). Conversion from log weights to ordinary prime proportions changes only bounded factors for these positive upper estimates.

**Incidence averaging with adaptive gcd.** Its ambient mass for a fixed b is O_a(1/(b log N)); the normalizer λ=E_n F≍θ. Expand (1+G)^4≤Σ_(d|g) c(d) with the positive divisor coefficients of the elementary squarefree divisor expansion (for a divisor with $`j`$ prime factors, take the $`j`$th forward difference of $`(1+t)^4`$ at $`t=0`$; these coefficients are nonnegative and vanish for $`j>4`$. The fixed anchor costs only a constant factor), satisfying Σ c(d)/d²<∞ and Σ_(d≤Z)c(d)/d≪(1+loglog(3Z))^4. As j0 depends on b, first sum in j on fixed b: d|g implies j≡j0 mod d and, as j≠j0, d≤J+CT. For every k the translates of f obey

    Σ_(j≤J, j≡j0 mod d) f(j+1−k)/sqrt(T) ≪ 1/d + 1/sqrt(T),

uniformly in j0. Only after this sum average b. For d≤J+CT~T,

    (1/log N) Σ_(b≤N^θ,d|b)1/b ≤ (1+θ log N)/(d log N) ≪ θ/d

for all sufficiently large N at fixed θ. Thus the translated envelope summed over j and weighted by the ambient incidence measure is O_a(θ), using the convergent d^-2 sum and the o(1) secondary term. At deficit k>M, split R_j+G<k/4 (angle error ≤2π|h|2^(-k/2)) versus its complement (probability decay O(1/(1+k²))). Summing k yields the displayed θ/(1+M) upper bound. This proof keeps the harmonic factor θ after reversing the order of summation, rather than treating the adaptive gcd distribution as if j0 were fixed.

The far real tail can use F(n)≤J+O(1) and an ambient elementary divisor average O(log N+sqrt N/H) at each shift through 3log₂N, with deterministic bound after that cutoff. Thus its size-biased mean is O(T log N·2^-J+T N^-2)=o(θ). Products telescope in unit modulus; all early omitted nonpositive deficits are neutral, and the chosen term at j0 is excluded as desired.

**Scope of this estimate.** The θ-scaled result is conditional tail control, not conditional isolation of the chosen half factor. A complementary finite-deficit statistic with tiny probability e^(-cM) still cannot dominate O(1/M) without more localized control. Neither same-sign alignment nor any specified word frequency follows.

**Exact source check for the short-BV hypothesis.** The publicly readable [Perelli–Pintz–Salerno](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASNSP_1984_4_11_4_529_0.pdf), printed pp. 529–530, displays (2) in the translated-interval max form: sum over q≤Q of the maximum absolute von Mangoldt discrepancy (max over reduced a, 0<h≤y, x/2≤z≤x), bounded by y/(log x)^A with Q=x^ν/(log x)^B, y=x^ϑ. It explicitly attributes to Huxley–Iwaniec the admissible ν<3ϑ−7/4−ε in 7/12<ϑ≤29/48. Choose ϑ strictly in that band below the uniform exponent a1 for the actual interval and fix a positive ν there. Subdivide a longer prime interval into O(Y/x^ϑ) adjacent pieces of length ≤x^ϑ, splitting across dyadic boundaries if needed. The max over lengths pays even the final shorter piece at the same cost. Summing absolute discrepancies gives O(Y/(log X)^A) uniformly on the actual physical p interval. The original [Huxley–Iwaniec article](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300006069) is bibliographically verified, but the formula in its publicly accessible extract is an unreadable formula image; the explicit displayed function, maxima and numerical range used here were checked against the cited print pages of the complementary primary paper, not inferred from the title or from an average-over-centers theorem.
