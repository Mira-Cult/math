# A genuine order-three cube obstruction at every conductor 3^a

Author: `/root/normalization`. New supplemental result, with manuscript-local
labels TC1--TC3. This file does not modify the scientific source
`d9bd9ac92851a9996c14dd2fdf49f1879bcae97b`, its CYC theorem, its canonical
pools, or its seven positive fixtures. Mathematical review and the independent
certificate replay are to be recorded separately at the supplemental revision.

**Result.** For every a>=1, no placement of 27 unramified prime elements of
Q(zeta_(3^a)), each in the frozen first-embedding torsion sector, can have
prime sums on all 49 full signed-coordinate lines of a 3-by-3-by-3 cube.
In particular the exact first 27 members of the prescribed K9 energy pool
cannot form such a cube. This is a finite arithmetic obstruction proved by
a small exact combinatorial certificate, not a conclusion from a failed
prime-array search.

The obstruction needs the first torsion sector and the omission of ramified
prime classes. It does not depend on energy minimization, a logarithmic
unit cell, class number one, the size or ordering of the entries, or their
distinctness. Consequently it applies to the frozen pools without changing
their normalization. It is consistent with CYC, which asserts a sufficiently
large complete tail and does not assert existence at order three.

## 1. Full units and the exceptional ramified associates

Set c=3^a, K=Q(zeta_c), and let sigma1(zeta_c)=exp(2 pi i/c). Put

    rho = -zeta_c^((c+1)/2),     w=2c,
    theta=pi/(2c),              beta=1+rho.

Thus rho is the root of unity with first argument pi/c=2 theta. The canonical
first sector is [-theta,theta). All roots of unity in K form mu_(2c).
There is one prime ideal P above 3, generated by 1-zeta_c, with residue
field F3. It is totally ramified. Since (c+1)/2 is prime to c,

    beta = 1-zeta_c^((c+1)/2),       |N_K/Q(beta)|=Phi_c(1)=3,

and v_P(beta)=1. These are the standard prime-power cyclotomic ramification
facts; see Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*, v3.08, Chapter 6, notably
Theorem 6.4, or the cyclotomic foundations used in GN3.

**Lemma TC1 (full CM unit index and sector exclusion).** Every unit of K is
a product of a root of unity and a unit of K^+=Q(zeta_c+zeta_c^(-1)). Every
associate of beta lies outside the open first sector (-theta,theta).
Every integral element lying on either boundary ray is divisible by P.

**Proof.** If u is a unit then u/conj(u) is an algebraic integral unit and
has absolute value one in every embedding. Kronecker's theorem therefore
gives u/conj(u)=rho^k for an integer k. This use of Kronecker is precisely
the usual full-unit argument, not a restriction to convenient cyclotomic
units; compare Milne, Chapter 5, and GN3.

If k is even, multiplication of u by rho^(-k/2) makes it real. The resulting
element and its inverse are integral and fixed by conjugation, hence are
units of O_(K^+).

If k is odd, multiply u by a root of unity so that the resulting unit v
satisfies v/conj(v)=rho. Since

    (1+rho)/(1+conj(rho))=rho,

the quotient t=v/(1+rho) belongs to K^+. But K/K^+ has ramification index
two at the unique prime above 3. Therefore v_P(t) is even for every
t in (K^+)^times. On the other hand v_P(t)=0-1=-1, a contradiction.
Thus k is always even and the full unit assertion follows.

The first argument of beta is theta, since 1+exp(2 i theta) is a positive
real multiple of exp(i theta). A real unit contributes phase zero or pi,
and a root of unity contributes an integer multiple of 2 theta. Consequently
every beta-associate has first phase theta modulo 2 theta. No such phase is
strictly between -theta and theta, even when the associate has arbitrarily
large height. This excludes all exceptional associates by angle alone.

If alpha is integral on the upper boundary, alpha/beta has real first
embedding and therefore belongs to K^+. Its P-valuation is even. It follows
that v_P(alpha)=1+2j is an odd nonnegative integer, hence is positive.
For the lower boundary use conj(beta), also of P-valuation one. Every
integral boundary element is therefore divisible by P. QED.

The same proof of the full-unit and boundary assertions works for any odd
prime-power conductor p^a, replacing 3 by p. Only the subsequent F3
combinatorics is specific to powers of three.

**Corollary TC1a.** Every unramified prime element normalized into
[-theta,theta) is strictly inside that sector. Every nonempty ordinary sum
of such elements is nonzero and strictly inside it. If such a sum is
divisible by P, it is neither a unit nor a prime element.

Indeed, a prime boundary element would generate P and would be ramified,
so all retained inputs are interior. The open cone

    Re(z)>0,     |Im(z)|<tan(theta) Re(z)

is closed under addition. A nonempty sum has positive real part. Divisibility
by P excludes units; if a divisible sum were prime it would generate P and
be a beta-associate, which TC1 excludes from the cone. No norm-growth or
bounded-associate argument is used.

## 2. Exact finite combinatorial certificate

Let V={0,1,2}^3. Its full signed-coordinate lines consist of the triples

    { x+t d : t=0,1,2 },

where d belongs to {-1,0,1}^3 minus zero and all three points are in V.
Reversing a line does not create a new line. There are 27 axis lines,
18 diagonals in coordinate slices, and four body diagonals, for a total of
49. Every line contains three entries with coefficient +1 in an ordinary
sum; the signs describe its spatial direction only.

**Lemma TC2 (finite non-two-colorability).** Every map V->{0,1} is constant
on at least one of these 49 lines.

The exact certificate is
[ternary_cube3_unsat.json](../inputs/ternary_cube3_unsat.json). Its SHA-256 is
`dd55e8f59cf73c47afce579e413d7572d744485c06feeeaecf636efa3ab316b9`.
It proves the lemma by the following elementary exhaustive inference rules.

1. Globally exchange the two colors if necessary so that the center has
   color zero. This is the only symmetry reduction used.
2. If two assigned cells of a required line have the same color, its third
   cell is forced to have the opposite color in any coloring without a
   monochromatic line.
3. At a branch, select an unassigned cell and include both possible colors
   as separate children. Every possible extension belongs to one child.
4. A leaf displays an actual required line whose three assigned colors are
   equal. No extension of that leaf can avoid a monochromatic line.

The displayed tree has 31 nodes: 15 branches and 16 conflicting leaves.
There are 220 individually recorded forced assignments, and its maximum
branch depth is five. By induction upward from the leaves, the root has no
valid extension. Color exchange covers every coloring. This proves TC2 by
a finite check of explicit assignments; the heuristic used to select branch
cells is irrelevant to the validity of the proof.

The producer
[generate_ternary_cube_obstruction.py](../code/generate_ternary_cube_obstruction.py)
enumerates lines by their signed direction vectors. The author replay program
[replay_ternary_cube_tree.py](../code/replay_ternary_cube_tree.py) independently
reconstructs all lines from endpoint pairs with coordinate differences in
{-2,0,2}, verifies every forcing rule, checks both children at every branch,
and checks every monochromatic conflict. It imports no producer or other
project module. These two programs have the same author; their different
implementations are not an independent authorship review. A separate reviewer
can replay the small certificate without reproducing any prime search.

The root author also implemented the separate independent verifier
[check_ternary_cube_obstruction.py](../code/check_ternary_cube_obstruction.py),
which imports neither author program and verifies the same elementary
inference rules. Its observed replay confirms all 31 nodes, 16 conflict
leaves, 220 forced assignments, and depth five, and rejects eight corrupted
certificates. The proof uses 47 of the 49 available required lines; the
checker still reconstructs the entire 49-line family before checking every
used support.

From the problem directory, the author commands are:

## 3. Arithmetic consequence and the exact K9 pool

**Theorem TC3 (order-three cube impossibility).** For every a>=1, a cube
whose 27 entries are unramified prime elements of Q(zeta_(3^a)) in the
canonical first torsion sector has at least one nonprime required line sum.

**Proof.** Reduction modulo P sends every entry to one of the two units
of F3, namely +1 and -1. Color the cells according to these residues.
Three elements of {+1,-1} sum to zero in F3 exactly when they are all equal:
three equal signs give +3 or -3, whereas a mixed triple has sum +1 or -1.
By TC2 some required line is monochromatic, so its ordinary element sum
is divisible by P. Corollary TC1a proves that this sum is nonzero, is not a
unit, and is not any exceptional ramified-prime associate. It is therefore
not a prime element. QED.

The prescribed K9 pool omits precisely the ramified prime class above 3,
so every one of its entries meets these hypotheses. For context, its exact
first 27 energy representatives have norm cutoff 127. There are 31 prime
ideals through that cutoff, and the specified coefficient tie selects the
initial 27. Their residue counts modulo P are eleven +1 and sixteen -1.
For a coefficient tuple alpha=(a0,...,a5), this residue is simply
sum(a_j) modulo 3 because zeta_9 maps to one. The producer calculation was
independently reproduced by the already frozen energy checker: its energy-18
ellipsoid has 2215 lattice points, its exact splitting counts cover all
31 ideals through norm 127, and its Minkowski certificate has conservative
integer bound five. These counts are not needed for TC3, which rules out
every possible two-coloring and hence every ordering of this exact pool.

The previous unsuccessful K9 order-three cube search can consequently be
reclassified using this theorem as an impossible order. The unsuccessful
search itself remains logically irrelevant.
