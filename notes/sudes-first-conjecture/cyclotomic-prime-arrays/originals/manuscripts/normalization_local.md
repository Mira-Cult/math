# Full units, a frozen canonical pool, and local arithmetic

Author: `/root/normalization`. Manuscript-local labels only; no permanent claim
identifiers are allocated. This file proves the normalization and elementary
local interfaces for the eventual-array composition. A finite computation is
not used to establish any universal statement below. The prime-distribution
input in §5 is supplied and audited separately in the analytic manuscript.

## N1. Fields, bases, units, and prime ideals

Write `z=exp(2*pi*i/m)`, use the integral basis `(1,z,z^2,z^3)`, and take the
ordered complex embeddings `sigma_1(z)=z`, `sigma_2(z)=z^s`, with `s=2,3,5`
for `m=5,8,12` respectively. The other embeddings are their conjugates.

| m | Phi_m(X) | discriminant | maximal real subfield | torsion generator rho, order w | free generator u | lambda=abs(sigma_1(u)) |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
|5|X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1|125|Q(sqrt(5))|`-z^3`, 10|`-z^2-z^3=(1+sqrt(5))/2`|`(1+sqrt(5))/2`|
|8|X^4+1|256|Q(sqrt(2))|`z`, 8|`1+z-z^3=1+sqrt(2)`|`1+sqrt(2)`|
|12|X^4-X^2+1|144|Q(sqrt(3))|`z`, 12|`1+z`|`sqrt(2+sqrt(3))`|

**Proposition N1 (full groups).** Each ring has class number one, and its entire
unit group is the direct product `mu_w x <u>` with the displayed generators.
For m=5,8 the real units and roots of unity already generate all units. For
m=12 their index in the full unit group is two.

**Proof.** Cyclotomic integrality gives the indicated bases; discriminants follow
by the resultant of `Phi_m` and its derivative. Minkowski's ideal-class bound
in a quartic field with two complex places is

`(4!/4^4)(4/pi)^2 sqrt(discriminant) = 3 sqrt(discriminant)/(2 pi^2)`.

For discriminants 125 and 144 it is less than 2. For 256 it is less than 3,
and the sole prime of norm two is generated by `1-z`. Every ideal class
therefore has a principal representative. This proves class number one.

The real-subfield integer rings are respectively `Z[(1+sqrt(5))/2]`,
`Z[sqrt(2)]`, `Z[sqrt(3)]`. Their positive real fundamental units are
`phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2`, `1+sqrt(2)`, and `2+sqrt(3)`. Here is an elementary
minimality verification, so a convenient unit is not silently assumed
fundamental. If a positive real quadratic unit x exceeds one, its conjugate
is `+/-1/x`; the coefficient of the square root is consequently
`(x-x')/(2sqrt(d))`, with the denominator `sqrt(5)` for the half-integral
basis. Between 1 and the displayed proposed generator this positive
coefficient is strictly below the smallest permitted positive integer.
For d=3 the norm-negative case is impossible modulo 3. Division by powers
of the proposed generator then reduces every positive unit to that empty
interval or to 1. Signs give all real units.

For any complex-field unit v, `v/conj(v)` is an algebraic integer with every
conjugate of absolute value one. It is a root of unity (Kronecker's elementary
argument: the powers have bounded integral characteristic polynomials, hence
some two powers coincide). The roots of unity in the three fields have the
orders w in the table. To check completeness, a root of unity in a quartic
field has degree dividing four, so its order is among
`1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12`. The quadratic subfields exclude any additional order:
K5 has only Q(sqrt(5)); K8 has Q(i), Q(sqrt(2)), Q(sqrt(-2)); K12 has Q(i),
Q(sqrt(3)), Q(sqrt(-3)). These subfields can also be read directly from the
four automorphisms.

For m=5,8 suppose `v/conj(v)=rho^j` with j odd. Multiplying v by a torsion
unit reduces this quotient to rho. Since `(1+rho)/(1+rho^-1)=rho`, the
quotient `v/(1+rho)` then belongs to the real subfield. But `1+rho` has
valuation exactly one at the unique prime above 5 or 2: its absolute norm
is respectively 5 or 2. The extension from the real subfield to K has
ramification index two there. Every real-subfield element, including a
fractional one, has even valuation in K. Thus v would have odd valuation,
contradicting that it is a unit. Hence j is even, a torsion adjustment makes
v real, and the real unit calculation finishes these two cases.

For m=12, `u=1+z` is a unit because `Phi_12(-1)=1`, and
`u/conj(u)=z`. Multiplication by a power of u makes the conjugation quotient
of any unit equal to one. The remaining real unit is a sign times a power
of `2+sqrt(3)=z^-1 u^2`. This proves the full group assertion. Since u has
conjugation quotient z, an odd exponent cannot be a root times a real unit;
the index is exactly two. No other unit-index assertion is needed. QED.

The LMFDB records 4.0.125.1, 4.0.256.1, and 4.0.144.1 independently agree
with the discriminants, class numbers, ranks and regulators. Their polynomial
for K5 uses a primitive tenth root, so its printed fundamental unit must not
be copied into the fifth-root basis without conversion. The proof above
certifies the exact units used here rather than relying on a numerical table.

## N2. Balanced representatives and finite effective prefixes

Put `L=log(lambda)>0` and `a=pi/w`. For every nonzero element alpha first
multiply by the unique power of u that puts

`t(alpha)=log(abs(sigma_1(alpha))/abs(sigma_2(alpha)))` in `[-L,L)`.

Then multiply by the unique torsion power that puts
`arg(sigma_1(alpha))` in `[-a,a)`. Denote the result by `can(alpha)`.
The interval endpoints are included on the left and excluded on the right.
This is the canonical choice used below. Unit multiplication is commutative,
but these two normalization maps need not commute for K12: balancing is
performed first, followed by torsion-sector selection.

The pool consists of these representatives for every prime-element associate
class, omitting exactly the classes of prime ideals above the rational primes
ramified in K. Thus one class is omitted for m=5, one for m=8, and two for
m=12. Order by `(absolute norm,a0,a1,a2,a3)`, with the coefficient tuple in
ordinary ascending lexicographic order. An incomplete final norm tie is kept
in that order. No unramified class is discarded.

**Proposition N2 (canonical pool).** The preceding procedure is unique,
complete, effective, and has finite norm prefixes. Its members are in
bijection with the unramified prime ideals of K.

**Proof.** Multiplication by u changes t by `2L` because the two complex
absolute values of u are lambda and lambda^-1. The first half-open interval
therefore contains exactly one point of every free-unit orbit. Torsion leaves
t unchanged, and its first-embedding rotations are exactly the w rotations
by `2pi/w`; the second half-open interval contains exactly one of them. N1
shows these are all associates. Every prime ideal is principal by N1;
prime elements, meaning generators of prime ideals, are therefore captured.

No transcendental comparison is required. If `A=abs(sigma_1(alpha))^2`
and `B=abs(sigma_2(alpha))^2`, balance is exactly

`lambda^-2 <= A/B < lambda^2`.

These are comparisons of real algebraic numbers (in the relevant real
quadratic field). Sector membership is checked by the signs of the oriented
areas with its two boundary rays and positive real projection. The rays are
algebraic roots of unity in a finite extension, so exact isolating intervals
and equality testing decide every boundary. In particular, floating point
rounding does not decide a tie. One may simply multiply by u or its inverse
until the balance comparisons hold, then test the finite torsion list.

For `N(alpha)<=B0`, put `R=B0^(1/4)`. Balance gives both embedding bounds
`abs(sigma_j(alpha)) <= sqrt(lambda) R`. The inverse embedding matrix gives
a coefficient box `abs(a_i)<=4R` in all three fields. For completeness: in K8
the four primitive eighth roots give the orthogonal Fourier bound
`abs(a_i)<=max_j abs(sigma_j(alpha))`; in K5 Fourier differences give
`abs(a_i)<=(8/5) max_j abs(sigma_j(alpha))`; in K12 write the two embeddings
as `x_j+i y_j`. Then

`a1=(x1-x2)/sqrt(3)`, `a2=(y1-y2)/sqrt(3)`,
`a0=(x1+x2)/2-a2/2`, `a3=(y1+y2)/2-a1/2`.

These bounds are all less than `4R`. Thus a finite integral box contains
every normalized element of bounded norm. Enumerating the box, applying
exact normalization and primality tests, and sorting gives the prefix.
Increasing B0 until its pool count reaches the desired length terminates,
for example by the infinitely many primes split completely in this fixed
cyclotomic field (Dirichlet). QED.

An exact prime-element certificate may be phrased entirely over finite fields.
Let alpha have integer norm `p^f` for a rational prime p. Compute
`gcd(Phi_m mod p, alpha(X) mod p)`. If this is an irreducible polynomial of
degree f, then `(alpha)` is the corresponding prime ideal: it is contained
in that ideal, and both indices are `p^f`. Conversely every prime element
has just such a certificate. Rational-prime norm is the case f=1, not the
whole criterion. Zero and norm-one units are excluded before this test.

For unramified p the residue degrees are `ord_m(p)` and the number of
prime ideals above p is `4/ord_m(p)`. Hence K5 includes rational inert
prime elements p for p congruent to 2 or 3 modulo 5, of norm p^4, and two
prime-element classes of norm p^2 when p is 4 modulo 5. K8 and K12 have
no unramified rational inert primes: their nonidentity Frobenius elements
have order two, giving two prime classes of norm p^2. A rational p in those
split cases is composite even though each prime factor has non-prime rational
norm. This explicitly retains all higher-residue-degree classes.

## N3. Comparison with minimal coefficient height

There is a second valid model, but it is not substituted into the theorem.
For each associate class minimize `H(alpha)=max_i abs(a_i)`, retain the sign
whose first nonzero coefficient is positive, and then choose the
lexicographically least coefficient tuple among the minimizers. This rule
is unique and effective: any candidate generator provides an upper bound
H0, so every minimizer lies in the finite box `[-H0,H0]^4`. Exact division
in K determines whether a candidate beta satisfies `beta/alpha` integral
with norm one, which is equivalent to being an integral unit. Enumerating
this box therefore tests the entire relevant part of the associate class.
This avoids assuming a unit basis merely to define the representative.

Balanced representatives give `H<=4B^(1/4)` in each norm prefix, so minimal
height representatives obey the same upper bound and have finite complete
prefixes too. However, finite definability and this outer bound do not prove
a usable centroid or a symmetric endpoint region for that different domain.
No such geometric theorem is claimed here. Its line sums differ from those
of N2. The balanced-log model is chosen because its geometry is explicit,
not because the two models have been proved equivalent.

## N4. Exact local ledger and the exceptional-associate issue

The relevant small quotient rings are as follows. All factorizations are
identities over the stated finite fields.

| Field | rational prime | polynomial factorization | quotient ring and residue field |
|---|---:|---|---|
|K5|2|Phi_5 irreducible|F16|
|K5|5|(X-1)^4|F5[X]/((X-1)^4), residue F5|
|K8|2|(X+1)^4|F2[X]/((X+1)^4), residue F2|
|K12|2|(X^2+X+1)^2|F2[X]/((X^2+X+1)^2), residue F4|
|K12|3|(X^2+1)^2|F3[X]/((X^2+1)^2), residue F9|

The irreducibility assertion for K5 at 2 follows because 2 has multiplicative
order four modulo 5. The displayed quadratic factors are irreducible in
F2 and F3 respectively. Thus only K8 has a residue field of size two.

For every ideal q and every finite prefix P of M entries, retain the exact
counts `C_b(P;q)=#{alpha in P:alpha=b mod q}` and the exact total T. They obey

`sum_b C_b=M`, `T mod q=sum_b C_b b`.

Every row partition, every column partition, and every axis-direction partition
of a cube sums exactly to T, hence obeys this identity after reduction. These
identities use the actual prefix, including its last tie. Higher incidence
identities among cube face lines are preserved by reducing the complete
integer incidence equations coordinatewise; a favorable residue histogram
alone is not a complete permutation construction.

For an unramified prime ideal q, a prime input can have residue zero only
when its ideal equals q, so at most one member of the full canonical pool
has that residue. For a ramified q the count is exactly zero because all
such input classes were omitted. This is different from pretending that
every input is a unit modulo every modulus: exceptional unramified input
classes must remain in finite counts and can be reserved separately by a
controller.

**Proposition N4 (strict-sector exception exclusion).** In K5 and K8 no
ordinary nonempty sum of canonical unramified prime elements is zero or
an associate of the unique ramified prime. Consequently in K8 no even-order
square or cube, with n>=2, has all the required prime-element line sums.

**Proof.** Let pi generate the ramified prime. For K5 use a root multiple
of `1-z` with first argument `-pi/10`; for K8 use `z-z^2`, of first
argument `-pi/8`. Such a root multiple exists since the argument of
`1-exp(2pi i/m)` is `pi/m-pi/2`. All free units in K5,K8 have positive
first real embedding; torsion rotates by `2pi/w`. Every associate of pi
in the closed canonical first sector is therefore on a boundary ray.

Suppose a nonzero integral element alpha lies on either boundary ray.
After multiplying pi by a torsion unit if necessary, the first embedding
of `alpha/pi` is real. Injectivity of the embedding shows `alpha/pi` is
fixed by complex conjugation, hence lies in the real subfield. At the
ramified prime its K-valuation is even, so alpha has odd valuation. As
alpha is integral this valuation is positive. If alpha is prime it must
therefore be in the ramified class itself. In particular every retained
prime lies strictly inside both first-sector boundaries. The sector has
angle less than pi, so a nonempty ordinary sum remains strictly inside it
and has positive first real part. It is neither zero nor a ramified-prime
associate. This excludes arbitrarily large exceptional associates, not
merely the normalized ramified representative.

In K8 every retained input is 1 modulo the ramified prime, whose residue
field is F2. Every even-length sum is divisible by that prime. It is nonzero
by the sector argument and is not a ramified-prime associate. A prime element
divisible by that prime would have to be such an associate; thus the sum is
not prime. Divisibility also excludes units. Any one required line proves
the impossibility. QED.

For K5 no parity obstruction follows: nonzero residues in F16 are not all
one. K12 has no norm-two residue field either. These absences alone are not
existence proofs. The analytic/controller composition must independently show
that all sufficiently large orders work for K5,K12 and all sufficiently large
odd orders work for K8. The arithmetic admissibility sets proposed for that
composition are therefore all positive integers for m=5,12 and positive odd
integers for m=8; they are not defined by array existence.

The K5/K8 boundary proof does not transfer to K12: `arg(sigma_1(u12))=pi/12`
is half the torsion step. Associates of the ramified prime over 2 can enter
the central ray after an odd free-unit power, and associates of the ramified
prime over 3 can enter it after an even power. Hence a zero residue at either
of those primes cannot automatically be called composite. Selecting target
primes in unit classes at the ramified ideals is a sufficient construction
condition, not a necessary condition for every possible prime-sum array.

Zero is excluded from every input sum by positive first projection. A general
odd-length sum need not be asserted nonunit merely because its first embedding
is large: free units defeat that inference. Eventual constructions must choose
certified prime targets; exact finite checkers must reject norm one. N4 uses
separate divisibility to exclude units in its even-order application.

For a finite quotient O/q, a class Delta is a difference of two units exactly
when it reduces to zero in each residue field F2 dividing q. In a residue
field of size at least three, choose b avoiding 0 and -Delta; then b and
b+Delta are nonzero. In F2 only Delta=0 works. Lift to prime-ideal powers
and use CRT. Likewise every residue is a sum of three units in fields of
size at least three; in F2 the sum must be 1. These are complete local
conditions for these two small patterns, not assertions about a growing
family of all array lines.

## N5. The representative domain, centroid, and the antipodal repair

The following geometric calculation applies to all three fields. Identify
Minkowski space with C^2 and scale a norm cutoff B by `R=B^(1/4)`. Write

`z1=rho exp(t/2) exp(i theta1)`,
`z2=rho exp(-t/2) exp(i theta2)`.

The normalized canonical domain D is exactly

`0<rho<=1`, `-L<=t<L`, `-a<=theta1<a`, `theta2 mod 2pi arbitrary`.

Its boundary has four-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero; it need not be
convex. Its Euclidean volume element is
`rho^3 d rho d t d theta1 d theta2`. Therefore

`Vol(D)=2 pi^2 L/w`,

and its centroid is `(c,0)`, where

`c=[8 sinh(L/2)/(5L)] [sin(a)/a]`.

The second component vanishes by integration over its full circle, not by
an unsupported assumption about finite-prefix symmetry. Uniform elementary
bounds for the three displayed L,a are

`3/4 < c < 5/6`.

Indeed `L<9/10`, `a<=pi/8`, `sinh(L/2)/(L/2)<207/200`, and
`sin(a)/a>1-a^2/6>97/100`; these give the stated strict bounds. These bounds
can be proved by positive Taylor-series bounds on `[0,9/20]` and
`pi<22/7`, without trusting numerical embeddings. For reference only, c is
approximately 0.794520, 0.805076, and 0.805259 for m=5,8,12.

The prime-distribution theorem audited in the analytic manuscript implies
weak convergence of the scaled canonical prime counting measures to normalized
Lebesgue measure on D. Its proof uses fixed convex boxes away from boundary
and origin, then finite inner/outer approximation; boundedness supplies first
moments. The prime-ideal theorem and the one-to-one normalization give

`M(B) ~ Li(B) ~ B/log B`,
`(1/M(B)) sum_{N(alpha)<=B} sigma(alpha) = R ((c,0)+o(1))`.

There are at most four prime ideals with any given norm in these fields, so
an incomplete last norm tie changes the count by at most three and the vector
total by `O(R)`. These asymptotics therefore also hold for every exact initial
prefix of length M, with `B~M log M`,

`R~(M log M)^(1/4)`.

For an input array M=n^D, arithmetic rank remains four while D is 2 or 3.
The radius is `R asymp n^(D/4) (log n)^(1/4)`, not the quadratic-field scale.

The centroid lies outside the canonical support: points with first component
near c and second component near zero violate balance. Consequently there is
no ordinary endpoint box centered at the mean. A verbatim transfer of the
quadratic mean-box lemma fails.

**Proposition N5 (symmetric endpoint interior).** Nevertheless D has a
full-dimensional symmetric pair supply about its centroid. For fixed c in
this formula, the two points

`A=(c,c)`, `B=(c,-c)` in C^2

are strict interior points of D and satisfy `(A+B)/2=(c,0)`. The set
`D intersect (2(c,0)-D)` has nonempty four-dimensional interior. There are
four linearly independent fixed gap vectors whose endpoint pairs have this
same midpoint, with a positive margin from every normalization and norm
boundary. This remains true around the actual finite-prefix mean for all
sufficiently large prefixes.

**Proof.** At both points rho=c<1, t=0, theta1=0, while the second phases
are 0 and pi. All defining inequalities that bound a genuine boundary are
strict. Thus some Euclidean ball of radius epsilon around A and the reflected
ball around B lie inside D. The parameter theta2 is a circle, so its chart
cut is not a boundary of D. Differences of symmetric pairs fill a ball about
`g0=(0,2c)` in C^2. In real coordinates `(Re z1,Im z1,Re z2,Im z2)`, choose

`v1=g0`, `v2=g0+delta e1`, `v3=g0+delta e2`, `v4=g0+delta e4`

for a fixed sufficiently small positive delta. Their determinant has absolute
value `2c delta^3`, so they are linearly independent and have a fixed finite
inverse-matrix norm. Their endpoints `(c,0)+/-vj/2` remain in D. Fixed smaller
endpoint balls preserve these properties. The actual mean divided by R tends
to `(c,0)`, so translating all centers to that actual mean is allowed once
the translation is less than the fixed margin. QED.

This is a tested repair of the *geometric lemma*, within the same frozen pool;
it does not rescale any input by a unit. Prime distribution in the two endpoint
boxes, in appropriate unit residue classes, supplies real pool elements. A
pigeonhole argument over their actual differences supplies equal-gap pairs.
Four endpoint-coordinate directions do not replace the two/four equal-gap
pairs needed by an array rectangle/cuboid. Those physical requirements are
proved in the separate controller manuscript.

## General fixed-field boundary

In a class-number-h field, prime elements still mean generators of principal
prime ideals; nonprincipal prime ideals contribute no prime-element class.
Irreducibles are a larger notion when h>1. A norm ordering of ideals never
defines their addition. A full unit basis, a bounded representative domain,
prime distribution in its cells, and a symmetric endpoint-interior property
analogous to N5 are separate obligations in higher degree. None follows merely
from the ring being cyclotomic. This file establishes them geometrically and
arithmetically for the three explicit quartic fields, with distribution
specified as a separate proved analytic interface; it makes no claim uniform
in m.
