# Constructing the two prime-top examples

**Mira Cult**

This note constructs the two models in the [classification](0134-prime-top-two-types.md), taking the true nonzero lower trace in its two square classes. The actual inverted reference has order $`p`$ and the actual stabilizer lift has order $`p^2`$; these are different group elements.

## Realizing the first Hall movement at prime top
For every odd prime p and every prime q with ord_q(p)=p, there is a finite simple proper left Bol loop of order q p^(2p+2), with reciprocal top of order p, Hall-moving Frattini deficit exactly e=p, and aligned rank-one crossed operator at the [first-moving threshold](prime-top-moving-layer.md#odd-two-prime-axial-first-movement-actual-fixed-sector). In particular p=3,q=13 gives order 85,293. This proves existence. Exhaustion and nonisomorphism are proved in the classification, not by this construction alone. Such a q always exists: take a prime divisor of 1+p+...+p^(p−1)>1. The sum is odd and 1 mod p. If that divisor also divided p−1, the sum would be p modulo the divisor, impossible. Hence the order is exactly p.

### The signed group and physical row

Let K=F_(p^p), R its p-power Frobenius, Tr the absolute trace, and S=ker Tr. Let zeta be an element of multiplicative order q. There are two first axes V_i=K and two lower axes F_i=K, and a single scalar upper C=F_p. Use the class-two BCH core N on their direct sum whose only nonzero brackets are
[c,v_1]=c v_1 in F_1 and [c,v_2]=−c v_2 in F_2.
The two Hall factors B_i act by zeta on the respective V_i,F_i, trivially elsewhere. Take the split top h of order p acting by R on axis 1, R^−1 on axis 2, fixing C and acting by reciprocal scalars p,p^−1 modulo q on B. These are consistent semidirect actions. Sign swaps axes (V and F and B), negates C, and inverts h. The bracket signs give an actual automorphism t of order two. Here N has exponent p and [N,N]=F_1⊕F_2=:F. The outer action is faithful on the first axial pair, so N is O_p(D) for D=N⋊(B⋊⟨h⟩).

Take an F_p-linear functional lambda on K with lambda(R−1)=Tr. Such exists since Tr kills the constants ker(R−1) (degree p). In particular lambda kills constants: the fixed line is the image of (R−1)^(p−1), and Tr(R−1)=0. Put
W={(v,0;f,0;c): c=Tr(v), Tr(f)=lambda(v)}.
Its size is p^(2p−1). It is a subgroup: trace of the first-axis bracket of two row elements is Tr(v)Tr(w)−Tr(w)Tr(v)=0. Choose f0 with Tr(f0)=1, let u have V=0, F=(f0,0), C=1, and set H generated by W, B2, u h. The B2 action fixes W and u, and h normalizes B2. Conjugating W by u h changes the first F coordinate of the rotated row by Rv (because of C=1), so the lower equation is exactly lambda(Rv)−lambda(v)=Tr(v). The actual pth power (u h)^p has first F-coordinate Tr(f0)·1; it is in W because its trace is zero. Thus H is an actual subgroup, |H|=q p^(2p), and |D:H|=q p^(2p+2).

### Actual full transversal

Let the inverted mark set be Lambda={g:t(g)=g^−1}. Products Lambda^−1 Lambda are products of two outside involutions in D⋊⟨t⟩. Therefore a necessary condition for g to be such a product is that t(g) be D-conjugate to g^−1: an outside involution conjugates a two-involution product to its inverse. The difference set is D-conjugacy invariant because all outside involutions are one class by odd coprime conjugacy. We show this necessary condition fails for every nonidentity g∈H; this proves actual avoidance and full transversality, not merely a projected condition.

First, if g has top zero but nonzero B2 coordinate, t(g) has B1 rather than B2 support in the outer group, impossible under conjugacy. For g∈W with v≠0, sign swaps the first-layer axis, whereas D conjugacy cannot swap axes on V=N/F. If v=0, then c=0 and g belongs to F1∩W; any nonzero such g again has opposite axis support after sign, impossible under conjugacy.

Now take g∈H with nonzero top j. Conjugating within B2 removes its entire B coordinate, since the action of h^j on B2 is fixed-point-free. We may therefore write g=w(u h)^j with w∈W and zero Hall coordinate. Its N-coordinate is (v,0;f,0;c). A conjugator from g^−1 to t(g) must project to h^k for some k, since its outer element centralizes h^−j and the fixed-point-free outer action gives precisely that centralizer. Thus write a putative conjugator n h^k, with n∈N. On the first V-axis comparison gives
(1−R^−j)w1=R^(k−j)v,
where w1 is the first V-coordinate of n. Hence Tr(v)=0. The actual H formula now yields c=j and Tr(f)=lambda(v)+j: u h supplies j copies of the unit lower trace, and its cross correction with w has trace zero.

Compare the first F trace under this same proposed conjugation. Put r=R^−j and a=R^(k−j)v. In BCH, before conjugating by n the inverse's first coordinates are (−a,−R^(k−j)f;−c); after multiplying n on the left and r(n)^−1 on the right, the linear contribution of the first F-coordinate of n cancels in trace. The two bracket terms, before multiplication by 1/2, add to
−2d a+(c+d)w1−(d−c)r w1
on the first axis, where d is the C-coordinate of n. Since Tr(a)=0 and trace is R-invariant, the correction to trace is c Tr(w1). The first F-coordinate of t(g) is zero, so comparison requires Tr(f)=c Tr(w1). But the first V equation implies lambda(v)=j Tr(w1): applying powers of R does not change lambda on trace-zero vectors, and lambda(1−R^−j)=j Tr. Hence the actual trace of f is c Tr(w1)+j, contradiction since j≠0. This completes the avoidance of H∩Lambda^−1 Lambda except identity. The fixed-index formula gives |Lambda|=|D:H| (on N the sign fixed dimension is 2p, outside it the fixed size is q), so Lambda is an actual transversal to every H-conjugate. The [literal q=13 checker](../code/check_prime_top_first_hall_moving_rank_one.py) independently repeats row closure, true top closure, and 118,098 constrained first-axis conjugation trace comparisons in the smallest case.

### Ordinary core, all strict signed cuts, and properness

For ordinary core-freeness, let a D-normal subgroup be contained in H. Nonzero outer top forces both Hall axes in its projection by commutation, impossible in B2⋊⟨h⟩. A nontrivial outer Hall element there instead forces nonzero second V-axis in its intersection with N by commutation, impossible in W. In N, normality under B splits the projection to V⊕C into its irreducible Hall axes and fixed scalar. A nonzero first axis projection produces all F1 by commutation with C; a nonzero C projection produces both F_i by commutation with axes. Neither can lie in W. If the normal subgroup already lies in F, irreducibility again rules out containment in the trace-zero F1 slice. Thus H is core-free in D.

The strict signed cuts exhaust as follows. Put M=V1⊕V2⊕F inside N (its axis group here is abelian). Inside N the only nonzero signed-normal possibilities are F, F C, M and N. The Hall/sign action makes F a minimal signed layer, and on the projection to V⊕C the signed-normal choices are zero, C, both axes, or all. Any nonzero such projection forces F by commutation with C or with an axis and by sign. The actual H intersections have dimensions p−1,p−1,2p−2,2p−1 respectively, all strictly smaller than the respective fixed dimensions p,p,2p,2p. For a signed normal subgroup I not contained in N, its nonzero outer image contains B: in the reciprocal outer quotient, the two distinct Hall eigenlines are exchanged by sign, and any nonzero top forces B by commutation. Commutation with N then shows I∩N projects to both V-axes, hence contains M by the interior list (or equals N). Since D/M=C×(B⋊⟨h⟩), such I contains M B: its image has B in the outer projection, and each B element there can be separated from the central C by coprime powering. Hence the proper candidates here correspond to proper vector subspaces J of C×⟨h⟩, dimension at most one, entirely sign negative. The fixed size of I is q p^(2p), whereas |H∩I|=q p^(2p−2+dim J): the H image spans C×⟨h⟩ (W covers C and u h covers top). These are all strict.

The inverted marks generate D: h, the negative C scalar, the Hall antidiagonal, and the additive antidiagonal in V are inverted. Top conjugation gives both Hall axes from their antidiagonal (reciprocal scalars are distinct); Hall conjugation spans the first axial modules, and commutation with C supplies both F_i. Thus the canonical action is faithful and generated by actual marks. The [signed Bol-folder criterion](../../packet/manuscripts/odd-folder-radicals.md#candidate-simplicity-criterion-using-τ-stable-normal-subgroups) gives a simple left Bol loop by the exhaustive strict cuts. It is not a Moufang loop: by [Glauberman's odd-order theorem](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0021869368900501) an odd simple Moufang loop is an abelian group of prime order, whereas this simple loop has composite order. Its parameters have dim V^−=p+1, so e=(2p+2)−1−(p+1)=p, attaining the first Hall-moving threshold without an abelian core or any fixed lower sector.
