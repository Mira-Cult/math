# Forcing the first Hall-moving Frattini layer

**Mira Cult**

At the [prime-top family orders](0134-prime-top-two-types.md), any Hall-moving Frattini subgroup has the first possible deficit. This note derives the layer and physical row restrictions, including the exceptional reflected case and the small-prime exclusions.


<a id="odd-two-prime-frattini-covering-bound"></a>

## A covering bound for the first normal layer at odd two-prime order
Let a finite simple proper left Bol loop have order p^a q with distinct odd primes p,q and a≥1, in the audited odd reciprocal signed-envelope reduction (normal generation, ordinary core-freeness and strict signed cuts). Denote the canonical translation envelope by D, sign by t, core N=O_p(D), and top order n=p^k. Then
```math
 d(N)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(N/\Phi(N))
 \ \le\ 2(a-k)+\left\lfloor\frac{2(a-k)}{p^k-1}\right\rfloor
 \ \le\ 3(a-k).
```
This bound covers abelian, nonabelian, central/noncentral Frattini and mixed Hall character cases. It is not a theorem for all loop orders. In fact the proof gives a finite first-layer template list, not a census of entire envelopes.

### Budget and splitting

Use the audited [reciprocal outer architecture](prime-top-outer-reduction.md#odd-prime-index-hall-radical-reciprocal-normal-form) and the [automatic normal Hall reduction](prime-top-outer-reduction.md#automatic-hall). Thus D/N=(C_q×C_q)⋊C_n, the outer top acts by reciprocal order-n scalars, t swaps Hall axes and inverts top, and n+k≤a. The p-part of the fixed index on this outer quotient is p^k: coprime fixed lifting through its normal Hall group, together with inversion on its cyclic top, proves this directly. Coprime fixed lifting through N now gives |N:C_N(t)|=p^{a-k} by the signed index. On V=N/Φ(N) write V^- for the negative sign eigenspace, and let b=dim V^-. Another use of coprime fixed lifting gives b≤a−k.

The Hall group B of order q² acts on V. Its fixed part V_0 and moving part V_1=[V,B] give a direct sum stable under the top and sign, by the coprime averaging projector and normality of the outer B.

### Hall-fixed heads cannot be positive

Pass to D/Φ(N) and kill V_1. Its remaining elementary kernel V_0 centralizes the Hall layer. The preimage of B is an abelian direct product of V_0 and a unique Hall complement B_0: V_0 is central there, a complement exists by the coprime splitting theorem, and every element of q-power order lies in that complement. Hence B_0 is normal and sign-stable in the whole quotient. Kill it to obtain an odd p-group E with normal elementary V_0 and cyclic quotient of order n. It is generated by images of inverted marks. If R is the top action on V_0, the subgroup (R−1)V_0 is sign-stable and normal; after killing it, E is abelian because it is an extension of a central group by a cyclic group. Its inverted generators force inversion on the entire abelian quotient, and in particular on the embedded V_0/(R−1)V_0.

Write Z=(R−1)(R+1)^{-1}; R+1 is invertible, Z^n=0, and tZt=−Z. The signed top quotient of each Jordan chain is therefore negative. For completeness, a homogeneous Jordan basis is obtained by selecting sign-eigenvector representatives of the head spaces of each exact Jordan length: use the filtration induced by ker Z^j on V_0/ZV_0, whose j-th increment represents blocks of length j. These subspaces and the individual kernels are sign-stable, so lift each eigenvector from the corresponding kernel by sign averaging. The resulting chains have the specified lengths and their heads form a basis of V_0/ZV_0; by descending through images of Z, they span V_0. The chain lengths count the Jordan partition, so this spanning set is a basis. Signs alternate from each negative head. Thus dim V_0^+≤dim V_0^-; its template data are Jordan lengths at most n, all with negative heads. This argument does not assume an actual split p-top over V_0.

### Moving weights and the total bound

Extend scalars to a splitting field for B. Each nonzero character of B has exact top orbit length n: one nonzero axis coordinate suffices, because its top multiplier has exact order n. Weight spaces within an orbit have the same dimension. The sign permutes these top orbits. If it swaps two of them, its plus and minus dimensions are equal on their sum, by exchange of weight spaces. If it preserves a top orbit then it acts on its n weights by a reflection of the cyclic index (sign inverts the top modulo B). There is exactly one fixed weight, since n is odd; the other n−1 weights occur in pairs. For orbit multiplicity h, those pairs contribute exactly (n−1)h/2 to each sign eigenspace. The fixed space can contribute at most h extra positives. Hence
```math
 \dim V_1^+-\dim V_1^-\le
 \left\lfloor\frac{2\dim V_1^-}{n-1}\right\rfloor.
```
All dimensions descend unchanged under scalar extension. Adding the fixed and moving parts gives the claimed inequality using b≤a−k. The argument does not require axial weights, multiplicity one, a central commutator, or any descent of physical stabilizer avoidance to the Frattini quotient.

### What this covers and what remains

At each admitted k, the list of first-layer templates is finite with dimension at most the displayed bound: choose the Hall-fixed negative-headed Jordan partition; choose nonzero top-weight orbits with equal multiplicities under Frobenius and sign, and their compatible descent/sign linear maps within the dimension ceiling. A literal finite covering algorithm is to enumerate matrices for the signed reciprocal outer presentation on F_p^d for each d up to the bound, then apply these weight and Jordan restrictions. Completeness follows from the actual outer action on V. The templates need not be realized: one must still lift to a p-core, choose its actual physical stabilizer, and pay true cuts and transversality. The compact C9 sufficient constructions do not cover these lifts. Beyond the p^a q scope we do not assume a reciprocal two-axis outer layer or claim this bound. For this scope the bound grows with a, as it must: previously constructed families add arbitrarily many distinct passive axial packets, increasing the Frattini dimension without changing the reciprocal top.

## Meeting top-down and constructive templates: the saturated layer
Use the [first-layer cover](prime-top-moving-layer.md#odd-two-prime-frattini-covering-bound) and the [universal core cover](prime-top-moving-layer.md#odd-two-prime-frattini-covering-bound). Write n=p^k, N=O_p(D), V=N/Phi(N), d=dim V and b=dim V^−. The remaining negative budget is e=a−k−b, measured exactly by |Phi(N):C_Phi(N)(t)|=p^e. Indeed the fixed-point map C_N(t)→C_V(t) is surjective by the coprime-action fixed-lifting theorem: on a fixed coset, the action of t is an involution on an odd set (the coset has size |Phi(N)|), hence has a fixed representative. Its kernel is C_Phi(N)(t). Factoring the indices and using |N:C_N(t)|=p^{a−k} gives the formula.

### Complete first lift reduction at deficit zero

Suppose e=0. Then t fixes F=Phi(N) pointwise. Each inverted generator x induces on F a conjugation whose conjugate by t equals both itself (since t acts identically there) and its inverse (since t(x)=x^−1). Its order is odd, so it is trivial. The canonical D itself is generated by these inversions, not merely N, giving F⊂Z(D). Hence N has class at most two, and [N,N] has exponent dividing p: each x^p lies in central F and the class-two formula gives [x,y]^p=[x^p,y]=1.

For odd p the class-two power formula now makes x↦x^p a homomorphism to F. Modulo F^p it induces an equivariant linear map from V to F/F^p, where both outer group and sign act trivially on the target. On the moving V_1 it vanishes by Hall averaging. On the Hall-fixed V_0 it factors through the top coinvariants, which are entirely negative by the first-layer theorem; sign equivariance forces it zero there too. Thus N^p⊂F^p. The Frattini identity F=N^p[N,N] now gives F=F^p[N,N]. Passing to the finite abelian p-group F/[N,N], surjectivity of p-th power forces the quotient to vanish. Therefore F=[N,N] is elementary and N has exponent p.

It follows that the whole p-core in this branch is controlled by a surjective alternating map beta: exterior² V→F invariant under the signed reciprocal outer action. Conversely every such map defines an exponent-p core of class at most two by multiplication (v,f)(w,g)=(v+w,f+g+beta(v,w)/2); its Frattini subgroup is F by surjectivity. In the other direction choose a basis of V and lifts of its vectors; the standard class-two collection using their commutators and p-th powers gives this model, up to a change of section. The beta assertion concerns the core, not the existence of an ambient envelope or stabilizer.

### The physical row cuts down beta and the lift

The true W=H∩N misses F: if H contained a nontrivial element of central F, its cyclic subgroup would be contained in every conjugate of H, contradicting ordinary core-freeness. Write U for its image in V, c=dim F. The physical N-orbits have size p^a, so dim U=d+c−a. The row U is invariant under the image of H in D/N, the specified Hall axis semidirect full top. Necessarily beta(U,U)=0. In particular c≤a. In the displayed BCH model a physical W over U is a graph of a linear map lambda:U→F, because U is isotropic. A sign-linear choice of BCH section exists by averaging; under it lambda restricted to U∩V^− must be injective. Indeed otherwise a nonzero graph entry in the true negative subspace is an inverted mark in H, which is prohibited. Conversely this injection is only the identity-top within-N necessary test, not a substitute for all outside products. For each candidate U and c, let M be the signed outer module exterior²V. The allowable beta factor through M/( [outer and sign,M]+exterior²U ); by the bracket identity this is a linear rank bound on c (stronger than c≤a whenever the computed dimension is smaller). In particular if this quotient is zero then only the elementary p-core is possible for that candidate U, even though the universal p-core could have many noncentral or ramified quotients.

The rest of the lift can now be stated as a finite *linear central-lift test* rather than arbitrary noncentral factor sets. Put G=D/F. Use the elementary-kernel extension of the reciprocal outer group by V and the actual row image Hbar in it (Hbar∩V=U). Enumerate F_p-central cocycles on the finite signed quotient G⋊⟨t⟩, restrict to V to get the beta with zero p-power part, and require that restriction to Hbar be a coboundary. Indeed H misses F and so splits the latter extension. Choosing a splitting is solving the coboundary equation, with ambiguity precisely Hom(Hbar,F). Central lifts over signed G determine the actual order-two sign lift uniquely over the distinguished involution since F has odd order.

There is an exact finite collision filter on the remaining cocycles and choices of splitting. For every quotient inverted element z and any lift ztilde, ztilde·t(ztilde) is central; exactly one multiply by an element of F makes this product 1 (solve twice that element). Thus the actual inverted set is in bijection with that of G. For every pair of distinct quotient marks z,w with z^−1w in Hbar, compare the central coordinate of the corresponding actual difference ztilde^−1 wtilde against the chosen splitting of z^−1w: the difference must be nonzero in F. This compares distinct right coset representatives; identical mark pairs impose no test. These are affine nonvanishing tests on a cocycle and splitting before quotient by coordinate changes. Products not projecting into Hbar impose nothing. This checks the actual H avoidance, equivalently the H transversal by the sharp index, without pretending it descends to G. Products of inverted marks are conjugacy invariant as a set (identify the marks times t with the outside involution class); therefore the same filter gives all conjugate H transversals. Group generation, ordinary core, strict signed cuts and properness must still be imposed literally. No claim that the resulting central filter always has a solution.

## One negative Frattini unit cannot carry Hall movement

In the audited odd reciprocal order p^a q scope, if the residual negative budget e=a−k−dim (N/Phi(N))^− is at most one, the actual Hall complement B of order q² centralizes Phi(N). This does not assert that Phi(N) is central in N or sign-fixed when e=1.

Let F=Phi(N) and W=F/Phi(F). The latter is elementary, its negative dimension is at most e, by fixed lifting over the odd cosets of Phi(F). Take a sign-stable actual B above the normal outer Hall layer: the complements to N in its preimage are conjugate by N (coprime Hall conjugacy), hence their set has odd cardinality; the sign involution fixes one. For the top generator choose an actual lift h normalizing B, again by conjugacy of complements. Its action on Hall characters is the prescribed reciprocal order-n action. The induced operators h,t on W need not factor through the outer group, but their permutations on the B-characters do: t swaps the axes and conjugates the reciprocal top permutation to its inverse. Consequently the weight-space pairing argument from the first-layer proof applies word for word to sign dimensions, including when h has bigger order on W.

A top orbit of nonzero weights has length n=p^k. If not preserved by t it pairs with another orbit, contributing at least n negatives. If preserved, t reflects its indices, and the n−1 off-center weights contribute at least (n−1)/2 negatives. For n≥5 both cases already exceed one. At n=3 the only possibility would be exactly one preserved nonzero orbit with one-dimensional weight spaces (over a splitting field), hence mixed weights: an axial orbit is sent to the other axis. Since W is rational over F_3 and there are no other nonzero orbits, Frobenius must preserve that lone orbit. On an initial weight (x,y) with both coordinates nonzero, this requires (3x,3y)=(alpha^j x,alpha^−j y) over F_q for some j, where alpha has order three. It implies 3=alpha^j=alpha^−j and then alpha^j=1, so q divides 2, impossible here. Thus no nonzero weight survives over the splitting field in any case. B acts trivially on W.

By the Burnside basis automorphism theorem the kernel of Aut(F)→Aut(F/Phi(F)) is a p-group. The image of the actual q-group B in this kernel must be trivial. This proves actual centralization of F, not merely a weight assertion on its quotient. The case F=1 is immediate.

Coverage consequence: anywhere among the top-down first-layer templates with e=1, Hall-moving Frattini and ramified Hall packets whose actual cubes lie in a moving lower layer are excluded. Existing compact Hall-moving C9 examples must therefore fall at e≥2; this is a placement obstruction, not an assertion about all sign-moving examples or about noncentral B-fixed cores.

## First possible Hall-moving Frattini layer

### Exact Hall threshold and first nontrivial templates

Use the audited odd reciprocal p^a q architecture and n=p^k, e=a−k−dim (N/Phi(N))^−. If e<n then the actual Hall B centralizes F=Phi(N). Thus the [physical class-two theorem](prime-top-hall-fixed-exclusion.md#odd-two-prime-hall-fixed-frattini-physical-class-two) applies to all e<n: actual N is then central-Frattini of exponent p, with the upper/moving matching equation of that theorem.

Indeed, as in the one-deficit proof, apply weight calculus to F/Phi(F) and an actual top normalizing the sign-stable B. A sign-exchanged pair of nonzero top orbits costs at least n negatives. A sign-preserved orbit must be mixed, since sign swaps axes. On such a mixed orbit a Frobenius return after r steps requires p^r=alpha^j=alpha^−j modulo q for some j; because alpha has odd order n it follows that alpha^j=1 and p^r=1. Thus the Frobenius orbit of this top orbit has exact length f=ord_q(p), and every one of its members is also preserved by sign. Since q≡1 mod n with q odd, q≥2n+1>p+1; hence p is neither 1 nor −1 modulo q, and f≥3. Each reflected orbit costs at least (n−1)/2 negatives, so the whole rational package costs at least f(n−1)/2≥n. The sign-negative dimension of F/Phi(F) is bounded by e by odd fixed-coset lifting. If e<n all Hall weights there are trivial, and the coprime Burnside automorphism-kernel argument centralizes the actual F.

At e=n with Hall movement, these inequalities leave exactly one moving package in F/Phi(F), with all weight multiplicities one, and no negatives left in the Hall-fixed part or Phi(F). The possible first-layer moving patterns are:
- A single sign-exchanged top-orbit pair. If mixed, Frobenius permutes the two orbits, so its square returns a mixed orbit, contradicting p² not congruent to 1 modulo q. Hence it is an axial pair. Frobenius stays within each axis and its return condition gives ord_q(p) dividing n. The sign exchanges the two entire packets and the pair costs exactly n.
- A purely reflected mixed package. Equality forces n=3, f=3, and q=13 (q is an odd divisor of 3³−1, not 2). It consists of the three Frobenius-conjugate top orbits, with each of their three reflected midpoints sign positive, to avoid additional negative cost.

There is no other moving rational pattern at the threshold: every exchanged pair costs n and every reflected Frobenius package costs at least n; the two kinds are stable under Frobenius since it commutes with sign. Equality in the fixed-index budget also makes Phi(F) pointwise sign-fixed. The inverted generators of D then centralize Phi(F), by the odd-order conjugation argument already used in the saturated theorem. Thus a first Hall-moving candidate still has F class at most two and its own Frattini layer is central in the full D. These are necessary templates at e=n, not a sufficiency claim for either pattern. In particular they say exactly where to look before trying higher-order Hall-moving lifts.

## Reflected first Hall movement requires axial multiplicity or mixed generators
Consider the exceptional first-moving reflected package at p=n=3,q=13,e=3 from the [equality templates](prime-top-moving-layer.md#odd-two-prime-first-hall-moving-deficit). Suppose the first layer V=N/Phi(N) has no mixed weights (it may have any Hall-fixed sector). For each reflected midpoint weight (x,x) occurring in that moving lower package, the axial spaces V_(x,0) and V_(0,x) must have equal dimension at least two. Thus in particular a single degree-three packet on each axis cannot support this reflected first movement, even with noncentral Phi(N).

### The moving mixed package survives in the central lower quotient

Put F=Phi(N), J=Phi(F), W=F/J. The equality template says that all nontrivial weights of W are in the unique reflected Frobenius package; each weight space is one-dimensional and the top and Frobenius together act transitively on this packet. All its midpoint weights are sign positive. There may also be a Hall-fixed part of W. The N-action on W factors through elementary V, since inner automorphisms of F act trivially on W. Use the augmentation filtration W_r=I^r W for the group algebra of the image of N, in characteristic p. It terminates: a finite p-group representation over characteristic p has a common upper triangular basis with diagonal 1, by selecting a fixed vector and inducting through quotients. All W_r are stable under the ambient group, sign and rational descent, since N is normal and sign-stable.

The unique mixed package must occur in exactly one associated grade: each of its weights is simple, and top and Frobenius transport its location to all other weights of the package. If the grade were r>0, the multiplication map
V tensor (W_(r−1)/W_r) → W_r/W_(r+1), (v,w)↦(v−1)w
would be surjective and Hall-equivariant. Bilinearity follows from the group-algebra identity for (v v'−1), whose cross term increases filtration by two, and the action factors through V. The preceding grade has only trivial Hall weights, whereas V has no mixed weights. The map cannot hit a mixed weight, contradiction. Therefore the moving mixed package occurs in grade zero and survives in W/[N,W].

Let J' be the inverse image of [N,W] in F. The quotient N/J' is class at most two with central elementary Frattini F/J', because [N,F] projects into [N,W]. It retains all the reflected mixed weights as the entirety of its moving Frattini weights (Hall-fixed weights may remain) and retains V as first layer. Since its commutators are elementary and p is odd, the p-power map to F/J' is a homomorphism inducing a Hall-equivariant linear map on V. It cannot hit mixed weights. The Frattini identity therefore forces the entire mixed packet to be supplied by the alternating bracket of V alone. No step here assumes physical transversality survives a quotient; only necessary module information is used.

### The sign at a midpoint

Over the splitting field, at the fixed weight (x,x) (x nonzero), the only Hall weight sums from V that can supply this bracket are (x,0)+(0,x). This is true even with arbitrary Hall-fixed multiplicity, because V has no mixed weights. Sign swaps these two axial spaces, so they have equal dimension. If they have dimension zero the bracket vanishes. If both have dimension one, choose a nonzero vector a in the first and its sign image b in the second. The sole spanning bracket beta(a,b) transforms by sign into beta(b,a)=−beta(a,b). But the target midpoint is positive in the threshold template, so the map is again zero. Surjectivity to that weight forces both dimensions at least two.

This eliminates the reflected equality pattern at two multiplicity-one axial packets plus an arbitrary Hall-fixed upper sector, including potential noncentral lower lifts. It does not rule out higher multiplicities, genuinely mixed first-layer weights, or the distinct sign-exchanged axial equality pattern.

## Completeness of the top-three, first Hall-moving slice at order 85,293
Let a simple proper left Bol loop have order 13·3^8 in the audited odd reciprocal architecture, cyclic top 3, and first Hall-moving deficit e=3 with actual Hall action nontrivial on F=Phi(N). Then it is in the [two-form axial site](85293-normal-form.md#odd-q13-prime-top-first-movement-rank-one-normal-form). Thus the scoped exact two-type result there, once independently checked, covers this entire top/deficit slice and not just fixed input weights. This does not cover the other deficits or tops at the same total order.

### Two packets at each layer and a central fixed sector

The first layer V=N/F has negative dimension 8−1−3=4. By the rational sign costs and the [equality template](prime-top-moving-layer.md#odd-two-prime-first-hall-moving-deficit), its moving part consists either of one axial pair (dimension six, three negative) or one reflected mixed package (dimension nine, three negative). A mixed exchanged rational package costs more than four; a reflected package consumes three more negatives if any midpoint is negative, because Frobenius permutes its three midpoints. Thus every mixed midpoint is positive: its weights are O×O at multiplicity one, where O is some scalar multiple of {1,3,9} in F_13. Both coordinates range independently, since the top shifts them in reciprocal directions while scalar Frobenius shifts in the same direction. In either case the first Hall-fixed summand V0 has negative dimension one; its homogeneous Jordan heads are negative, so it is a singleton or a chain with signs (−,+). The lower Frattini layer W0'=F/J for J=Phi(F) also has exactly one moving package, axial or reflected mixed, with all other weights Hall-fixed and sign positive. J itself is central in D by equality saturation.

Use the augmentation filtration for the N-action on F/J, exactly as in the [reflected obstruction](prime-top-moving-layer.md#odd-two-prime-reflected-first-movement-multiplicity-obstruction). Quotient by the inverse image of [N,F/J] so the surviving lower layer is central and elementary in the core quotient. Its Hall-fixed sector is trivial under D and sign positive: N acts trivially; the Hall is trivial; and since sign is the identity there, its conjugate-inverse relation on the odd top makes that top act trivially too. The surviving sector cannot be generated by powers: the class-two cube map factors V to this sector and kills Hall-moving inputs and the negative top-head coinvariants on V0. It cannot be generated by brackets either. In each moving axial orbit O there are no opposite weights; likewise on a mixed rectangle O×O, so no bracket of two moving first weights has trivial total Hall weight. (Opposite axes also cannot sum to zero.) The only remaining bracket would be on V0, whose wedge has no positive sign (dimension at most two, with signs −,+ in dimension two). By the Frattini identity the surviving Hall-fixed sector is therefore zero.

It follows that the unique moving lower packet is in augmentation degree zero: the degree-zero module is nonzero, has no Hall-fixed piece, and the entire moving packet occurs in only one grade by simple weights and sign/top/Frobenius transport. Thus [N,F/J] is exactly its Hall-fixed sector. The actual F0=C_F(B) is the preimage of this sector (J is Hall-fixed and fixed cosets lift coprimely), so [N,F]≤F0 and F0 is D-normal. It is pointwise sign-fixed by equality and fixed lifting. Inverted generation then centralizes it in D. In particular H∩F0=1.

In fact F is abelian: its bracket factors through exterior²(F/J) to J by centrality of J, and moving lower weights again have no opposite weights; the fixed part already lifts to central F0. Since F is abelian, it is F0⊕F_mov by Hall averaging. F_mov is elementary since F^3=J is Hall-fixed. Now [N,F] is elementary since its commutators are central and depend homomorphically on the elementary moving factor alone. They project onto F0/J, hence generate F0 by abelian Frattini generation (J=F^3=F0^3). Thus F0=[N,F] is elementary, and J=1. We use this actual statement below, not quotient transversality.

In both first-layer cases the physical row W=H∩N maps onto the first Hall-fixed sector V0. Indeed let M=[N,B], normal in D by the usual commutator identity and Hall normalizer argument; its first-layer image is [V,B]. The group D/MB is a p-group, with MB normal because (NB)/M is a direct product over N/M and the Hall B is then unique. Normal generation by H gives surjection to D/MB; a preimage in H of an element of N/M can be powered by a multiple of 13 congruent to one modulo its image's 3-order, to lie in W. Hence W projects onto all V0. Normalize actual B2 in H by coprime conjugacy for necessary row constraints, which preserves cut sizes and core-freeness.

### Mixed first layer is impossible

When V is mixed, the moving lower packet cannot be axial: in the central quotient by F0 the moving lower packet is generated by powers and brackets of V; powers retain weights, brackets with fixed V0 stay mixed, and mixed plus mixed never becomes axial since neither coordinate of O+O contains zero. Therefore F_mov also has mixed weights and dimension nine. Its B2-representation and that of V_mov have no trivial weights; all irreducible degrees under B2 are three.

Write l=dim F0, v_+=dim V0^+, u=dim U_mov for the moving part of the image U of the actual W in V, and s=dim(W∩F). The actual W∩F misses F0 and is B2-stable, so it is inside F_mov (apply the fixed projector). The image U contains V0 by the prior surjection and B2 averaging. The exact physical size [N:W]=3^8, with dim V=10+v_+ and dim F=9+l, gives u+s=10+l. If l=0 this is impossible because u,s are multiples of three.

If l>0, the actual bilinear commutator V_mov tensor F_mov→F0 is onto as a span since F0=[N,F], F is abelian, and fixed V0 cannot pair Hall-equivariantly with the moving F. Select a nonzero linear functional on F0. Its scalar contraction is nonzero, is Hall and top invariant, and is nondegenerate on the two nine-dimensional moving spaces. Indeed over the splitting field it can pair only opposite weights; separate matched spaces have multiplicity one, and the joint top and Frobenius action is transitive on each nine-weight packet. A nonzero coefficient transfers to every matched coefficient, since Frobenius changes the coefficient by a field automorphism, which preserves nonzero. Thus all matched coefficients are nonzero (and the supports must be inverse, because at least one pair is hit). In the actual subgroup W one has [W,W∩F]⊂H∩F0=1, so the projected u-dimensional subspace and the actual s-dimensional lower subspace are orthogonal for this perfect contraction. This gives u+s≤9, contradicting 10+l. No mixed first layer survives.

### Axial first layer cannot have a fixed lower sector

For axial V there is just one irreducible degree-three packet per side and no mixed weights. The reflected lower pattern is excluded by the [multiplicity-one midpoint theorem](prime-top-moving-layer.md#odd-two-prime-reflected-first-movement-multiplicity-obstruction), so F_mov has one axial degree-three packet per side. Suppose l=dim F0>0. The actual central pairing V tensor F_mov→F0 now requires opposite Hall packets on either side, and its image has rank at most one: within each axis the actual top alone is transitive on the three matching weight pairs (so over the splitting field there is at most one top-invariant contraction parameter per axis), and sign exchanges axes but fixes the target. Since F0=[N,F] and is nonzero, l=1 and the pairing on each axis is perfect. Let F_i be the actual moving lower axis.

Modulo F0 the core is class two, with elementary lower layers and zero power map by Hall weights: first and lower axial packets are opposite, not equal, and there is no fixed lower target in this quotient. Each F_i is therefore supplied by the same-axis bracket, since the crossed C bracket cannot hit an opposite packet (its first Hall character would be unchanged), and mixed-axis brackets also cannot hit F_i. The map exterior² V_i→F_i is an isomorphism: surjectivity follows from the Frattini identity and each dimension is three. Mixed-axis brackets are zero modulo F0. These are enough even without determining upper brackets into F0.

Let P2 be the preimage of V2 inside C_N(B1), an actual subgroup with kernel F2⊕F0 by coprime lifting. It is D-normal: Hall normalizer preserves its layers, and conjugation by N changes its elements only by F2 and F0, by the preceding quotient bracket identities. The bracket on P2 gives all F2 modulo F0, and its perfect pairing with F2 gives all F0, hence Phi(P2)=F2⊕F0. If W had a nonzero second first-axis component, its B2-commutators would generate a subgroup of W∩P2 surjecting onto V2 (irreducible). The actual top stabilizes S and U_1 and makes f top-equivariant, since on V and F all inner N-actions are trivial. Signed index and row counting give
s+d_1+dim C^− = 2p−1.
For the proper signed-normal subgroup F C, physical H intersection has dimension s+dim ker f, and its fixed dimension is p+dim C^+. Thus the strict cut gives rank f≥p−d_1. This is the dimension of its codomain, so f is surjective.

The bracket of two physical row elements must lie in S, never assumed zero. Let representatives for their first axial coordinates be x,y∈K, with c,d in C where f(c)=x mod U_1 and f(d)=y mod U_1. Then
b(c)y−b(d)x ∈ S.
For c∈ker f one may take x=0 and choose y arbitrarily by surjectivity. A nonzero b(c) would send K onto K, not into proper S. Thus b=β f for a linear β:V_1/U_1→K. Set T=β composed with the quotient map K→V_1/U_1. Then the exact matched condition is
T(x)y−T(y)x ∈ S for all x,y∈K.
Equivalently T is self-adjoint for every nonzero trace form (x,y)↦Tr(ell x y) with ell in the trace annihilator S^perp. If codim S≥2, comparing two independent nonzero ell shows that T commutes with multiplication by their ratio. This ratio is outside F_p and generates the field extension of prime degree p. Hence T is field-scalar; genuine crossing makes it nonzero and invertible, so U_1=0 and dim C^−=2p−1−s≥p+1. If codim S=1, instead one has a single trace-self-adjoint operator with U_1 contained in its kernel. Together with top-equivariance this is the next exact operator problem. Neither operator branch is claimed physically realizable: p-powers, winding top lifts and all outside phases must still be checked.

### Hyperplane lower label: a polynomial, with odd rank

In the last branch s=p−1, choose field coordinates on V_1 and F_1 so that their top action is the same order-p Frobenius generator sigma. Indeed each is a one-dimensional K-space with a sigma-semilinear action whose pth power is identity (inner top carries act trivially on these layers); the scalar norm is 1, so finite-field Hilbert 90 removes it independently on each space. The normalized crossed scalar b transforms by sigma as well. Since S is top-invariant of codimension one, it is the unique invariant hyperplane ker Tr in this regular unipotent p-cycle module (use a normal basis or the rank of sigma−1). The operator T is sigma-equivariant from b=βf and f equivariant, so it is a polynomial in sigma, since a regular single Jordan block has polynomial centralizer. The trace pairing Tr(xy) makes the adjoint of sigma equal sigma^−1. In the centered variable Z=(sigma−1)/(sigma+1), adjoint sends Z to −Z. Therefore the self-adjoint operator is an even polynomial in Z. If nonzero its first occupied grade is even, so its kernel has even dimension and its rank is odd. Here dim C^−=p−d_1 and d_1≤dim ker T, giving dim C^−≥rank T. In the reflected-field size p=3 of this aligned axial branch, the only possible crossed ranks in the hyperplane case are one and three. These are necessary phases/row data, not a realization proof.
