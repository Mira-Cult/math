# Induced axial constructions at the higher-rotation minima

**Mira Cult**

The construction below gives the examples needed for the [sharp abelian minimum](0135-higher-cyclic-abelian-cores.md). It uses the [signed normal-cut criterion](../reviews/first-degree-prime-pair-upstream-necessity-audit.md#physical-signed-input).

**Theorem.** Let $`p,q`$ be distinct odd primes, $`k\ge2`$, and $`n=p^k\mid q-1`$. Write $`f=\mathrm{ord}_q(p)`$ and $`d=\mathrm{lcm}(n,f)`$. There is a finite simple proper left Bol loop of order $`q p^{kd+k}`$, with canonical cyclic top of order $`n`$ and abelian canonical $`p`$-core of exponent $`p^k`$ whose nontrivial weights are purely axial. No unrestricted minimality is claimed.

Let $`g=\gcd(n,f)`$, $`m=n/g`$, and choose $`\alpha`$ of order $`n`$ in $`\mathbb F_q^\times`$. Put $`E=\mathrm{GR}(p^k,f)`$ with Teichmüller root $`\zeta`$ of order $`q`$. The intersection of the cyclic groups generated by $`p`$ and $`\alpha`$ modulo $`q`$ has order $`g`$. It is generated by $`\alpha^m`$, so there is an automorphism $`\varphi`$ of $`E`$ induced by lifted Frobenius, with $`\varphi(\zeta)=\zeta^{\alpha^m}`$, of order $`g`$. On $`A=E^m`$ define $`b`$ by multiplication by $`\zeta^{\alpha^{-i}}`$ on block $`0\le i<m`$. Let $`R`$ shift each block index up one, applying $`\varphi`$ on wrap from $`m-1`$ to zero. Then $`R b R^{-1}=b^\alpha`$: away from wrap the scalar from block $`i-1`$ is $`\zeta^{\alpha^{1-i}}`$, and at wrap it is $`\varphi(\zeta^{\alpha^{1-m}})=\zeta^\alpha`$. Also $`R^m`$ is $`\varphi`$ on every block and $`R^n=1`$.

On the residue field the group generated by $`\varphi`$ is the full Galois group over its fixed field of size $`p^{f/g}`$. Choose a normal generator over this fixed field, multiply it by a prime-field basis of the fixed field and lift the $`f/g`$ products to $`E`$. Their $`\varphi`$-translates reduce to a full prime-field basis, so are an integral basis modulo $`p^k`$. Placing the lifts in block zero and applying all powers of $`R`$ gives $`f/g`$ regular cycles of length $`n`$ which together form a $`\mathbb Z/p^k`$-basis of $`A`$. Take $`x`$ a generator of one cycle and let $`F:A\to\mathbb Z/p^k`$ sum coefficients in that cycle, zero on the others. Then $`FR=F`$ and $`F(x)=1`$.

Use $`A_1=A_2=A`$, $`N=A_1\oplus A_2`$, and $`B=B_1B_2\cong C_q^2`$, each $`B_i`$ acting as $`b`$ on its own axis and trivially on the other. Put $`D=N\rtimes(B\rtimes\langle h\rangle)`$ where $`h`$ has order $`n`$, acts by $`(R,R^{-1})`$ on $`N`$ and scales $`B`$ by $`(\alpha,\alpha^{-1})`$. The sign $`t`$ exchanges the two $`A`$- and $`B`$-axes and inverts $`h`$. The quotient by $`N`$ has no nontrivial normal $`p`$-subgroup, since it would centralize $`B`$ on which the top acts faithfully. Thus $`N=O_p(D)`$. With $`K=\ker F`$ set $`H=\langle (K,0),B_2,xh\rangle`$. The lift normalizes $`(K,0)`$ and $`B_2`$, and its $`n`$th power has first axis augmentation $`n=0`$. Thus $`H\cap N=(K,0)`$ and $`H`$ has index $`q|A|n=q p^{kd+k}`$. The inverted set $`T`$ consists of the products of the unique outside involution class $`Tt`$ by $`t`$. Its size is the same $`q|A|n`$, by coprime fixed lifting, the swaps, and inversion of the odd top.

For a collision in $`(Tt)^2\cap H`$, project to $`D/N`$. Nonidentity pure $`B_2`$ cannot be inverted by either outside involution because they exchange the axes. If the projection is identity, the two projected signs coincide and both moving summands lie in the same negative graph from $`A_1`$ to $`A_2`$; zero second coordinate forces the combined vector to vanish. If the projection has nonzero top $`j`$, conjugate by an actual $`B_2`$ element of $`H`$ to make it $`h^j`$ (the rotation is fixed-point-free on $`B_2`$). An outside sign inverting this power has zero $`B`$-shift, because all of $`B`$ is fixed-point-free under $`h^j`$. The two signs project to $`h^i t,h^{i-j}t`$. In their product the moving contributions lie respectively in the negative graphs of $`h^i t,h^{i+j}t`$, namely $`(u,-R^{-i}u)`$ and $`(u',-R^{-i-j}u')`$. Zero second coordinate forces $`u'=-R^ju`$, making the first coordinate lie in $`(R^j-1)A_1`$, with augmentation zero. But the actual $`H`$ coset over that top power has augmentation $`j`$ modulo $`n`$, nonzero. Thus avoidance holds, and the mark count gives a sharply transitive, inverse- and sandwich-closed folder.

The subgroup generated by $`T`$ is normal, since it is also generated by the conjugacy-invariant $`(Tt)^2`$. It contains $`h`$; commutators with $`B`$ give $`B`$, and then commutators with $`N`$ give $`N`$, since $`\zeta^u-1`$ is a unit for $`u\ne0\bmod q`$. To see core-freeness and strict cuts, a $`B`$-invariant subgroup of $`N`$ decomposes into its separate axes by coprime averaging and then into the $`m`$ blocks on each axis. Indeed the Frobenius cosets of $`\alpha^{-i}`$ are distinct for $`0\le i<m`$, since $`\alpha^s`$ belongs to the group generated by $`p`$ precisely when $`m\mid s`$. The distinct factors over the residue field of the $`q`$th cyclotomic polynomial lift to pairwise coprime factors over $`\mathbb Z/p^k`$ and yield block projectors via the Chinese remainder theorem. In each block an invariant subgroup is an ideal $`p^r E`$ because the local primitive root generates the ring over $`\mathbb Z/p^k`$. Rotation transitivity across the blocks forces common $`r`$ within each axis for $`D`$-normal subgroups, and sign swaps the two axis exponents for signed-normal subgroups. A nonzero normal subgroup inside $`(K,0)`$ would have to contain $`p^r A_1`$ for some $`r<k`$, impossible since $`F(p^r x)=p^r\ne0`$. A normal subgroup disjoint from $`N`$ centralizes $`N`$, hence is trivial: the action of the quotient on $`N`$ is faithful, since a quotient element acting trivially on $`N`$ must act trivially by conjugation on the faithfully represented $`B`$, so its top is zero, and then faithfulness of $`B`$ on $`N`$ makes the whole quotient element trivial. This gives core-freeness.

For a nonzero signed-normal $`I\le N`$, its form is $`p^r A_1\oplus p^r A_2`$; the fixed diagonal has size $`|p^r A|`$ while its actual stabilizer intersection is strictly smaller, by $`F(p^r x)\ne0`$. For a signed-normal subgroup not contained in $`N`$, its image contains both $`q`$-axes, by the distinct reciprocal eigenvalues and their sign exchange if the image is contained in $`B`$, and by commutators with $`B`$ otherwise. Sylow conjugacy in $`NB`$ gives $`B\le I`$, hence $`N=[N,B]\le I`$. In the nontrivial quotient by a proper such $`I`$, the projected stabilizer is full because $`H`$ covers the cyclic top, while the projected marks generate and have size greater than one. These are all strict cuts. The loop is simple and nonassociative, the latter because its faithful stabilizer is nontrivial; odd-order Moufang solvability makes it proper. Its weight assertion follows from the two axial actions.

## The ternary order-nine minimum

For sharpness, take the induced moving module $`A`$ of the construction above reduced modulo 3, using $`n=9`$ in its character orbit and block wrap. It has dimension $`d`$ with rotation a sum of regular 9-cycles. Use it in place of the two copies of the field in the [physical order-nine proof](third-degree-ternary-order-nine-top-construction.md); retain the same upper elementary group $`C=\mathbb F_3v\oplus\mathbb F_3 w`$, $`Jv=w`$, $`Jw=0`$, and signs $`(-,+)`$. On one regular cycle choose a length-nine $`(R-1)`$ chain with top vector $`x`$; take $`K`$ omitting only $`x`$, and let $`\eta((R-1)x)=v`$, $`\eta((R-1)^2x)=w`$, with all other chain vectors and cycles mapped to zero. This is exactly the stabilizer of that proof, whose lift closes and whose phase avoidance depends only on the rotation and that chain, not on the $`q`$ action inside the moving modules. The counts now use $`d`$ in place of $`f`$ and give order $`3^{d+3}q`$.

The only place needing a new check is the actual normal structure on the moving part. Coprime averaging separates the two axes and the fixed part. Within an axis, the distinct rational $`q`$-weight blocks of the induced module are simple $`\mathbb F_3 B_i`$-modules and are permuted transitively by $`h`$. Thus a $`D`$-normal subgroup in the moving axis is zero or the whole axis, exactly as needed for the core-free argument; the triangular graph misses $`C`$, misses the second axis, and its first projection is proper. Also the quotient acts faithfully on the axes by the reciprocal scaling of $`B`$ and the faithful action of each $`B_i`$. For signed-normal cuts inside $`N`$ the two axes are present together or absent together, and in the latter case a nonzero $`C`$ part contains a positive fixed line. In the former case the exact order-nine proof gives strict cut using the onto $`\eta`$. Outside $`N`$ the quotient image contains both $`q`$-axes, so Sylow conjugacy and commutation give actual $`B,M`$ in the normal subgroup and the stabilizer image is full in the remaining quotient. Generation follows by $`h,v`$, and their commutators with $`B`$ and the moving part as in that proof. Thus all signed-folder checks hold for the induced module, and $`d+3`$ is attained, with no assumption $`9\mid f`$.
