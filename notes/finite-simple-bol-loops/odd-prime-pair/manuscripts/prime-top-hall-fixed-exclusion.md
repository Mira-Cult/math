# Eliminating Hall-fixed Frattini subgroups at the prime-top family orders

**Mira Cult**

For $`\mathrm{ord}_q(p)=p`$, no loop at $`qp^{2p+2}`$ has its canonical Hall subgroup fixing the Frattini subgroup of the normal $`p`$-core. We first give the physical class-two reduction, then the exact small-characteristic and uniform exclusions.

## The physical row forces class two and exponent p for Hall-fixed Frattini
Use its ordinary core-free physical H and normal generation by H, as supplied by simplicity and properness via the existing normal-cut criterion. Let N=O_p(D), F=Phi(N), and suppose the actual Hall complement B of order q² centralizes F. Then F is central in N, and N has class at most two and exponent p. No assumption of abelian F, deficit size, or centrality in D is made. The result applies in particular to every residual e=1 candidate by the [Hall centralization theorem](prime-top-moving-layer.md#odd-two-prime-one-deficit-hall-centrality), superseding the weaker class-four and exponent-p² bounds from layer algebra alone.

### Actual Hall splitting and normal quotients

Write C=C_N(B) and M=[N,B], using M generated by n^−1 n^b. The Hall group acts trivially on F, so n and n^b induce identical automorphisms of F, and M centralizes F. The subgroup C is normal in N: for c∈C, its N-conjugates differ from c by commutators in F and so are still B-fixed. For any n and b, the induced conjugation of n and n^b on C is identical since C is fixed by B and normalized by n. Therefore [C,M]=1.

M is normal in N from the identity (nz)^−1(nz)^b=(n^−1 n^b)^z(z^−1 z^b), and normalized by N_D(B) by its definition. The preimage NB of the reciprocal outer Hall layer is normal in D. Its q-complements are N-conjugate by Schur–Zassenhaus, whence D=N N_D(B): a conjugate of B under any d is restored by an element of N. Thus M is normal in D. Similarly C is normalized by N_D(B) and already normal in N, hence D-normal. The images of C and M in V=N/F are its Hall-fixed and moving parts, respectively, by coprime fixed lifting and averaging. Their images span V, so the Frattini generation theorem gives N=CM. This is an actual commuting product, not a direct product assertion.

The group NB/M is (N/M)×B because B acts trivially on N/M, and B intersects M trivially. Its unique q-Hall subgroup is the image of B: a q-element has trivial projection into the p-group N/M. Consequently MB is normal in D, and D/MB is a finite p-group (extension of N/M by the reciprocal cyclic p-top).

### Surjectivity of the physical row and the commutator argument

The canonical simple proper loop reduction gives HD′=D: see [the quotient-to-abelian-group argument](../../packet/manuscripts/odd-folder-radicals.md#normal-closure-of-the-fixed-stabilizer). The normal closure of H must be D, since otherwise its quotient would be a nontrivial finite solvable perfect group. Thus normal generation is an inherited envelope consequence, not derived from permutation core-freeness. Hence the image of H normally generates D/MB. A proper subgroup of a finite p-group lies in a normal maximal subgroup, so this image is the entire quotient. Let W=H∩N. Then W maps onto the actual N/M, not merely onto its Frattini image: take a representative h∈H of any u in N/M inside D/MB. Its top coordinate is zero, so h∈NB. Choose an integer j divisible by q and congruent to 1 modulo the p-power order of u. Since hN belongs to the exponent-q B layer, h^j lies in W; its image is still u. Thus for every c∈C there is w=c m with w∈W and m∈M.

As M centralizes F, its own commutators are central in M, and its p-th powers belong to F. Hence M has class at most two and its commutators have exponent dividing p by the class-two identity [x,y]^p=[x^p,y]. For any c1,c2,c3 in C, choose actual w_i=c_i m_i in W. The cross commutators vanish and the M triple does too, so [[c1,c2],c3]=[[w1,w2],w3]∈H. These triples generate the third lower-central subgroup of C, which is characteristic in the D-normal C. Ordinary core-freeness forces it to vanish. Both C and M, and thus N, have class at most two. Next [w1,w2]=[c1,c2][m1,m2]; taking p-th powers gives [c1,c2]^p∈H because the M commutator has exponent p. The subgroup these powers generate is characteristic in C and D-normal, and again lies in H, so vanishes. Thus all of [N,N] has exponent dividing p, and N^p is central by the class-two commutator identity. This proves F=N^p[N,N]≤Z(N).

For odd p the class-two power map x↦x^p is now a homomorphism N→F, since the commutator correction is raised to a multiple of p. It is equivariant for B with B-fixed target, so it kills all generators n^−1 n^b of M. For each c∈C the actual w=c m yields w^p=c^p∈H. The group C^p is again characteristic in C and D-normal, and it lies in H; hence it is trivial. Therefore N has exponent p, and F=[N,N] is elementary. This final exclusion uses the physical row and is stronger than the previous e=1 exponent-p² result.

### The upper/moving matching condition

Also H∩F=1, despite F not necessarily central in D. It is normalized by N and B since both centralize F; it is stabilized by an element of H mapping to a generator of the outer cyclic top; these generate D with N and B. B_0(x,y)=−B_1,red(f(x),f(y)).
These equations express the required matching, not merely a rank budget. For example if the moving-row perpendicular quotient has zero induced bracket, the whole Hall-fixed subgroup C must have zero bracket. The upper bracket can no longer be chosen independently of the lower row. The equations are necessary; the true lower-label, top lifts and outside-phase tests still determine whether an envelope is realized. No Hall-moving Φ case or whole-order census is claimed.

### Sign costs and a uniform moving bracket bound

In this family the nonzero weight orbits on an axial side have length p under both the top and Frobenius group, and an axial signed pair has dimension 2p and negative cost p. A reflected mixed rational packet has dimension p² and negative cost p(p−1)/2 with positive midpoint signs, or p(p+1)/2 with negative midpoint signs: the p reflected top orbits are permuted by Frobenius, including their p midpoints. Its weights lie in a rectangle O×O for a size-p scalar orbit O (reflection means the two initial coordinates are in the same orbit). A sign-exchanged mixed package costs p² negatives, since its Frobenius orbit of odd length p cannot contain its sign partner unless sign preserves each top orbit. These statements also imply that no single package supports inverse weights on the same axis or in a mixed rectangle, since the scalar group has odd order. A higher multiplicity repeats the corresponding one-dimensional midpoint-sign or exchanged contributions.

More generally in any odd reciprocal Hall-fixed Φ branch, for any nonzero top-invariant functional ell on F, put r=rank of its alternating contraction on V_mov=[V,B]. Then a≥dim F+r/2: the actual row intersection with V_mov has dimension dim V_mov+dim F−a and is isotropic for that contraction. One may choose ell also a sign eigenfunctional, since the top coinvariant module is sign stable; its contraction is nonzero as moving brackets span F. In a general reciprocal top n=p^k and f=ord_q(p), put L=lcm(n,f). If an axial match is active, the Hall pair of sides gives rank at least 4L if f is odd and 2L if f is even, by propagating a nonzero rank through the joint top/Frobenius orbit, its inverse orbit and its sign partner. If a mixed match is active its joint orbit has size nf; the inverse orbit coincides only if f is even, giving ranks at least 2nf or nf respectively. These are lower bounds on contraction rank, not automatic nonsingularity in the presence of repeated packets. In the family-order slices below the active weight spaces have multiplicity one, so this argument gives perfect contractions on the respective dual support.

### Nonzero F: only two dual axial packets before the physical tests

The genuine F bracket requires opposite moving weights. The negative budget in V is at most 2p+1. Thus there must be exactly two cheapest axial packages, dual to each other, or two reflected mixed dual packages (the latter can fit only at p=3). No single moving package or non-dual pair can supply F, and no third packet fits. The mixed dual pair is excluded by the perfect alternating contraction: the actual row in its 2p²-dimensional moving space has dimension 2p²+dim F−(2p+2)>p². Thus there are two dual axial packets, with first/second sides of dimension 2p each, and e=dim F^−≤1. The Hall-fixed first sector V0 has negative dimension 1−e. With no negative heads available it is zero for e=1, and for e=0 it is the singleton or length-two (−,+) by the first-layer theorem.

Normalize actual B2 in H by coprime conjugacy for necessary tests (ordinary core, signed-cut sizes and mark avoidance are conjugation invariant). Its two nonisomorphic irreducibles on the second side force U2, the B2-moving part of the projected isotropic row, to be either zero or exactly one degree-p packet. It cannot be both by the perfect alternating contraction on that side. The first row part U1=U∩V1 has dimension at most p. Thus U2 has dimension p, U1 has dimension p+dim F−2, and dim F≤2. If e=0, F is central in D and dim F=1: the invariant moving bracket of dimension-one inverse weight lines has just one coefficient per axial side after top transitivity, and sign relates those two sides, so its quotient onto a trivial F has dimension at most one.

The following negative-line rule works uniformly beyond this family order. If A is an actual elementary signed-normal subgroup centralized by N and B (for instance Hall-fixed F), every signed Jordan chain of its top action ends positive. Indeed any negative vector in the kernel of R_A−1 spans a proper signed-normal negative line, impossible by the strict cut, whose fixed size would be one. The centered operator anti-commutes with sign, so alternating homogeneous chains apply. In particular rank(R_A−1)≤2 dim A^−, and the maximum chain length is 2 dim A^−+1. For e=1 this permits a length-two (−,+) or length-three (+,−,+) active block, and forbids both a negative singleton and the opposite length-two orientation; no assertion is made that the length-three possibility is impossible at larger orders.

Here e=1 and dim F≤2 therefore leaves only length-two (−,+). Let X2=U2, X1 its sign partner and Y_i the dual packets. The proper signed-normal preimage of X1⊕X2 (including F) has fixed dimension p+1 and actual H intersection dimension p+dim(U1∩X1). Strictness forces U1∩X1=0, and U1 now projects onto all Y1. On the top coinvariant axial plus traces, U2 supplies X and U1 supplies Y, hence their sign-symmetric images span. As in the [literal p=3 proof](prime-top-hall-fixed-exclusion.md#odd-q13-order-85293-hall-fixed-no-go), choose an actual inverted reference h normalizing B and normalize the H lift to have Hall zero. Then D/F has split top since h^p lies in C_N(B)=F. Adjust the projected H top generator by the row to kill its sign-symmetric trace. The resulting top-one element is conjugate to its signed inverse in D/F, hence a difference of two quotient marks of distinct top residues. In the true F module their affine fibre directions are distinct lines (the length-two action has order p), so the two directions span F and the lifted differences fill the whole coset. In particular they hit the actual H representative, an actual contradiction.

For e=0, let C=C_N(B), an elementary group central in N because M centralizes it and its own bracket is zero: V0 is either a singleton or has signs (−,+), whose wedge is negative while F is positive. The actual C positive subspace is top-fixed: when V0 is a singleton this is F, and for the length-two case the centered top operator kills positive vectors since it kills their projected terminal and its remaining image would be negative in positive F. If V0 is singleton the negative C vector must move nontrivially into F by the strict negative-line rule, and if V0 is length two it moves to a nonzero positive lift of the terminal. Hence W∩C=1: any positive subgroup there would violate ordinary core, and top stability of a negative-containing vector would generate a positive one. Then the B2-fixed physical row of dimension p−1+dim V0 injects isotropically into V1, excluding length-two V0. For singleton V0, this p-dimensional row projects onto all Y1. Otherwise its Y kernel has nonzero X1 (since W∩C=1); multiply by the full X2 row to make the first two moving vectors signed negatives of one another. Its upper sign is negative modulo F, and any F discrepancy can be erased by conjugation in N using the perfect moving bracket. This would give an actual top-zero element in the mark difference set. So the dual plus traces are again full, giving a projected top-one collision. The actual negative C vector's top shear sweeps the whole F via (1−R_C^−1)c when conjugating a top-minus-one element, so it lifts to actual inverse-conjugacy and violates transversality. This argument uses the same outside-inverter-to-involution implication as in the literal p=3 proof; it is valid in all odd orders. An inverted reference really splits here (its pth power must be top-fixed and negative in C, which has no such line).

### Elementary case

Here F=1 and the first sign budget is 2p+1. A single axial pair has moving dimension 2p<a, meaning the row cannot project onto its entire Hall-fixed first sector, while a single mixed packet has nontrivial B2 action throughout and needs its moving row dimension p²−(2p+2) divisible by p, impossible for every odd p. A sign-exchanged mixed packet cannot fit. For p≥7 no reflected mixed packet fits; for p=5 a positive-midpoint reflected one cannot be combined with any other moving package. At p=3 the additional possibilities are exactly those in the [literal weight cover](prime-top-hall-fixed-exclusion.md#odd-q13-order-85293-hall-fixed-cover): two mixed positive-midpoint packets give moving row dimension ten, not divisible by three, and one axial plus one mixed positive-midpoint is kept below for the physical test. All that remains generally is two axial packets (possibly repeated), or that p=3 axial-plus-mixed type.

In either remaining type V0 has one negative and is the singleton or (−,+) length two. The singleton is signed-normal and wholly negative, impossible. With length two, the split negative reference has h^p=1 because C_N(B)=V0 has no top-fixed negative. Ordinary core forces W∩V0=0 by actual top stability. With two axial packets W is B2-fixed because its possible second component is itself D-normal (B2 stable, B1 trivial, actual top stable since N is abelian). Its dimension is 2p, so it projects onto the whole first side. With p=3 axial-plus-mixed, the B2-fixed row has dimension three and projects onto the entire first axial three-space: its moving kernel has dimension seven, precisely one fixed dimension there and six moving dimensions by the degree-three B2 restriction. Consequently in either type an actual Hall-zero H top lift, normalized in the B2-normalizer, can be modified by W^{B2} to have no moving coordinate at all, since its N-correction is B2-fixed. It becomes c h with c∈V0. On that length-two chain t(c)+R^−1 c has only positive terminal, hit by 1−R^−1, giving actual inverse-conjugacy. This is an actual forbidden mark difference. All Hall-fixed possibilities at this family order have now failed.

The exact threshold family of known constructions lies in Hall-moving Φ and is not excluded here. For p=3 the result is the earlier complete Hall-fixed exclusion; for all larger odd primes it gives the same structural necessity without any enumeration of charts or lifts.
