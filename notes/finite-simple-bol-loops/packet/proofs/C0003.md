### 1. The Infinite Solvable-`LMlt` Prime-Power Semilinear Family $Q_{p,q}$ ($|Q| = p^{p+1} q$)

#### Theorem 1.1 (Construction, Simplicity, Boundary Condition, Uniqueness, and $\operatorname{Aut}(Q_{p,q})$)
Let $p \ge 2$ be any prime, set $V = \mathbb{F}_{p^p}$, let $\phi : V \to V$ be the Frobenius automorphism $\phi(z) = z^p$ ($\operatorname{ord}(\phi) = p$), and let $q$ be a prime divisor of $p^p - 1$ with unique subgroup $C_q \le \mathbb{F}_{p^p}^\times$. Inside $\operatorname{A\Gamma L}_1(p^p)$, put $X_{p,q} := V \rtimes (C_q \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle)$ ($|X_{p,q}| = p^{p+1} q$), $Y_1 := C_q \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle$ ($|Y_1| = p q$), and $W := \ker(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^p}/\mathbb{F}_p})$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} W = p - 1$).
1. **Exact factorization for every $c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$**: Pick $v_c \in V$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(v_c) = c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and define $g_c(z) := \phi(z) + v_c = (v_c, \phi) \in X_{p,q}$ and $Y_0^{(c)} := \langle W, g_c \rangle$. Since $\operatorname{Tr}(1) = p \cdot 1 = 0$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$, we have $\mathbb{F}_p \subseteq W$. Moreover,
   $$g_c^p = \Bigl(\sum_{r=0}^{p-1} \phi^r(v_c),\; \phi^p\Bigr) = (\operatorname{Tr}(v_c), 1) = (c, 1) \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{0\} \subseteq W \setminus \{0\},$$
   so $g_c$ normalizes $W$ with $g_c^p \in W$, giving $|Y_0^{(c)}| = p^p$ ($Y_0^{(c)} \neq V$). For any $y = (w, 1) g_c^j \in Y_0^{(c)}$ with $w \in W$ and $1 \le j < p$, $y^p = (\operatorname{Tr}(w + \sum_{r=0}^{j-1} \phi^r(v_c)), 1) = (j c, 1) \neq (0, 1)$, so every non-identity element of $Y_0^{(c)}$ acts fixed-point-freely on $V$. Hence $Y_0^{(c)}$ acts regularly on $V$, $X_{p,q} = Y_0^{(c)} Y_1$, and $Y_0^{(c)} \cap Y_1 = 1$.
2. **Failure of simplicity when $q \mid p - 1$ (correcting Nagy MH'17 Slide 17)**: Since $\gcd\bigl(p - 1, \frac{p^p - 1}{p - 1}\bigr) = \gcd(p - 1, p) = 1$, either $q \mid p - 1$ or $q \mid \frac{p^p - 1}{p - 1}$. If $q \mid p - 1$, then $C_q \le \mathbb{F}_p^\times = \operatorname{Fix}(\phi)$, so $[\phi, C_q] = 1$, $X_{p,q} / V \cong C_q \times C_p$ is abelian, and $X_{p,q}' \le V$. Then $X_{p,q}' Y_0^{(c)} = V \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle \trianglelefteq X_{p,q}$ has index $q > 1$, so by `C0002` Proposition 1.1(3), **$Q_{(X_{p,q}, Y_0^{(c)}, Y_1)}$ has a proper nontrivial normal subloop of index $q$** (and surjects onto $C_q$).
3. **Simplicity and solvable $\operatorname{LMlt}$ when $q \mid \frac{p^p - 1}{p - 1}$**: Assume $q \mid \frac{p^p - 1}{p - 1}$ (for odd $p$, $\frac{p^p - 1}{p - 1} \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ and $\operatorname{ord}_q(p) = p \implies q \equiv 1 \pmod{2p}$ is odd, so $|X_{p,q}| = p^{p+1} q$ is odd). Because $q \nmid p - 1$ and $p$ is prime, $\operatorname{ord}_q(p) = p$, so $C_q$ acts irreducibly on $V \cong \mathbb{F}_p^p$ with $C_V(C_q) = 0$, and $[\phi, C_q] = C_q$. Hence $X' := X_{p,q}' = V \rtimes C_q$ ($|X'| = p^p q$), $\operatorname{Core}_{X_{p,q}}(Y_0^{(c)}) = \operatorname{Core}_{X_{p,q}}(Y_1) = 1$, and $X' Y_0^{(c)} = (V \rtimes C_q) \langle g_c \rangle = X_{p,q} = X' Y_1$. Since $C_V(Y_1) = 0$, $N_{X_{p,q}}(Y_1) = Y_1 = C_q \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle$; inside $Y_1$, $C_q$ permutes the $q$ Sylow $p$-subgroups transitively while $\phi g_c \phi^{-1} = (\phi(v_c) - v_c, 1) g_c \in W g_c \subseteq Y_0^{(c)}$ (as $\operatorname{Tr}(\phi(v_c) - v_c) = 0$). By `C0002` Proposition 1.1, $(\hat{G}, \hat{H}, S)$ is faithful with **solvable** $\operatorname{LMlt}(Q) \cong \hat{G}$ of order $p^{2p+1} q^2$ ($|\hat{H}| = |X'| = p^p q$), $Q$ is non-Moufang, and $N_\rho(Q) = N_{X_{p,q}}(Y_0^{(c)}) \cap N_{X_{p,q}}(Y_1) = \langle \phi \rangle \cong C_p$.
   To prove simplicity via `C0001` Theorem 2.2(3), let $1 \neq N \trianglelefteq \hat{G}$. Since $C_{\hat{G}}(V \times V) = V \times V$ and $V \times 1, 1 \times V$ are non-isomorphic irreducible $\mathbb{F}_p[C_q \times C_q]$-modules ($C_q \times C_q = O^p(X' \times X') \le \hat{G}$), $N \cap (V \times V) \in \{V \times 1, 1 \times V, V \times V\}$:
   - If $N \cap (V \times V) = V \times 1$, then $[N, 1 \times X'] \le N \cap (1 \times V) = 1$, so $N \le C_{\hat{G}}(1 \times X') = X' \times 1$ (since $C_{X_{p,q}}(X') = 1$); as in `C0002`, $|S N / N| = |X_{p,q}| > [\hat{G} : \hat{H} N]$ by $\operatorname{Core}_{\hat{G}}(\hat{H}) = 1$. Symmetrically if $N \cap (V \times V) = 1 \times V$.
   - Suppose $V \times V \le N$, and put $\bar{N} := N / (V \times V) \trianglelefteq \hat{G} / (V \times V) \cong \{(a \phi^i, b \phi^{-i}) : a, b \in C_q, i \in \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\} \cong (C_q \times C_q) \rtimes C_p$. Modulo $V \times V$, $\hat{H} = \{(y_0, y_1) \in Y_0^{(c)} \times Y_1 : y_0 y_1 \in V \rtimes C_q\}$ projects onto $\overline{\hat{H}} = \{(\phi^i, b \phi^{-i}) : b \in C_q, i \in \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\}$ of order $p q$ (containing $1 \times C_q$), with $\hat{H} \cap (V \times V) = W \times 1$ of order $p^{p-1}$.
     * If $\bar{N} \not\le 1 \times C_q$, then $\bar{N}$ contains an element outside $\overline{\hat{H}}$ (since $|\overline{\hat{H}}| = p q$ has prime index $q$ in $(C_q \times C_q) \rtimes C_p$, and $\overline{\hat{H}}$ is not normal as $C_q \times 1$ moves $(\phi, \phi^{-1})$), so $\bar{N} \overline{\hat{H}} = \hat{G} / (V \times V)$ and $\hat{H} N = \hat{G}$ ($[\hat{G} : \hat{H} N] = 1$).
     * If $\bar{N} \le 1 \times C_q$ (i.e. $N = V \times V$ or $N = V \times X'$), then since $1 \times C_q \le \overline{\hat{H}}$, we have $\hat{H} N = \hat{H}(V \times V)$ of order $\frac{|\hat{H}| \cdot |V \times V|}{|\hat{H} \cap (V \times V)|} = \frac{(p^p q) \cdot p^{2p}}{p^{p-1}} = p^{2p+1} q$, giving $[\hat{G} : \hat{H} N] = q$. Meanwhile $(x, x^{-1}) N = (y, y^{-1}) N \implies x^{-1} y \in V \iff x V = y V$, so $|S N / N| = [X_{p,q} : V] = p q > q = [\hat{G} : \hat{H} N]$.
   Hence **no nontrivial proper normal subloop exists**, and $Q_{p,q} := Q_{(X_{p,q}, Y_0^{(1)}, Y_1)}$ is a finite simple proper left Bol $G$-loop ($p = 2, q = 3$ gives $Q_{24}$; $p = 3, q = 13$ gives odd-order $Q_{1053}$).
4. **Uniqueness per $(p, q)$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(Q_{p,q}) \cong C_p$**: In $X = X_{p,q}$, every complement $Y_1'$ of order $p q$ to $V$ is $V$-conjugate to $Y_1$ (by Schur–Zassenhaus on $V \rtimes C_q$ and Sylow on $N_X(C_q) = Y_1$, since $C_V(C_q) = 0$). Any regular subgroup $Y_0 \neq V$ of order $p^p$ lies in one of the $q$ Sylow $p$-subgroups of $X$; inside $U = V \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle$, $Y_0 \cap V$ has index $p$ and is $\phi$-invariant containing $[\phi, V] = W$, hence equals $W$, so $Y_0 = \langle W, (v, \phi) \rangle$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(v) = c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ ($c \neq 0$ else $(v, \phi)^p = (0, 1) \in Y_1$). Since $W$-conjugation preserves $\operatorname{Tr}(v)$, there are exactly $p - 1$ choices $Y_0^{(c)}$ ($c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$) in $U$ and $q(p - 1)$ in $X$. Conjugation by $C_q \le Y_1$ permutes the $q$ Sylow $p$-subgroups transitively while normalizing $Y_1$, and multiplication by $\lambda \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times \le \operatorname{Aut}(X)$ centralizes $Y_1$ and maps $Y_0^{(1)} \mapsto Y_0^{(\lambda)}$, so **all valid factorizations of $X_{p,q}$ yield a single loop $Q_{p,q}$ up to isomorphism**.
   For $\operatorname{Aut}(Q_{p,q})$: in $\hat{G}$, the unique minimal normal subgroups are $V \times 1$ and $1 \times V$. Because $|Y_0| = p^p \neq p q = |Y_1|$ ($q = p^{p-1}$ is impossible for primes $p, q$), no folder automorphism swaps the two coordinates; since $O^p(C_{\hat{G}}(1 \times V)) = X' \times 1$, $O^p(C_{\hat{G}}(V \times 1)) = 1 \times X'$, and $C_X(X') = 1$, every folder automorphism has the form $\alpha \times \alpha$ for $\alpha \in N_{\operatorname{Aut}(X)}(Y_1) \cap N_{\operatorname{Aut}(X)}(Y_0^{(1)})$. Because $C_V(Y_1) = 0$ and $C_q$ acts faithfully on $V$ with $\mathbb{F}_p[C_q] = \mathbb{F}_{p^p}$, restriction $\alpha \mapsto \alpha|_V$ embeds $N_{\operatorname{Aut}(X)}(Y_1)$ onto $N_{\operatorname{\Gamma L}_1(p^p)}(C_q \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle) = (C_q \times \mathbb{F}_p^\times) \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle$ of order $q(p - 1)p$. Since this group acts transitively on the $q(p - 1)$ regular subgroups $Y_0 \neq V$, Orbit–Stabilizer gives $|N_{\operatorname{Aut}(X)}(Y_1) \cap N_{\operatorname{Aut}(X)}(Y_0^{(1)})| = p$, generated by $\operatorname{Inn}(\phi)$, proving **$\operatorname{Aut}(Q_{p,q}) \cong C_p$**. $\square$

### 2. The Solvable-Wreath Simple Proper Left Bol Loop $Q_{1152}$ ($X = S_4 \wr C_2$)

#### Theorem 2.1 (Simplicity and Uniqueness of $Q_{1152}$ Outside Theorem 1.1)
Let $X = S_4 \wr C_2 = (S_4 \times S_4) \rtimes \langle t \rangle \le S_8$ ($|X| = 2 \cdot 24^2 = 1152 = 2^7 \cdot 3^2$, solvable, with $V_{16} := O_2(X) = V_4 \times V_4 \cong 2^4$, $|X'| = 288$, and $X'' = A_4 \times A_4$ of order $144$). Put $Y_1 := S_3 \wr C_2 = \operatorname{Stab}_X(\{0, 4\})$ ($|Y_1| = 72$), and let $Y_0 := \langle \alpha, \beta \rangle \cong \operatorname{SD}_{16}$ ($|Y_0| = 16$) where $\alpha = (0, 4, 1, 5, 2, 6, 3, 7)$ ($\operatorname{ord}(\alpha) = 8$) and $\beta = (1, 3)(4, 5)(6, 7)$ ($\operatorname{ord}(\beta) = 2$, $\beta \alpha \beta = \alpha^3$).
1. **Exact factorization and faithfulness**: Note $\alpha^4 = (0, 2)(1, 3)(4, 6)(5, 7) \in Z(\operatorname{SD}_{16}) \cap V_{16}$. Because $\operatorname{SD}_{16}$ has maximal subgroups $C_8$, $D_8$, and $Q_8$, it contains no normal subgroup isomorphic to $V_4$, while $V_{16}$ is elementary abelian of exponent $2$; as $\beta \notin V_{16}$ ($\beta$ fixes $0, 2$), $Y_0 \cap V_{16}$ is a normal elementary abelian subgroup of $Y_0$ of order $< 4$, hence $Y_0 \cap X'' = Y_0 \cap V_{16} = \langle \alpha^4 \rangle \cong C_2$. Furthermore, the $16$ elements of $Y_0$ map $\{0, 4\}$ onto all $16$ transversal pairs $\{i, j + 4\}$ ($0 \le i, j \le 3$), so $Y_0 \cap Y_1 = 1$ and $X = Y_0 Y_1$. Since $|X'' Y_0| = \frac{144 \cdot 16}{2} = 1152 = |X|$ and $|X'' Y_1| = \frac{144 \cdot 72}{9} = 1152 = |X|$, we have $X'' Y_0 = X'' Y_1 = X$ (hence $X' Y_0 = X' Y_1 = X$). Also $\operatorname{Core}_X(Y_1) = 1$ (faithful action on the $16$ pairs) and $\operatorname{Core}_X(Y_0) = 1$ (as $Y_0 \cap V_{16} = \langle \alpha^4 \rangle$ is diagonal and not $(S_4 \times S_4)$-normal). By `C0002` Proposition 1.1, $(\hat{G}, \hat{H}, S)$ is faithful ($|\hat{G}| = 1152 \cdot 288 = 331{,}776$, **solvable**, $|\hat{H}| = 288$), and $Q_{1152} := Q_{(X, Y_0, Y_1)}$ is non-Moufang.
2. **Analytic simplicity proof**: Suppose $P_U \trianglelefteq Q_{1152}$ is a nontrivial proper normal subloop (`C0001` Theorem 2.2), with $1 \neq N = \operatorname{Core}_{\hat{G}}(U) \trianglelefteq \hat{G}$ satisfying $|S N / N| = [\hat{G} : \hat{H} N] > 1$. Since $\sigma(u, v) = (v, u) \in \operatorname{Aut}(\hat{G})$ inverts $S$, for any $s_1, s_2 \in S$ we have $\sigma(s_1^{-1} s_2) = s_1 s_2^{-1} = s_1 (s_1^{-1} s_2)^{-1} s_1^{-1}$, so $s_1^{-1} s_2 \in N \iff s_1^{-1} s_2 \in \sigma(N)$. By `C0001` Theorem 2.2(2)–(3), $U = (S \cap N)\hat{H} = (S \cap \sigma(N))\hat{H}$, whence $N = \operatorname{Core}_{\hat{G}}(U)$ is **$\sigma$-invariant** ($\sigma(N) = N$).
   Because $C_{\hat{G}}(V_{16} \times V_{16}) = V_{16} \times V_{16}$, $N \cap (V_{16} \times V_{16}) \neq 1$. Under $X'' \times X'' = A_4^4 \le \hat{G}$, $V_{16} \times V_{16} = V_4^{(1)} \oplus V_4^{(2)} \oplus V_4^{(3)} \oplus V_4^{(4)}$ decomposes into four pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible $\mathbb{F}_2[A_4^4]$-modules, so $N \cap (V_{16} \times V_{16}) = \bigoplus_{i \in I} V_4^{(i)}$ for some nonempty $I \subseteq \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$. Conjugation by $(t, t) \in \hat{G}$ induces $(1, 2)(3, 4)$ on $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, while $\sigma(N) = N$ induces $(1, 3)(2, 4)$; since $\langle (1, 2)(3, 4), (1, 3)(2, 4) \rangle$ acts transitively on $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, we obtain $I = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, i.e. **$V_{16} \times V_{16} \le N$**:
   - If $N = V_{16} \times V_{16}$, then $(x, x^{-1}) N = (y, y^{-1}) N \iff x V_{16} = y V_{16}$, giving $|S N / N| = [X : V_{16}] = 72$. Meanwhile $|\hat{H} \cap (V_{16} \times V_{16})| = |Y_0 \cap V_{16}| \cdot |Y_1 \cap V_{16}| = 2 \cdot 1 = 2$, so $|\hat{H} N| = \frac{288 \cdot 256}{2} = 36{,}864$ and $[\hat{G} : \hat{H} N] = \frac{331{,}776}{36{,}864} = 9 < 72 = |S N / N|$, violating `C0001` Theorem 2.2(3).
   - If $N \supsetneq V_{16} \times V_{16}$, then in $\hat{G} / (V_{16} \times V_{16}) \le (S_3 \wr C_2)^2$, $\operatorname{Fit}(\hat{G} / (V_{16} \times V_{16})) = (X'' / V_{16}) \times (X'' / V_{16}) \cong C_3^4$, so $N / (V_{16} \times V_{16})$ meets $C_3^4$ nontrivially. Inside $\hat{G} / (V_{16} \times V_{16})$, elements $((\tau, \tau), (1, 1))$ and $((\tau, 1), (\tau, 1))$ ($\tau \in S_3$ a transposition) act on the four $C_3$ coordinate lines with pairwise distinct eigenvalue pairs $\{(-1, -1), (-1, +1), (+1, -1), (+1, +1)\}$, so $N / (V_{16} \times V_{16}) \cap C_3^4$ is a nonempty direct product of coordinate $C_3$ factors; by the same transitive $\langle (t, t), \sigma \rangle$-invariance, $N$ contains all of $X'' \times X''$. Since $X'' Y_0 = X'' Y_1 = X$, $(X'' \times X'')\hat{H} = \hat{G}$, contradicting $[\hat{G} : \hat{H} N] > 1$.

   Because $|Q_{1152}| = 2^7 \cdot 3^2$ has composite odd part $9$, $Q_{1152}$ lies strictly outside the prime-power semilinear family $Q_{p,q}$ ($|Q_{p,q}| = p^{p+1} q$, $q$ prime), and exhaustive search (`search_folders.py` Sweep D) confirms $Q_{1152}$ is the unique simple proper left Bol exact-factorization loop on $S_4 \wr C_2$ up to isomorphism. $\square$
