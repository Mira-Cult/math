# Proof of Claim `0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0001`

Throughout, we use the left Bol identity $x * (y * (x * z)) = (x * (y * x)) * z$, equivalently $L_x L_y L_x = L_{x * (y * x)}$ under left permutation composition $(f g)(z) = f(g(z))$.

### 1. Inverses, Moufang Equivalences, and Right-to-Left Folder Transport

#### Proposition 1.1 (Two-Sided Inverses, LIP, Moufang Equivalences, and Opposite Transport)
Let $(Q, *)$ be any left Bol loop (finite or infinite) with identity $1$.
1. **Two-sided inverses and LIP**: For each $x \in Q$, let $x^\lambda \in Q$ satisfy $x^\lambda * x = 1$. Then $L_x L_{x^\lambda} L_x = L_{x * (x^\lambda * x)} = L_x$. Cancelling the bijection $L_x$ on the right yields $L_x L_{x^\lambda} = \mathrm{id}_Q$. Evaluating at $1$ gives $x * x^\lambda = 1$, so $x^{-1} := x^\lambda$ is a unique two-sided inverse, $(x^{-1})^{-1} = x$, and $L_x^{-1} = L_{x^{-1}}$ (left inverse property, LIP).
2. **Moufang equivalences**: For a left Bol loop $Q$, the following are equivalent:
   - (i) $Q$ satisfies the right inverse property (RIP): $(z * y) * y^{-1} = z$ for all $y, z \in Q$ (equivalently $R_{y^{-1}} = R_y^{-1}$);
   - (ii) $Q$ satisfies the automorphic inverse property for anti-automorphisms (AAIP): $(x * y)^{-1} = y^{-1} * x^{-1}$ for all $x, y \in Q$;
   - (iii) $Q$ is right Bol ($R_x R_y R_x = R_{(x * y) * x}$);
   - (iv) $Q$ is Moufang ($(x * y) * (z * x) = (x * (y * z)) * x$).

   *Proof*: For (iv) $\Rightarrow$ (i), setting $z = 1$ in $(x * y) * (z * x) = (x * (y * z)) * x$ gives flexibility $(x * y) * x = x * (y * x)$; then setting $z = x^{-1}$ gives $x * y = (x * (y * x^{-1})) * x = x * ((y * x^{-1}) * x)$, cancelling $L_x$ gives $y = (y * x^{-1}) * x$, and substituting $x \leftarrow x^{-1}$ yields RIP $(y * x) * x^{-1} = y$. Next, (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) holds by left-right duality of (1). For (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii), set $z = (y * x)^{-1}$ in Left Bol: $x * (y * (x * (y * x)^{-1})) = (x * (y * x)) * (y * x)^{-1} = x$ by RIP; applying $L_x^{-1}, L_y^{-1}, L_x^{-1}$ yields $(y * x)^{-1} = x^{-1} * y^{-1}$. For (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii), the bijection $J(u) = u^{-1}$ satisfies $(J L_a J^{-1})(u) = (a * u^{-1})^{-1} = u * a^{-1} = R_{a^{-1}}(u)$, so conjugating $L_x L_y L_x = L_{x * (y * x)}$ by $J$ yields Right Bol (and hence (i)). Finally, assume (i), (ii), and (iii) hold alongside Left Bol: setting $z = x^{-1}$ in Left Bol gives $x * y = (x * (y * x)) * x^{-1}$, so by RIP $(x * y) * x = x * (y * x)$, i.e. $L_x R_x = R_x L_x$. Substituting $u = x * w$ ($w = L_x^{-1}(u)$) in Left Bol gives $(L_x R_x)(y) * L_x^{-1}(u) = L_x(y * u)$, so $\Theta_x := (L_x R_x, L_x^{-1}, L_x) \in \operatorname{Atp}(Q)$; dually, substituting $t = v * x$ ($v = R_x^{-1}(t)$) in Right Bol $((v * x) * y) * x = v * ((x * y) * x)$ gives $\Phi_x := (R_x^{-1}, L_x R_x, R_x) \in \operatorname{Atp}(Q)$. Since $L_x R_x = R_x L_x$, composing in $\operatorname{Atp}(Q)$ yields
   $$\Phi_x \circ \Theta_x = (R_x^{-1} L_x R_x,\; L_x R_x L_x^{-1},\; R_x L_x) = (L_x,\; R_x,\; L_x R_x) \in \operatorname{Atp}(Q),$$
   which evaluated on $(y, z)$ is $(x * y) * (z * x) = (x * (y * z)) * x$, proving (iv). $\square$
3. **No anti-automorphism for proper left Bol loops ($Q \not\cong Q^{\mathrm{op}}$)**: If $\psi : Q \xrightarrow{\sim} Q^{\mathrm{op}}$ were an isomorphism ($\psi(x * y) = \psi(y) * \psi(x)$), then $Q$ would also be right Bol, hence Moufang by (2).
4. **Right-to-left folder transport on the identical triple $(G, H, K)$**: Let $(G, H, K)$ be a right Bol folder ($1 \in K$, $a b a \in K$ for all $a, b \in K$, and $K$ a right transversal to all $H^g$, with right Bol product $H a b = H(a \circ_{\mathrm{R}} b)$) satisfying $K^{-1} = K$ (automatic whenever $[G : H] < \infty$). Inverting $H x^{-1} y^{-1} = H(x^{-1} \circ_{\mathrm{R}} y^{-1})$ in $G$ gives $y x H = (x^{-1} \circ_{\mathrm{R}} y^{-1})^{-1} H$, and $(x H g)^{-1} = g^{-1} H x^{-1}$ shows $K = K^{-1}$ is a left transversal to all $g^{-1} H g$. Hence $(G, H, K)$ is a left Bol folder under $y *_{\mathrm{L}} x \in K \cap y x H$, and $x \mapsto x^{-1}$ is a loop isomorphism $(K, \circ_{\mathrm{R}})^{\mathrm{op}} \xrightarrow{\sim} (K, *_{\mathrm{L}})$.

#### Lemma 1.2 (Moufang Normalizer and Primitivity Obstruction)
In any Moufang loop $(Q, *)$, holding $u, y \in Q$ fixed and varying $z \in Q$ in $(u * y) * (z * u) = (u * (y * z)) * u$ gives $L_{u * y} R_u = R_u L_u L_y$, while holding $u, z \in Q$ fixed and varying $y \in Q$ gives $R_{z * u} L_u = R_u L_u R_z$. Setting $y = L_u^{-1}(w)$ in the first identity yields $R_u^{-1} L_w R_u = L_u L_{u^{-1} * w} \in G := \operatorname{LMlt}(Q)$ (and $R_u G R_u^{-1} = R_{u^{-1}}^{-1} G R_{u^{-1}} \subseteq G$ by RIP), so $G \trianglelefteq M := \operatorname{Mlt}(Q)$. Reducing the second identity modulo $G$ gives $\overline{R_{z * u}} = \overline{R_u} \, \overline{R_z}$ in $M / G$, so $x \mapsto R_x G$ is a surjective loop homomorphism $Q \twoheadrightarrow (M / G)^{\mathrm{op}}$. Consequently, if $Q$ is **simple and nonassociative**, its kernel cannot be $\{1\}$, forcing $R_x \in G$ for all $x \in Q$; hence **$\operatorname{LMlt}(Q) = \operatorname{RMlt}(Q) = \operatorname{Mlt}(Q)$ acts primitively on $Q$** (every nontrivial normal subgroup of $\operatorname{LMlt}(Q)$ is transitive on $Q$).

### 2. Left Bol Folder Axioms, Faithful Reduction, and Isomorphism Bijection

#### Theorem 1.3 (Equivalence of Folder Axiom Systems and Automatic Finiteness)
Let $G$ be a group, $H \le G$ a subgroup, and $K \subseteq G$ a subset. The following two axiom systems are equivalent:
- **System A (Aschbacher–Nagy)**: (F1) $1 \in K$ and $\forall a, b \in K,\; a b a \in K$; (F2) $K$ is a left transversal to $g H g^{-1}$ in $G$ for every $g \in G$.
- **System B (Single-Stabilizer Form)**: (B1) $1 \in K$; (B2) $K$ is a left transversal to $H$ in $G$; (B3) $\forall a, b \in K,\; a b a \in K$; (B4) $K^{-1} = K$.

Moreover, **whenever $[G : H] < \infty$ (in particular for all finite loops), (B4) and (F2) follow automatically from (B1)–(B3)**.

*Proof*:
- **(A) $\Rightarrow$ (B)**: Assume (F1)–(F2) and define $a * b$ as the unique element of $K \cap a b H$. For $a, c \in K$, $a * b = c \iff b \in K \cap (a^{-1} c) H$ (unique by $g = 1$ in (F2)), and $x * a = c \iff x \in K \cap (c a^{-1})(a H a^{-1})$ (unique by $g = a$ in (F2)). Since $1 * a = a = a * 1$, $(K, *)$ is a loop; in particular, for each $s \in K$, $x * s = 1 \iff x \in K \cap s^{-1}(s H s^{-1})$ gives a unique left inverse $s' \in K$ ($s' * s = 1$). For $a, b \in K$, $b * a \in b a H \implies a(b * a) \in a b a H$; since $a b a \in K$, coset-representative uniqueness gives $a * (b * a) = a b a$ in $G$. Thus for all $c \in K$, $a * (b * (a * c)) \in K \cap a b a c H = \{(a b a) * c\} = \{(a * (b * a)) * c\}$, so $(K, *)$ is a left Bol loop. By Proposition 1.1(1), $s = s * (s' * s) = s s' s$ in $G$, whence $s' = s^{-1} \in K$, proving (B4).
- **(B) $\Rightarrow$ (A)**: Assume (B1)–(B4). Given $g \in G$, write $g = s h$ ($s \in K, h \in H$) by (B2), so $g H g^{-1} = s H s^{-1}$. By (B3)–(B4), $u \mapsto s u s$ is a bijection $K \xrightarrow{\sim} K$ with inverse $v \mapsto s^{-1} v s^{-1}$. For any $x \in G$ and $u \in K$, $u \in x(s H s^{-1}) \iff u = x s h_0 s^{-1}$ ($\exists h_0 \in H$) $\iff s u s = (s x s) h_0 \in (s x s) H$; by (B2) there is a unique $v \in K \cap (s x s) H$, hence a unique $u = s^{-1} v s^{-1} \in K \cap x(g H g^{-1})$, proving (F2).
- **Automatic finiteness when $[G : H] < \infty$**: Assume (B1)–(B3) and $|K| = [G : H] < \infty$. For any $a \in K$, $a^0 = 1 \in K$, $a^1 = a \in K$, and inductively $a^{n+2} = a a^n a \in K$ ($\forall n \ge 0$). Since $\{a^n : n \ge 0\} \subseteq K$ is finite, $\operatorname{ord}_G(a) = m < \infty$, so $a^{-1} = a^{m-1} \in K$, giving (B4) and hence (F2). $\square$

#### Theorem 1.4 (Canonical Faithful Folder, Isomorphism Bijection, and 2-Variable Moufang Criterion)
1. **Faithful reduction**: For any left Bol folder $(G, H, K)$ defining $Q = (K, *)$, let $G_0 := \langle K \rangle$, $H_0 := H \cap G_0$, and $N_0 := \operatorname{Core}_{G_0}(H_0) \trianglelefteq G_0$. Since $K \subseteq G_0$ is a left transversal to $H$ in $G$, $G_0 H = G$ and $K$ is a left transversal to $H_0$ in $G_0$ carrying the identical loop operation $a * b \in K \cap a b H_0$. Because $N_0 \le H_0$ is normal in $G_0$, $k_1 N_0 = k_2 N_0$ ($k_1, k_2 \in K$) implies $k_1^{-1} k_2 \in N_0 \le H_0 \implies k_1 = k_2$, so $k \mapsto k N_0$ is a loop isomorphism onto $(\bar{G}, \bar{H}, \bar{K}) := (G_0/N_0, H_0/N_0, K N_0/N_0)$, which is a **faithful left Bol folder** ($\bar{G} = \langle \bar{K} \rangle$, $\operatorname{Core}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{H}) = 1$) canonically isomorphic via $k N_0 \mapsto L_k$ to $\mathcal{F}(Q) := (\operatorname{LMlt}(Q), \operatorname{LInn}(Q), \{L_x : x \in Q\})$.
2. **Exact isomorphism bijection**: Let $\mathcal{F}_i = (G_i, H_i, K_i)$ ($i = 1, 2$) be faithful left Bol folders with associated left Bol loops $Q_i = (K_i, *_i)$. Define a *folder isomorphism* $\Phi : \mathcal{F}_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{F}_2$ to be a group isomorphism $\Phi : G_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} G_2$ with $\Phi(H_1) = H_2$ and $\Phi(K_1) = K_2$. Then restriction $\Phi \mapsto \Phi|_{K_1}$ is a **bijection**:
   $$\operatorname{Iso}_{\mathbf{BolFld}}(\mathcal{F}_1, \mathcal{F}_2) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Iso}_{\mathbf{Loop}}(Q_1, Q_2).$$
   In particular, $\operatorname{Aut}(Q) \cong N_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(H) \cap \operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(K)$, and isomorphism classes of left Bol loops with $\operatorname{LMlt}(Q) \cong G$ are in bijection with $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$-orbits of admissible pairs $(H, K)$ satisfying $G = \langle K \rangle$ and $\operatorname{Core}_G(H) = 1$.

   *Proof*: If $\Phi : \mathcal{F}_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{F}_2$ is a folder isomorphism, $a *_1 b \in K_1 \cap a b H_1 \implies \Phi(a *_1 b) \in K_2 \cap \Phi(a)\Phi(b)H_2 = \{\Phi(a) *_2 \Phi(b)\}$, so $\Phi|_{K_1} \in \operatorname{Iso}_{\mathbf{Loop}}(Q_1, Q_2)$. Injectivity holds because $G_1 = \langle K_1 \rangle$. For surjectivity, identify $\mathcal{F}_i \cong \mathcal{F}(Q_i)$ by (1); given $\varphi \in \operatorname{Iso}_{\mathbf{Loop}}(Q_1, Q_2)$, conjugation $\Psi_\varphi(\pi) := \varphi \circ \pi \circ \varphi^{-1} : \operatorname{Sym}(Q_1) \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{Sym}(Q_2)$ satisfies $\Psi_\varphi(L_x^{(1)})(u) = \varphi(x *_1 \varphi^{-1}(u)) = \varphi(x) *_2 u = L_{\varphi(x)}^{(2)}(u)$, so $\Psi_\varphi$ maps $K_1 \to K_2$, $G_1 = \langle K_1 \rangle \to \langle K_2 \rangle = G_2$, and $H_1 = (G_1)_{1_{Q_1}} \to (G_2)_{1_{Q_2}} = H_2$, lifting $\varphi$. $\square$
3. **2-variable folder criterion for Moufang**: In a left Bol folder $(G, H, K)$, $(K, *)$ is Moufang iff $\forall x, y \in K$, the unique $z \in K \cap x y H$ also lies in $H x y$ (equivalently $|K \cap x y H \cap H x y| = 1$).

   *Proof*: By Proposition 1.1(2), $(K, *)$ is Moufang iff $(x * y)^{-1} = y^{-1} * x^{-1}$ ($\forall x, y \in K$). Writing $z = x * y \in K \cap x y H$, we have $z^{-1} \in K$ and $y^{-1} * x^{-1} \in K \cap y^{-1} x^{-1} H$, so $z^{-1} = y^{-1} * x^{-1} \iff z^{-1} \in y^{-1} x^{-1} H \iff z \in (y^{-1} x^{-1} H)^{-1} = H x y$. $\square$

### 3. Nuclei Structure and Bounds in Simple Proper Left Bol Loops

#### Theorem 1.5 ($N_\lambda(Q) = N_\mu(Q) \trianglelefteq Q$ and Folder Structure of $N_\rho(Q)$)
Let $(Q, *)$ be any left Bol loop with faithful folder $(G, H, K) = (\operatorname{LMlt}(Q), \operatorname{LInn}(Q), \{L_x : x \in Q\})$.
1. **Left and middle nuclei coincide and form a normal subloop**: For every $x \in Q$, setting $w = x * z$ in $x * (y * (x * z)) = (x * (y * x)) * z$ gives $(L_x R_x)(y) * L_x^{-1}(w) = L_x(y * w)$, so $\Theta_x := (L_x R_x, L_x^{-1}, L_x) \in \operatorname{Atp}(Q)$. For any $a \in N_\lambda(Q)$, $(L_a, \mathrm{id}_Q, L_a) \in \operatorname{Atp}(Q)$, and conjugating in $\operatorname{Atp}(Q)$ gives
   $$\Theta_x (L_a, \mathrm{id}_Q, L_a) \Theta_x^{-1} = (\psi, \mathrm{id}_Q, L_x L_a L_x^{-1}) \in \operatorname{Atp}(Q).$$
   Evaluating $\psi(u) * v = (L_x L_a L_x^{-1})(u * v)$ at $v = 1$ then $u = 1$ forces $\psi = L_x L_a L_x^{-1} = L_c$ with $c = x * (a * x^{-1}) \in N_\lambda(Q)$. Thus $L_x L_a = L_c L_x$, giving both $x * a = c * x$ (at $1$) and $x * (a * z) = c * (x * z) = (c * x) * z = (x * a) * z$ ($\forall x, z \in Q$), so $N_\lambda(Q) \subseteq N_\mu(Q)$. Conversely, if $m \in N_\mu(Q)$, then $((m * y) * m) * z = (m * y) * (m * z)$ and $(m * y) * m = m * (y * m)$, so Left Bol $(m * (y * m)) * z = m * (y * (m * z))$ yields $(m * y) * w = m * (y * w)$ for all $y \in Q$ and $w = m * z \in Q$, proving $N_\lambda(Q) = N_\mu(Q)$. With $N := N_\lambda(Q) = N_\mu(Q)$, $x * a = c * x$ gives $x * N = N * x$ ($\forall x \in Q$), whence $(N * x) * y = N * (x * y)$ and $x * (y * N) = x * (N * y) = (x * N) * y = (N * x) * y = N * (x * y) = (x * y) * N$. Thus **$N_\lambda(Q) = N_\mu(Q) \trianglelefteq Q$ unconditionally**, and if $Q$ is simple and nonassociative ($N_\lambda(Q) \neq Q$), **$N_\lambda(Q) = N_\mu(Q) = \{1\}$**.
2. **Right nucleus $N_\rho(Q)$ and divisor bound**: Under $Q \leftrightarrow G/H$ ($x \leftrightarrow L_x H$),
   $$a \in N_\rho(Q) \iff \forall x, y \in Q,\; L_{x * y}(a) = L_x(L_y(a)) \iff a \in \operatorname{Fix}_Q(H) \iff L_a^{-1} H L_a = H \iff L_a \in K \cap N_G(H),$$
   and $a \mapsto L_a H$ is a group isomorphism $(N_\rho(Q), *) \xrightarrow{\sim} N_G(H) / H$. In particular, for finite $Q$, $|N_\rho(Q)| = [N_G(H) : H]$ divides $[G : H] = |Q|$. If $Q$ is finite, simple, and nonassociative ($|Q| > 2$), no proper normal subgroup $M \lneq G$ can contain $H$ (else $\operatorname{Core}_G(M) = M$, $H M = M$, and $k_1^{-1} k_2 \in M \iff k_1 M = k_2 M$, so Theorem 2.2 below gives a proper nontrivial normal subloop $K \cap M \trianglelefteq Q$ of index $[G : M] > 1$). Hence $[G : N_G(H)] \notin \{1, 2\}$ (index $1$ or $2$ makes $N_G(H) \trianglelefteq G$ with $H \le N_G(H) \lneq G$ since $H \neq 1 = \operatorname{Core}_G(H)$), so **$|N_\rho(Q)| \mid |Q|$ and $|N_\rho(Q)| \le |Q| / 3$**.

### 4. Exact Simplicity and Normal-Subloop Criterion in $(G, H, K)$

#### Proposition 2.1 (Loop Simplicity $\iff$ Primitivity of $\operatorname{Mlt}(Q)$)
Let $Q$ be a loop with $|Q| \ge 2$ and $M = \operatorname{Mlt}(Q) = \langle L_x, R_x : x \in Q \rangle$. Loop congruences $\sim$ on $Q$ (bijecting with normal subloops $P \trianglelefteq Q$ via $P = [1]_\sim$ and $x \sim y \iff L_x^{-1}(y) \in P$) coincide with $M$-invariant equivalence relations (block systems of $M$): if $\sim$ is $M$-invariant and $x_1 \sim x_2, y_1 \sim y_2$, then $x_1 * y_1 = R_{y_1}(x_1) \sim R_{y_1}(x_2) = L_{x_2}(y_1) \sim L_{x_2}(y_2) = x_2 * y_2$, and for $u_i = L_{x_i}^{-1}(z_i)$ with $z_1 \sim z_2$, $L_{x_2}(u_1) = R_{u_1}(x_2) \sim R_{u_1}(x_1) = z_1 \sim z_2 = L_{x_2}(u_2) \implies u_1 \sim u_2$ (and symmetrically for $R_{y_i}^{-1}$). Since $|Q| \ge 2$ makes $M$ transitive on $Q$, **$Q$ is simple $\iff \operatorname{Mlt}(Q)$ acts primitively on $Q$**. If $Q$ is finite, simple, and nonassociative, then $Z(Q) = 1$, $\operatorname{Inn}(Q) = M_1 \neq 1$, and $\operatorname{Mlt}(Q)$ is nonsolvable by Vesanen's Theorem (1996, Theorem 1).

#### Theorem 2.2 (Exact Normal-Subloop Correspondence in a Faithful Left Bol Folder)
Let $(G, H, K)$ be a faithful left Bol folder for a loop $Q = (K, *)$. Under $K \xrightarrow{\sim} G/H$ ($k \mapsto k H$), $G$-block systems $\sim_U$ correspond bijectively to intermediate subgroups $H \le U \le G$ ($k_1 \sim_U k_2 \iff k_1^{-1} k_2 \in U$) with base block $P_U := K \cap U$ ($U = P_U H$). The following are equivalent:
1. $P_U \trianglelefteq Q$ is a normal subloop of $Q$ (equivalently, $\sim_U$ is a block system of $\operatorname{Mlt}(Q)$);
2. For all $k_1, k_2 \in K$, $k_1^{-1} k_2 \in U \implies k_1^{-1} k_2 \in \operatorname{Core}_G(U)$;
3. Setting $N := \operatorname{Core}_G(U) \trianglelefteq G$, we have $U = H N$ **and** $K N / N$ is a left transversal to $H N / N$ in $G / N$ (for $[G : H N] < \infty$, equivalently **$|K N / N| = [G : H N]$**).

When (1)–(3) hold, $(G/N, H N/N, K N/N)$ is directly the **faithful** left Bol folder of $Q / P_U$.

*Proof*:
- **(1) $\Rightarrow$ (2)**: Let $\pi : Q \twoheadrightarrow \bar{Q} := Q / P_U$ be the quotient homomorphism. Each $a \in K$ acts on $\bar{Q}$ via $\pi(a * x) = \pi(a) \bar{*} \pi(x) = L_{\pi(a)}(\pi(x))$, inducing a surjective homomorphism $\Psi : G = \langle K \rangle \twoheadrightarrow \operatorname{LMlt}(\bar{Q})$ with $\Psi(a) = L_{\pi(a)}$. Now $g \in \ker \Psi \iff \forall x \in K,\; \pi(g \cdot_Q x) = \pi(x) \iff g$ fixes every $\sim_U$-block setwise $\iff g \in \bigcap_{x \in G} x U x^{-1} = \operatorname{Core}_G(U) = N$. For $k_1, k_2 \in K$, $k_1^{-1} k_2 \in U \iff \pi(k_1) = \pi(k_2) \iff L_{\pi(k_1)} = L_{\pi(k_2)} \iff \Psi(k_1) = \Psi(k_2) \iff k_1^{-1} k_2 \in \ker \Psi = N$.
- **(2) $\Rightarrow$ (3)**: For $u \in U$, write $u = k h$ ($k \in K, h \in H$); then $1^{-1} k = u h^{-1} \in U \implies k \in N$ by (2), so $u \in H N$ and $U = H N$. Since $G = K H$, $K N / N$ meets every left coset of $H N / N$ in $G / N$; if $k_1 N \in (k_2 N)(H N / N)$ ($k_1, k_2 \in K$), then $k_2^{-1} k_1 \in H N = U \implies k_2^{-1} k_1 \in N$ by (2), so $k_1 N = k_2 N$.
- **(3) $\Rightarrow$ (1) + Faithful quotient**: Assume $U = H N$ ($N = \operatorname{Core}_G(U)$) and $K N / N$ is a left transversal to $H N / N$ in $G / N$. Since $(G, H, K)$ satisfies System B (Theorem 1.3), $(\bar{G}, \bar{H}, \bar{K}) := (G/N, H N/N, K N/N)$ satisfies (B1)–(B4) (as $\bar{K}^{-1} = \bar{K}$ and $\bar{a}\bar{b}\bar{a} \in \bar{K}$), hence is a left Bol folder, faithful since $\langle \bar{K} \rangle = \bar{G}$ and $\operatorname{Core}_{G/N}(U/N) = \operatorname{Core}_G(U)/N = 1$. Moreover, $a * b \in K \cap a b H \implies (a * b)N \in K N \cap a b H N$, so $k \mapsto k N$ is a surjective loop homomorphism $(K, *) \twoheadrightarrow (\bar{K}, \bar{*})$ with kernel $\{k \in K : k N = N\} = K \cap N = K \cap H N = P_U$. $\square$

#### Remark 2.3 (Explicit Counterexample Refuting the Naive Converse of Nagy `N07` Lemma 2.2)
Nagy `N07` Lemma 2.2 shows that if $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(G)$ inverts $K$ ($k^\sigma = k^{-1}$ for all $k \in K$) and every nontrivial $\sigma$-invariant normal subgroup $N \trianglelefteq G$ acts transitively on $G / H$ ($[G : H N] = 1$), then $Q$ is simple. The converse is **false** even for solvable $G$: in $Q_{24} = Q_{(S_4, C_4, S_3)}$ (`N07` §4 Example II), $G = \{(u, v) \in S_4 \times S_4 : u v \in A_4\}$ ($|G| = 288$), $H = (C_4 \times S_3) \cap G$ ($|H| = 12$), $K = \{(x, x^{-1}) : x \in S_4\}$, and $\sigma(u, v) = (v, u)$ inverts $K$. The normal subgroup $N = V_4 \times V_4 \trianglelefteq G$ ($|N| = 16$, where $V_4 = O_2(S_4)$) is **nontrivial, $\sigma$-invariant, and intransitive** ($|H N| = \frac{12 \cdot 16}{2} = 96 < 288$, $[G : H N] = 3$), yet $|K N / N| = |S_4 / V_4| = 6 > 3 = [G : H N]$, so $P_{H N}$ fails Theorem 2.2(3) and $Q_{24}$ is simple. (Similarly, in $N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}}$, $N = J_0 \trianglelefteq G$ has $[G : H N] = 6$ while $|K N / N| = 21 > 6$.)
