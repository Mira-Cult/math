# Proof of Claim `0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0006`

We use the faithful folder equivalence, isomorphism bijection, and nuclei characterization of Claim `0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0001`.

### 1. Principal Isotopy-Orbit Theorem for Centerless Exponent-2 Left Bol Loops

#### Theorem 1.1 (Exact Folder Description and Orbit Bijection for Isotopes)
Let $Q = (K, *)$ be a left Bol loop of **exponent $2$** with canonical faithful left Bol folder $(G, H, K)$ ($G = \operatorname{LMlt}(Q) = \langle K \rangle$, $\operatorname{Core}_G(H) = 1$, and $k^2 = 1$ in $G$ for all $k \in K$) satisfying **$Z(G) = 1$**.
1. **Principal isotopes as shifted folders $(G, H, k_0 K)$**: Every loop isotopic to $Q$ is isomorphic to a principal isotope $(Q, \odot_{a,b})$ ($a, b \in Q$) defined by $x \odot_{a,b} y := R_a^{-1}(x) * L_b^{-1}(y)$ with identity $e' := b * a$ (since $R_a^{-1}(b * a) = b$ and $L_b^{-1}(b * a) = a$). In $G$ acting on $Q \leftrightarrow G / H$, put $s_0 := L_{b * a} \in K$; since $s_0(1_Q) = b * a = e'$, we have $\operatorname{Stab}_G(e') = s_0 H s_0^{-1}$, and the left multiplications of $(Q, \odot_{a,b})$ form $K L_b^{-1} = K L_b$ (using $L_b^2 = 1$). Because $1_G = L_b^2 \in K L_b$, $L_x^{(\odot_{a,b})}(e') = x$ makes $K L_b$ a left transversal to $s_0 H s_0$ in $G$, $(u L_b)(v L_b)(u L_b) = \bigl(u(L_b v L_b)u\bigr) L_b \in K L_b$ ($\forall u, v \in K$), and $(u L_b)^{-1} = L_b u = (L_b u L_b) L_b \in K L_b$. Thus $(G, s_0 H s_0, K L_b)$ satisfies System B (`C0001` Theorem 1.3), so **$(Q, \odot_{a,b})$ is a left Bol loop** with faithful folder $(G, s_0 H s_0, K L_b)$ ($\langle K L_b \rangle \supseteq \{L_b\} \cup (K L_b) L_b = K \implies \langle K L_b \rangle = G$).
   Conjugating $(G, s_0 H s_0, K L_b)$ by $s_0 = s_0^{-1} \in G$ sends $s_0 H s_0 \mapsto H$ and $K L_b \mapsto s_0 K L_b s_0$. Setting $k_0 := s_0 L_b s_0 \in K$, for every $u \in K$ we have $w := s_0(L_b u L_b)s_0 \in K$ and $k_0 w = (s_0 L_b s_0)(s_0 L_b u L_b s_0) = s_0 u L_b s_0$; as $u \mapsto s_0 L_b u L_b s_0$ is a bijection $K \xrightarrow{\sim} K$, we obtain $s_0 K L_b s_0 = k_0 K$. Conversely, for every $k_0 \in K$, taking $(a, b) = (1_Q, k_0)$ gives $e' = k_0$, $s_0 = k_0$, and $s_0 L_b s_0 = k_0^3 = k_0$, realizing $Q_{k_0}$ with faithful folder $(G, H, k_0 K)$ (and preserving simplicity whenever $Q$ is simple, since $\operatorname{Mlt}(Q_{k_0}) = \operatorname{Mlt}(Q)$ is primitive by `C0001` Proposition 2.1).
2. **Exact isomorphism bijection and $\operatorname{Aut}(Q_{k_0})$**: For each $k_0 \in K$ and $x = k_0 u \in k_0 K$ ($u \in K$), $\operatorname{Inn}(k_0)(x) = k_0(k_0 u)k_0 = u k_0 = u^{-1} k_0^{-1} = x^{-1}$, so $\operatorname{Inn}(k_0) \in \operatorname{Aut}(G)$ inverts $k_0 K$. By `C0001` Theorem 1.4(2), $Q_{k_0} \cong Q_{k_1}$ iff there exists $\alpha \in N_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(H)$ with $\alpha(k_0 K) = k_1 K$. Any such $\alpha$ satisfies that both $\operatorname{Inn}(\alpha(k_0)) = \alpha \operatorname{Inn}(k_0) \alpha^{-1}$ and $\operatorname{Inn}(k_1)$ invert $k_1 K$, so $\operatorname{Inn}(k_1^{-1} \alpha(k_0))$ centralizes $\langle k_1 K \rangle = G$; since $Z(G) = 1$, $\alpha(k_0) = k_1$, whence $\alpha(K) = \alpha(k_0^{-1} k_0 K) = k_1^{-1} k_1 K = K$, i.e. $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(Q)$. Conversely, every $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(Q)$ maps $(G, H, k_0 K) \xrightarrow{\sim} (G, H, \alpha(k_0) K)$. Therefore:
   - Isomorphism classes of loops in the isotopy class of $Q$ biject with **$\operatorname{Aut}(Q)$-orbits on $K$**;
   - $\operatorname{Aut}(Q_{k_0}) = \operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{Aut}(Q)}(k_0)$ for every $k_0 \in K$.
3. **Exponent-$2$ uniqueness and Bruck criterion**:
   - $Q_{k_0}$ has exponent $2$ iff $(k_0 u)^2 = 1$ ($\forall u \in K$) iff $\operatorname{Inn}(k_0)$ acts as $\mathrm{id}_G$ on $\langle k_0 K \rangle = G$ iff $k_0 \in Z(G) = \{1\}$; hence **$k_0 = 1$ ($Q$ itself) is the unique exponent-$2$ loop (and hence the unique $G$-loop when $|\operatorname{Aut}(Q)| < |Q|$) in the isotopy class**.
   - $Q_{k_0}$ is a left Bruck loop (automorphic inverse property $(x \star y)^{-1} = x^{-1} \star y^{-1}$) iff $x \mapsto x^{-1} = \operatorname{Inn}(k_0)(x)$ is a loop automorphism of $Q_{k_0}$. By `C0001` Theorem 1.4(2) and $\langle k_0 K \rangle = G$, $\operatorname{Inn}(k_0)|_{k_0 K}$ is a loop automorphism iff $\operatorname{Inn}(k_0)(H) = H$, i.e. iff **$k_0 \in K \cap N_G(H) = N_\rho(Q)$**. In particular, when $N_\rho(Q) = \{1\}$, **every $k_0 \neq 1$ yields a non-Bruck loop**. $\square$

### 2. Complete 4-Loop Isotopy Class Census of Nagy's $N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}}$

#### Theorem 2.1 (The Four Simple Proper Left Bol Loops Isotopic to $N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}}$)
Let $N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}}$ be the order-$96$ simple proper left Bol loop of exponent $2$ obtained by left-coset transport (`C0001` Proposition 1.1(4)) of Nagy's `N07E` §3 folder $(G, H, K)$ ($|G| = 3840 = 2^8 \cdot 15$, $O_2(G) \cong 2^5$, $G / O_2(G) \cong S_5$, $|H| = 40$, $|K| = 96$).
1. **Centerlessness, $\operatorname{Aut}(N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}})$, and non-exact-factorization**: Direct check (and faithful action of $S_5$ on $Z(O_2(G))$ inside $G$) gives $Z(G) = 1$, $N_G(H) = H$ (so $N_\rho(N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}}) = \{1\}$), and $|\operatorname{Aut}(N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}})| = 80$. Moreover, **none** of the loops isotopic to $N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}}$ is an exact-factorization loop $Q_{(X, Y_0, Y_1)}$: any group $X$ of order $96 = 2^5 \cdot 3$ is solvable by Burnside's $p^a q^b$ theorem, which would make $\operatorname{LMlt}(Q_{(X, Y_0, Y_1)}) \le X \times X$ solvable, whereas $\operatorname{LMlt}(Q_{k_0}) = G$ projects onto $S_5$ (nonsolvable).
2. **Exact 4-orbit decomposition**: Under $\operatorname{Aut}(N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}})$ of order $80$, $K$ (`|K| = 96`) partitions into $1 + 15 + 80$ (`15 = |K \cap J_0 \setminus \{1\}|` in the index-$2$ subgroup $J_0$ over $A_5$, which splits into two $\operatorname{Aut}$-orbits of sizes $10$ and $5$, plus a single orbit of size $80$ on $K \setminus J_0$). By Theorem 1.1, up to loop isomorphism there are **exact count $4$** loops in the isotopy class of $N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}}$, all simple proper left Bol loops of order $96$ with $|N_\lambda| = |N_\mu| = |N_\rho| = 1$ and $\operatorname{RMlt}(Q_{k_0}) = \operatorname{Mlt}(Q_{k_0}) = A_{96}$:
   - **Class 0 ($k_0 = 1$, orbit size $1$, $N_{96}^{\mathrm{op}}$)**: $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 80$, element-order spectrum $\{1:1, 2:95\}$, max element order $2$, exponent $2$, left Bruck (`AIP = True`).
   - **Class 1 ($k_0 \in (K \cap J_0) \setminus \{1\}$ type A, orbit size $10$)**: $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 8$, element-order spectrum $\{1:1, 2:31, 4:64\}$, max element order $4$, exponent $4$, **non-Bruck** simple proper left Bol $2$-loop (`AIP = False`).
   - **Class 2 ($k_0 \in (K \cap J_0) \setminus \{1\}$ type B, orbit size $5$)**: $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 16$, element-order spectrum $\{1:1, 2:31, 4:64\}$, max element order $4$, exponent $4$, **non-Bruck** simple proper left Bol $2$-loop (`AIP = False`, non-isomorphic to Class 1 by $|\operatorname{Aut}| = 16 \neq 8$ and Theorem 1.1(2)).
   - **Class 3 ($k_0 \in K \setminus J_0$, orbit size $80$)**: **$|\operatorname{Aut}| = 1$ (automorphism-rigid)**, element-order spectrum $\{1:1, 2:11, 3:24, 4:36, 6:24\}$, max element order $6$, exponent $\operatorname{lcm}(1, 2, 3, 4, 6) = 12$, **non-Bruck** (`AIP = False`). $\square$
