We apply the exact-factorization framework of Claim `0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0002` and the folder isomorphism and normal-subloop theorems of Claim `0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0001`.

### 1. Universal Modular Regular-Subgroup Criterion in Affine Semidirect Products

#### Theorem 1.1 (Cauchy–Hyperplane Regular-Subgroup Criterion)
Let $p$ be a prime, let $V \cong \mathbb{F}_p^d$ have dimension $d \ge 2$, and let $G_0 \le \operatorname{GL}(V)$ be any subgroup, with semidirect product $X := V \rtimes G_0$ and natural projection $\pi : X \twoheadrightarrow G_0$. Then $X$ admits a non-translation regular subgroup $Y_0 \neq V$ on $V$ (equivalently, $|Y_0| = p^d$, $Y_0 \cap G_0 = 1$, and $Y_0 \neq V$, giving an exact factorization $X = Y_0 G_0$) **if and only if $p \mid |G_0|$**.

*Proof*:
- **($\Rightarrow$)**: If $Y_0 \le X$ has $|Y_0| = p^d$ and $Y_0 \neq V$, then $\pi(Y_0) \le G_0$ is a $p$-subgroup; if $\pi(Y_0) = 1$, then $Y_0 \le \ker(\pi) = V$ of order $p^d$, forcing $Y_0 = V$. Hence $\pi(Y_0) \neq 1$ and $p \mid |G_0|$.
- **($\Leftarrow$)**: Assume $p \mid |G_0|$, and by Cauchy's Theorem choose $u \in G_0$ of order $p$. Over $\mathbb{F}_p$, $(u - I_V)^p = u^p - I_V = 0$, so $\ker(u - I_V) \neq 0$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} \operatorname{im}(u - I_V) \le d - 1$. Choose any $\mathbb{F}_p$-hyperplane $V_0 \subset V$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} V_0 = d - 1 \ge 1$) containing $\operatorname{im}(u - I_V)$, and pick $v_0 \in V \setminus V_0$. Put $W_0 := V_0 \times \{I_V\} \le X$ and $g := (v_0, u) \in X$. For every $w \in V_0$, $u(w) - w \in \operatorname{im}(u - I_V) \subseteq V_0$, so $g$ normalizes $W_0$. Because $p - 1 \ge 1$,
  $$g^p = \Bigl(\sum_{r=0}^{p-1} u^r(v_0),\; u^p\Bigr) = \bigl((u - I_V)^{p-1}(v_0),\; I_V\bigr) \in W_0.$$
  Since $\operatorname{ord}(\pi(g)) = p$, $Y_0 := \langle W_0, g \rangle = \bigsqcup_{k=0}^{p-1} W_0 g^k$ is a subgroup of order $p^{d-1} \cdot p = p^d$ with $\pi(Y_0) = \langle u \rangle \cong C_p$ ($Y_0 \neq V$). For $0 \le k < p$, $g^k(0) = \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} u^r(v_0) = k v_0 + w_k$ with $w_k := \sum_{r=0}^{k-1}(u^r(v_0) - v_0) \in V_0$, so for every $w \in V_0$,
  $$(w, I_V) g^k(0) = k v_0 + (w + w_k).$$
  As $(k, w)$ ranges over $\{0, \dots, p-1\} \times V_0$, $k v_0 + (w + w_k)$ hits every element of $V = \bigsqcup_{k=0}^{p-1}(k v_0 + V_0)$ uniquely. Hence $Y_0$ acts regularly on $V$, $Y_0 \cap G_0 = \operatorname{Stab}_{Y_0}(0) = 1$, and $X = Y_0 G_0$. $\square$

*(Remark on literature scope: Pellegrini and Tamburini Bellani, *Comm. Algebra* **45** (2017), 3032–3040, study *translation-free* regular subgroups satisfying $Y_0 \cap V = 1$; core-free exact factorizations of $V \rtimes G_0$ require only $\operatorname{Core}_X(Y_0) = 1$, which holds automatically for irreducible $G_0$ whenever $Y_0 \neq V$, including $1 < Y_0 \cap V < V$.)*

### 2. Simplicity, Isomorphism Criterion, and Infinite Subfamilies for Quasisimple Irreducible $G_0$

#### Theorem 2.1 (Affine-Simple and Quasisimple Exact-Factorization Theorem)
Let $V \cong \mathbb{F}_p^d$ ($d \ge 2$), let $G_0 \le \operatorname{GL}(V)$ be a **faithful irreducible quasisimple** subgroup ($G_0 = G_0'$ and $G_0 / Z(G_0)$ nonabelian simple) with $p \mid |G_0|$, and let $Y_0 \le X := V \rtimes G_0$ be any non-translation regular subgroup ($|Y_0| = p^d$, $Y_0 \cap G_0 = 1$, $Y_0 \neq V$).
1. **Faithfulness, properness, and nuclei**: Since $V$ is faithful and irreducible for $G_0$ with $d \ge 2$, $C_V(G_0) = 0$, $[V, G_0] = V$, and $C_X(V) = V$ (hence $Z(X) = 1$). Together with $G_0 = G_0'$, this gives $X' = X$, so the reduced folder (`C0002` Proposition 1.1) is $(\hat{G}, \hat{H}, S) = (X \times X, Y_0 \times G_0, S)$ of order $p^{2d} |G_0|^2$. Moreover $\operatorname{Core}_X(G_0) = C_{G_0}(V) = 1$, and $\operatorname{Core}_X(Y_0) \cap V \le Y_0 \cap V \subsetneq V$ is a proper $G_0$-submodule of $V$, hence $0$, so $\operatorname{Core}_X(Y_0) \le C_X(V) = V$ forces $\operatorname{Core}_X(Y_0) = 1$. Thus $(\hat{G}, \hat{H}, S)$ is faithful, and $N_\rho(Q_{(X, Y_0, G_0)}) = N_X(Y_0) \cap N_X(G_0) = N_{G_0}(Y_0)$ (as $N_X(G_0) = C_V(G_0) \rtimes G_0 = G_0$).
2. **Simplicity (including $Z(G_0) \neq 1$)**: Note first that $p \nmid |Z(G_0)|$: if $z \in Z(G_0)$ had order $p$, then $0 \neq \ker(z - I_V)$ would be a nonzero $G_0$-submodule of $V$, forcing $\ker(z - I_V) = V$ and $z = I_V$. Let $1 \neq N \trianglelefteq X \times X$. Because $C_{X \times X}(V \times V) = V \times V$ and $V \times 1, 1 \times V$ are non-isomorphic irreducible $\mathbb{F}_p[G_0 \times G_0]$-modules, $N \cap (V \times V) \in \{V \times 1, 1 \times V, V \times V\}$:
   - If $N \cap (V \times V) = V \times 1$, then $[N, 1 \times V] \le N \cap (1 \times V) = 1$. Thus the second projection of $N$ centralizes $V$ and lies in $C_X(V)=V$. Consequently $[N,1 \times X] \le N \cap (1 \times V)=1$, so $N \le C_{X \times X}(1 \times X) = X \times 1$ (since $Z(X) = 1$); hence $|S N / N| = |X| > [\hat{G} : \hat{H} N]$ by $\operatorname{Core}_{\hat{G}}(\hat{H}) = 1$. Symmetrically if $N \cap (V \times V) = 1 \times V$.
   - Suppose $V \times V \le N$, and put $\bar{N} := N / (V \times V) \trianglelefteq G_0 \times G_0$. Since $G_0$ is quasisimple, a normal subgroup of $G_0 \times G_0$ either contains $G_0 \times 1$ or $1 \times G_0$, or is contained in $Z(G_0) \times Z(G_0)$. Indeed, if a projection is noncentral, commutators with that direct factor contain the perfect group $G_0$. If $G_0 \times 1 \le \bar{N}$, then $X \times V \le N$ and $\hat HN=\hat G$.
   - If instead $1 \times G_0 \le \bar{N}$, then $\bar N=L\times G_0$ for $L\trianglelefteq G_0$. If $L=G_0$, $\hat HN=\hat G$. Otherwise $L\le Z(G_0)$ and $N=(V\rtimes L)\times X$. Put $P_0=\pi(Y_0)$; it is a nontrivial $p$-subgroup, while $p\nmid |L|$, so $P_0\cap L=1$. Hence
     $$|SN/N|=|G_0:L|=|P_0|\,[\hat G:\hat HN]>[\hat G:\hat HN],$$
     since $\hat HN=(V\rtimes P_0L)\times X$. The first equality follows by projecting $S$ to the first coordinate modulo $V\rtimes L$.
   - In the central case $N = (V \times V) \rtimes D$ ($D \le Z(G_0) \times Z(G_0)$), let $P_0 := \pi(Y_0) \le G_0$. Since $Y_0 \neq V$, $|P_0| \ge p$ is a $p$-power, while $|\operatorname{pr}_1(D)|$ divides $|Z(G_0)|$ (coprime to $p$), so $P_0 \cap \operatorname{pr}_1(D) = 1$. Because $(V \times V)\hat{H} = (V \rtimes P_0) \times X$, we have $\hat{H} N = \bigl(V \rtimes (P_0 \operatorname{pr}_1(D))\bigr) \times X$ of index $[\hat{G} : \hat{H} N] = \frac{|G_0|}{|P_0| \cdot |\operatorname{pr}_1(D)|}$. Meanwhile $(x, x^{-1}) N = (y, y^{-1}) N \iff (\pi(x)^{-1}\pi(y), \pi(x)\pi(y)^{-1}) \in D \iff \pi(x)^{-1}\pi(y) \in D_\Delta := \{z \in Z(G_0) : (z, z^{-1}) \in D\}$. Since $|D_\Delta| \le |\operatorname{pr}_1(D)|$,
     $$|S N / N| = \frac{|G_0|}{|D_\Delta|} \ge \frac{|G_0|}{|\operatorname{pr}_1(D)|} = |P_0| \cdot [\hat{G} : \hat{H} N] \ge p \, [\hat{G} : \hat{H} N] > [\hat{G} : \hat{H} N].$$
   By `C0001` Theorem 2.2(3), $Q_{(X, Y_0, G_0)}$ is simple. The properness conclusion of `C0002` Proposition 1.1 and the nuclei result of `C0001` Theorem 1.5 now give that it is non-Moufang and $N_\lambda=N_\mu=\{1\}$. Thus it is a **finite simple proper left Bol $G$-loop**.
3. **Exact isomorphism and automorphism theorem**: Let $X = V \rtimes G_0$ and $\widetilde{X} = \widetilde{V} \rtimes \widetilde{G}_0$ satisfy the hypotheses above with non-translation regular subgroups $Y_0, \widetilde{Y}_0$. In $\hat{G} = X \times X$, $V \times 1$ and $1 \times V$ are the unique minimal normal subgroups, and since $C_X(V) = V$ and $X = X'$, we recover the direct factors intrinsically via
   $$\bigl(C_{X \times X}(1 \times V)\bigr)' = (X \times V)' = X \times 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \bigl(C_{X \times X}(V \times 1)\bigr)' = (V \times X)' = 1 \times X.$$
   By `C0001` Theorem 1.4(2), any loop isomorphism $Q_{(X, Y_0, G_0)} \xrightarrow{\sim} Q_{(\widetilde{X}, \widetilde{Y}_0, \widetilde{G}_0)}$ lifts uniquely to a folder isomorphism $\Phi : (X \times X, Y_0 \times G_0, S) \xrightarrow{\sim} (\widetilde{X} \times \widetilde{X}, \widetilde{Y}_0 \times \widetilde{G}_0, \widetilde{S})$, which must send $\{X \times 1, 1 \times X\}$ to $\{\widetilde{X} \times 1, 1 \times \widetilde{X}\}$. Because $|Y_0| = p^d$ is a prime power whereas $|\widetilde{G}_0|$ is nonsolvable (hence never a prime power), $\Phi$ cannot swap the two factors; thus $\Phi = \alpha_1 \times \alpha_2$, and $\Phi(S) = \widetilde{S}$ forces $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 =: \alpha : X \xrightarrow{\sim} \widetilde{X}$ with $\alpha(Y_0) = \widetilde{Y}_0$ and $\alpha(G_0) = \widetilde{G}_0$.
   For $X = \widetilde{X}$, $V$ is the unique minimal normal subgroup of $X$, so it is characteristic. Any $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(X)$ fixing $G_0$ therefore stabilizes $V$, with $g_\alpha := \alpha|_V \in \operatorname{GL}(V)$, and conjugating $(v, 1)$ by $h \in G_0$ shows $\alpha(h) = g_\alpha h g_\alpha^{-1}$ (by faithfulness of $G_0$ on $V$). Hence $g_\alpha \in N_{\operatorname{GL}(V)}(G_0)$ determines $\alpha((v, h)) = (g_\alpha(v), g_\alpha h g_\alpha^{-1})$ uniquely, proving
   $$\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(Q_{(V \rtimes G_0, Y_0, G_0)}\bigr) \cong N_{N_{\operatorname{GL}(V)}(G_0)}(Y_0).$$
4. **Infinite subfamilies and exact low-order censuses**: Theorems 1.1 and 2.1 immediately establish simplicity across:
   - **(a) Natural special linear modules**: $\mathbb{F}_p^d \rtimes \operatorname{SL}_d(p)$ for all primes $p$ and all $d \ge 3$ ($|Q| = p^d |\operatorname{SL}_d(p)|$). At $(p, d) = (2, 3)$ ($\mathbb{F}_2^3 \rtimes \operatorname{SL}_3(2)$, order $1344$), exhaustive classification (`search_folders.py` Sweep C) yields exactly **$7$** isomorphism classes ($3$ with $Y_0 \cong C_4 \times C_2$ and $4$ with $Y_0 \cong D_8$).
   - **(b) Deleted permutation modules**: $V_{\mathrm{del}} \rtimes A_n$ ($V_{\mathrm{del}} = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{F}_p^n : \sum x_i = 0\} \cong \mathbb{F}_p^n / \mathbb{F}_p \mathbf{1}$ of dimension $n - 1$ since $p \nmid n$) for all $n \ge 5$ and all primes $p \le n$ with $p \nmid n$ (for $p = 2$ and odd $n \ge 5$, $(1,2)(3,4) \in A_n$; for odd $p < n$, a $p$-cycle lies in $A_n$). At $(p, n) = (2, 5)$ ($2^4 \rtimes A_5$, order $960$), there are **$2$** isomorphism classes; at $(p, n) = (3, 5)$, $|Q_{4860}| = 3^4 \cdot 60 = 4860$.
   - **(c) Field-extension modules**: $\mathbb{F}_{p^f}^d \rtimes \operatorname{SL}_d(p^f)$ viewed over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of dimension $d f$ ($d \ge 2$, $(d, p^f) \notin \{(2, 2), (2, 3)\}$). At $d = 2, p^f = 4$ ($\mathbb{F}_4^2 \rtimes \operatorname{SL}_2(4)$, order $960$), there are **$3$** isomorphism classes, giving **$3 + 2 = 5$** total isomorphism classes of affine-simple exact-factorization loops at order $960$.
   - **(d) Non-trivial-center quasisimple modules**: $\mathbb{F}_p^2 \rtimes \operatorname{SL}_2(p)$ for all primes $p \ge 5$ ($Z(\operatorname{SL}_2(p)) \cong C_2$, smallest order $25 \cdot 120 = 3000$ at $p = 5$). $\square$
