## A uniform multitransversal through any elementary layer

Let \(1\ne V\lhd G\) be a proper τ-stable elementary abelian p-subgroup; it need not be minimal. Write
\[L=G/V,\quad J=HV/V,\quad \bar K=KV/V,\quad W=H\cap V,\quad V=V_+\oplus V_-\]
for the induced involution \(\theta\) on L and the τ-eigenspaces. Put \(a=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}V_+\), \(w=\dim W\), \(\delta=a-w\). Strict defect gives \(\delta\ge1\).

The induced \(\bar K\) is the **full θ-inverted subset** of L, by coprime outside-involution lifting. It is not generally a transversal, and no quotient loop is assumed. Each \(\bar k\in\bar K\) has exactly \(|V_-|\) inverted lifts in K. Indeed, for one lift k, the other lifts are \(vk\) with v inverted by the marked involution \(\widehat\tau k\). Every marked involution is G-conjugate to \(\widehat\tau\) in odd order, so its minus eigenspace on the normal module V has the same size as \(V_-\).

Fix \(g\in G\). Every coset of \(H^gV\) contains precisely \(|V|/|W|\) elements of K, because K is a transversal to \(H^g\). It is a union of whole fibers of \(K\to\bar K\), all of size \(|V_-|\). Therefore
\[|\bar K\cap \ell J^{\bar g}|=\frac{|V|}{|W|\,|V_-|}
 =\frac{|V_+|}{|W|}=p^\delta
\quad(\ell\in L).\tag{1}\]
Thus \(\bar K\) is an exact \(p^\delta\)-fold transversal to every conjugate of J. This is a quotient-group counting condition and an affine-fiber interface, not a projected Bol folder or a sufficient lifting criterion.

## The projected stabilizer overlap has nontrivial p-core

Put \(X=J\cap\theta(J)\). It is θ-stable, and its full θ-inverted subset is \(\bar K\cap J\): inversion carries an element of J into θ(J), and \(\bar K\) is the full inverted subset of L. Equation (1) at the identity coset gives
\[|\{x\in X:\theta(x)=x^{-1}\}|=[X:C_X(\theta)]=p^\delta.\tag{2}\]
The fixed-point index equality is the standard coprime outside-involution count.

We use the following elementary group lemma. Let Y be a finite odd solvable group with involution α, and suppose its full α-inverted subset \(K_Y\) has p-power size. Then \(P=\langle K_Y\rangle\) is a normal p-subgroup of Y. Normality follows because \(K_Y\) is the set of α-commutators: every inverted element is \(y^{-1}\alpha(y)\) using an odd square root, and the subgroup generated by automorphism commutators is normal.

For the p-group assertion, induct on \(|Y|\). Choose an elementary abelian minimal normal subgroup \(M\le Y\) of \(Y\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle\). Coprime involution lifting and the constant-fiber argument give
\[|K_Y|=|M_-|\,|K_{Y/M}|.\tag{3}\]
If M is a p-group, induction gives p-group image of P in Y/M and hence P itself is a p-group. If M is a q-group for \(q\ne p\), (3) gives \(M_-=1\), so α fixes M pointwise. Every \(k\in K_Y\) centralizes M: for \(m\in M\), fixedness of \(kmk^{-1}\in M\) gives \(kmk^{-1}=k^{-1}mk\), so oddness makes k centralize m. Thus \(M\cap P\le Z(P)\). Induction makes \(P/(M\cap P)\) a p-group; its central q-group kernel splits as a direct factor by Schur–Zassenhaus. That factor is α-fixed, so every inverted element of P has trivial coordinate in it. Since P is generated by inverted elements, the factor is trivial. Hence P is a p-group. The base case is immediate.

Applying the lemma to (2),
\[1\ne P=\langle\bar K\cap J\rangle\triangleleft X,\qquad P\le O_p(X).\tag{4}\]
Moreover θ is trivial on X/P because \(x^{-1}\theta(x)\) is inverted and lies in P. Coprime fixed-point lifting gives
\[X=P\,C_X(\theta),\qquad |P:C_P(\theta)|=p^\delta.\tag{5}\]
In particular \(O_p(J\cap\theta J)\ne1\). This excludes every quotient stabilizer with trivial p-core in that overlap from being lifted through such a V. It does not assert that P is elementary, normal in all J or L, or that \(\bar K\) defines a quotient loop.

## Consequences for a monolithic canonical group

Assume now that G has a unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup V. Odd-order solvability makes V elementary abelian; uniqueness makes it τ-stable, and every nontrivial normal subgroup contains it. Also \(V\le G'\), since G′ is nontrivial for a nonassociative canonical folder.

The Fitting subgroup \(F(G)\) is a p-group, where p is the characteristic of V: its nontrivial Sylow factors are characteristic in \(F(G)\), normal in G and each must contain V. The same applies to \(F(G')\). If \(G'\) were nilpotent, each of its nontrivial Sylow subgroups would contain V, so \(G'\) would be a p-group. But \(G=HG'\) and \(C_G(\tau)\le G'\) imply \(|G/G'|\) divides \(|H|=|C_G(\tau)|\), hence is also a p-power. Then G and \(|Q|=[G:H]\) are p-powers, contrary to C0011. Therefore
\[G'\text{ is not nilpotent}.\tag{6}\]

Strict defect makes \(V_+\ne0\). If τ is the identity on V, the reviewed fixed-normal lemma makes V central, and minimality makes \(|V|=p\). Conversely the reviewed odd-center theorem fixes \(Z(G)\) pointwise. Thus either V is a central order-p subgroup, or V is noncentral with both eigenspaces nonzero. In the latter case \(Z(G)=1\), and the involution induced on \(G/C_G(V)\) is nontrivial and outer: an inner automorphism of this odd-order group cannot have order two, and a trivial induced action would make both eigenspaces G-invariant, contrary to irreducibility.

Coprime fixed-point lifting also gives
\[|J|=p^\delta |C_L(\theta)|.\tag{7}\]
Hence p divides \(|G/V|\): V is not a normal Hall subgroup. If \(G=H C_G(V)\), H acts irreducibly on V; \(H\cap V\) is H-invariant and proper by core-freeness, so it is zero. The exact multiplicity in (1) is then \(|V_+|\). A transitive V would be regular because it is abelian, giving \(|Q|=|V|\), contrary to C0011. Thus \(HV<G\).

If in addition G′ is metabelian, the independently accepted [nilpotent-residual splitting](odd-metabelian-residual-splitting.md) at `7530ae6eeeb0614d7c94ed6ab1f6761f3493ab88` applies: its nonzero nilpotent residual \(R=\gamma_\infty(G')\) is abelian, \(G'=R\rtimes N\) with N nilpotent, and the action of the specified p′-Hall of N on \(R_p\) has no common nonzero fixed vector. Monolithicity forces R to be a p-group and contain V. Therefore V is noncentral and in the mixed-eigenspace case. This is a structural reduction, not a classification or a proof that such a folder exists.

## An irreducible coprime-complement exclusion

The following further exclusion does not assume that \(1\to V\to G\to L\to1\) splits. Suppose L has a nontrivial θ-stable normal p′-subgroup \(B\le J\) whose restriction on V is irreducible. Then no such simple canonical folder exists.

The H-invariant subspace W is B-invariant. Irreducibility gives W=0 or V; strict defect excludes V, so W=0. In the inverse image of \(B\rtimes\langle\widehat\theta\rangle\) in \(G\rtimes\langle\widehat\tau\rangle\), V is a normal Hall p-subgroup. Schur–Zassenhaus gives a complement, and conjugacy in the inverse image of the order-two subgroup lets its B-part \(B_0\) be normalized by the **actual** \(\widehat\tau\). Since \(W=0\), H contains a B-complement above B. Conjugate H by an element of V to arrange \(B_0\le H\). The original K remains a transversal to every conjugate of the original H, so this temporary alignment preserves \(H\cap K=1\); it does not identify a quotient loop or assume τ fixes the aligned H.

If θ acts nontrivially on B, then some \(b^{-1}\tau(b)\ne1\) belongs to \(B_0\cap K\), contradiction. Otherwise τ fixes \(B_0\) pointwise. Its action σ on V commutes with the irreducible B-action. The endomorphism ring is a finite field, and \(\sigma^2=1\) gives \(\sigma=\pm1\). The negative case contradicts \(V_+\ne0\); the positive case makes V central in G by the fixed-normal lemma. Then the full inverse image of B is \(V\times B_0\), whose unique p′-Hall subgroup \(B_0\) is characteristic. Since B is normal in L, \(B_0\lhd G\), contradicting core-freeness of the aligned H.

Thus any surviving monolithic folder avoids this configuration. The exclusion does not apply merely because B is a p′-subgroup: normality, θ-stability, containment in J, and irreducibility on V are all required. No computation, formal verification, or classification is claimed by this manuscript.

## Parallel affine fibers at defect one

**Independently reviewed continuation.** Retain the elementary normal layer and odd-folder hypotheses above, and suppose
\[\delta=\dim V_+-\dim(H\cap V)=1.\]
No splitting of \(G\to G/V\) or quotient-loop structure is assumed. For every \(x\in G\), put \(H_x=xHx^{-1}\) and \(W_x=H_x\cap V\). Let \(\ell=\pi(x)\), where \(\pi:G\to L=G/V\). By the multitransversal formula (1), the full inverted set \(\bar K\) meets the coset \(\ell J\) in exactly \(p\) elements. For each such element choose a lift \(k_i\in K\) and an \(h_i\in H\) with \(\pi(k_i)=\pi(xh_i)\), and define
\[
 v_i=k_i h_i^{-1}x^{-1}\in V,\qquad
 N_i=\{v\in V:(\operatorname{Int}(k_i)\tau)(v)=-v\}.
\]
Then the \(p\) affine subsets
\[
 v_i+(N_i+W_x)/W_x\subseteq V/W_x                         \tag{8}
\]
are distinct parallel hyperplanes and partition \(V/W_x\). In particular, their directions \((N_i+W_x)/W_x\) coincide. The statement holds for every \(x\) and every choice of the displayed lifts.

Indeed, an element in the fiber of \(k_i\) has the form \(vk_i\), \(v\in V\), and is inverted by \(\tau\) exactly when \(v\in N_i\). The outside involution \(\operatorname{Int}(k_i)\tau\) is conjugate to \(\tau\) in the odd extension, so \(\dim N_i=\dim V_-\). Moreover \((vk_i)H=(v+v_i)xH\). Transversality of \(K\) to the \(H\)-cosets therefore makes the sets (8) disjoint and exhaustive, and makes \(N_i\to V/W_x\) injective. Since \(\dim W_x=\dim V_+-1\), the quotient has dimension \(\dim V_-+1\), so every set (8) is an affine hyperplane. Two affine hyperplanes with different directions intersect; disjointness forces all directions to agree.

Equivalently, for each chosen \(x\), a nonzero line in \((V/W_x)^*\) annihilates all the images of the \(N_i\). The line may vary with the projected coset and with \(x\); no globally invariant functional follows from this argument. This is a necessary condition on the original folder, not a sufficient lifting criterion or an assertion that \(\bar K\) is a quotient transversal.

## An exact defect-one lift criterion

**Theorem with the scoped independent reviews below.** The following makes the preceding necessary affine-fiber condition necessary and sufficient for transversality, with the stabilizer lift retained as one global cochain. It applies only at defect one. It does not assume that the extension splits, that \(H\) is \(\tau\)-stable, or that the quotient defines a loop; it is not an exhaustion of odd canonical envelopes. Faithfulness, generation and the optional simple-loop condition remain separate checks.

Let \(p\) be odd, let \(V\) be an elementary abelian \(p\)-group, and consider an actual finite odd group extension and involution
\[
 1\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow G\xrightarrow{\pi}L\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \tau(V)=V,\quad \tau^2=1.
\]
Write \(S=\tau|_V\), let \(\theta\) be the induced involution of \(L\), and let \(\rho\) be the conjugation action of \(L\) on \(V\). Choose a normalized section \(s:L\to G\), and define \(c(l,m),t(l)\in V\) by
\[
 s(l)s(m)=c(l,m)s(lm),\qquad \tau(s(l))=t(l)s(\theta l).
\]
The actual extension data satisfy \(c(1,l)=c(l,1)=t(1)=0\), \(S^2=1\), \(S\rho(l)=\rho(\theta l)S\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 c(l,m)+c(lm,n)&=\rho(l)c(m,n)+c(l,mn),\\
 S c(l,m)+t(lm)&=t(l)+\rho(\theta l)t(m)+c(\theta l,\theta m),\\
 S t(l)+t(\theta l)&=0.
\end{aligned}                                                    \tag{9}
\]
Conversely, normalized data satisfying these identities, together with the stated action and involutions, define such an extension and involution by the displayed multiplication and \(\tau\)-action.

Choose \(J\le L\) and a \(J\)-invariant subspace \(W\le V\) with
\[
 \dim W=\dim V_+-1.                                               \tag{10}
\]
A subgroup \(H\le\pi^{-1}(J)\) projecting onto \(J\) with \(H\cap V=W\) is specified by a normalized map \(f:J\to V/W\), \(f(1)=0\), satisfying
\[
 f(jk)=f(j)+\rho(j)f(k)+c(j,k)\pmod W.                            \tag{11}
\]
Its elements over \(j\) are \((w+f(j))s(j)\), \(w\in W\). Equation (11) is an affine linear system: it has a solution exactly when the restricted extension class in \(H^2(J,V/W)\) vanishes, and its solution set, if nonempty, is a torsor for \(Z^1(J,V/W)\).

Let \(\bar K=\{l\in L:\theta(l)=l^{-1}\}\). For each \(l\in\bar K\), solve
\[
 (I+\rho(l)S)v=-\rho(l)t(l)-c(l,\theta l).                        \tag{12}
\]
Odd coprime involution lifting supplies a solution \(v_l\). Put \(N_l=\ker(I+\rho(l)S)\). The solutions are \(v_l+N_l\), precisely the inverted elements in the fiber \(Vs(l)\). The outside element \((v_l s(l))\widehat\tau\) is an involution, with action \(\rho(l)S\) on \(V\); all outside involutions are conjugate in the odd extension, so \(\dim N_l=\dim V_-\).

For each coset \(T=tJ\), put \(B_T=\bar K\cap tJ\), and define
\[
 D_T=\operatorname{ann}\!\left(\rho(t)W+\sum_{l\in B_T}N_l\right)\le V^*.
                                                                    \tag{13}
\]
For a selected solution \(f\) of (11), the full inverted set \(K=\{g\in G:\tau(g)=g^{-1}\}\) is a transversal to every conjugate of \(H\) **if and only if** the following hold for every \(T=tJ\):

1. \(|B_T|=p\).
2. \(N_l\cap\rho(t)W=0\) for each \(l\in B_T\), and \(D_T\ne0\).
3. For any nonzero \(\nu_T\in D_T\), the \(p\) values
   \[
    \beta_T(l)=\nu_T\bigl(v_l-c(t,j)-\rho(t)f(j)\bigr),
       \qquad l=tj\in B_T,                                       \tag{14}
   \]
   are pairwise distinct in \(\mathbb F_p\). Here any representatives of the classes \(f(j)\) may be used.

In condition 2, \(D_T\) automatically has dimension one: an injective fiber image already has codimension one in \(V/\rho(t)W\), and a nonzero common annihilator makes every other image share its direction. Thus \(D_T\) is a canonical projective normal line for the coset whenever conditions 1–2 hold.

To prove the criterion, note that the \(H\)-coset of an inverted lift \(vs(l)\), \(l=tj\), is determined by the class of
\[
 v-c(t,j)-\rho(t)f(j)\quad\text{in }V/\rho(t)W.
\]
The quotient has dimension \(\dim V_-+1\) by (10). Conditions 1–3 say exactly that its \(p\) inverted-fiber images are injective, distinct parallel affine hyperplanes, hence partition the quotient. This proves transversality to \(H\). Conversely, uniqueness and coverage of the \(H\)-cosets force condition 1 by the constant inverted-fiber size, then conditions 2–3 by the parallel-hyperplane argument above. In the odd extension the outside involutions form one \(G\)-conjugacy class \(\Lambda=\widehat\tau K\), and \(K^{-1}K=\Lambda^2\) is \(G\)-conjugacy-invariant. Therefore transversality to \(H\) is equivalent to transversality to every conjugate of \(H\). The full inverted set contains the identity, is inverse-closed, and is closed under \(uvu\), so the cited folder interface applies.

For a faithful generating folder add \(\operatorname{core}_G(H)=1\) and \(\langle K\rangle=G\). If a simple loop is required, additionally impose the reviewed strict-defect condition \(|C_N(\tau)|>|H\cap N|\) for every nontrivial proper \(\tau\)-stable normal subgroup \(N\lhd G\). These are separate group and normal-subgroup conditions; the linear transversality test does not by itself imply them.

For fixed \(G,\tau,J,W\), the kernels \(N_l\) and normals \(D_T\) do not depend on \(f\). Each difference of two values in (14) is an affine linear function on the solution space of (11). Its required nonvanishing excludes a proper affine hyperplane when its linear part is nonzero; an identically zero difference excludes the whole solution space, while a nonzero constant imposes no exclusion. Thus the admissible lift cochains are exactly the resulting affine hyperplane arrangement complement, subject to the independent group conditions. The labels for the various quotient cosets are not chosen independently: the single global cochain \(f\) supplies their gluing.

The construction is independent of section choices. If \(s'(l)=b(l)s(l)\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 c'(l,m)&=c(l,m)+b(l)+\rho(l)b(m)-b(lm),\\
 t'(l)&=t(l)+Sb(l)-b(\theta l),\\
 f'(j)&=f(j)-b(j)\pmod W,\qquad v'_l=v_l-b(l).
\end{aligned}
\]
The vector in (14) changes by the common translation \(-b(t)\) for all \(l\in tJ\), so its pairwise differences are unchanged. Replacing \(t\) by another representative of \(T\) gives the same subspace \(\rho(t)W\) and changes the affine labels by a common translation after the natural identification. Replacing \(v_l\) by another solution changes it by \(N_l\), which \(\nu_T\) annihilates.

Generation can also be reduced to linear algebra over \(V\). First require that \(\bar K\) generate \(L\). Use a presentation of \(L\) on symbols for **all** \(l\in\bar K\), with each symbol lifted to the selected \(v_ls(l)\), and evaluate a set of presentation relators in \(V\). Then \(\langle K\rangle\cap V\) is the \(L\)-submodule generated by these relator values and all \(N_l\). Hence generation is equivalent to that submodule being \(V\). Using fewer quotient generators requires including words expressing every other selected inverted lift and their resulting vector differences; otherwise this kernel test can miss generators.

Finally, \(C_G(\tau)\cap N_G(H)\) acts by conjugation on the quotient cosets and their dual data, but a one-orbit reduction is not automatic. In the reviewed Heisenberg control, \(N_L(J)=JZ(T)\) and \(C_L(\theta)\cap N_L(J)=1\), while \([L:J]=49\). Other symmetries may exist in a particular extension; their orbit reductions require a separate proof.
