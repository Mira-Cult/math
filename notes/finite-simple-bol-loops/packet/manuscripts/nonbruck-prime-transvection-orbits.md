## Uniform transvection orbit theorem

**Proposition.** Let d≥3 and q=p^f. Put V=Fq^d as an Fp-space, A=SL_d(q), X=V⋊A. Among regular subgroups Y≤X whose projection π(Y) has order p and is generated by a transvection, exactly two ΓL_d(q)-orbits occur. They are distinguished by whether N=Y∩V contains H=C_V(π(Y)). For d=2 there is exactly one orbit.

*Proof.* Every nonidentity transvection is GL_d(q)-conjugate to u=I+E12, so normalize T=π(Y)=⟨u⟩. Since |Y|=|V|=q^d and |T|=p, N=Y∩V is a T-invariant Fp-hyperplane. The p-group T acts trivially on the one-dimensional Fp-space V/N, so W=[T,V]=Fq e1 is contained in N. Regular subgroups over this T biject with nonzero Fp-linear functionals λ with λ(W)=0: N=ker λ, and ζ(u) is the unique quotient element with λ-value 1. Conversely, any such λ gives N=ker λ and a unique additive isomorphism ζ:T→V/N sending u to that element. The set {(v,t):v+N=ζ(t)} is then a subgroup of order q^d whose intersection with the zero stabilizer is trivial, so it acts regularly.

Write vectors (x,y,z) with z∈Fq^{d−2}; then W=Fq e1 and H={y=0}. The linear normalizer of T comprises the matrices

    g = [[a,b,c],[0,d0,0],[0,E,K]],
    a,d0∈Fq*, K∈GL_{d−2}(q), E a column, c a row,
    a/d0 ∈ Fp*.

Preservation of image W and fixed hyperplane H yields this shape, and conjugation sends E12 to (a/d0)E12. Coordinatewise Frobenius fixes u and, with these matrices, gives the full normalizer in ΓL_d(q). If ζ(u) is the cocycle value and r=a/d0, then the transformed value at u is r⁻¹gζ(u), because gu g⁻¹=u^r and T acts trivially on the quotient. Hence r transforms λ to r λ∘g⁻¹.

The trace pairing uniquely writes λ(x,y,z)=Tr(A0 y+Bz), with A0∈Fq and B a row in Fq^{d−2}, not both zero. For B=0 use d0=A0, a=d0, E=0 and K=I to obtain Tr(y). For B≠0 choose K whose first row is B, E whose first entry is A0, and a=d0=1; then the first coordinate of z'=Ey+Kz equals A0y+Bz, so the transformed functional is Tr(z1). The existence of K follows because every nonzero row extends to a basis. The two orbits are distinct since B=0 iff H⊆N, an intrinsic condition. Both forms are nonzero, including when p divides f, by nondegeneracy of the finite-field trace pairing. For d=2 there is no z-coordinate, so only the first case occurs. This proves the proposition. ∎

**Loop interface.** For d≥3, the natural A-module over Fp is faithful and irreducible. The group SL_d(q) is perfect with nonabelian simple projective quotient in this range, including SL₃(2); its order is divisible by p. Thus C0004 applies, conditional on its precise proof and review status. Moreover End_{Fp A}(V)=Fq: commuting with every I+tEij forces an Fp-linear endomorphism to be scalar by the elementary-root calculation in [the natural SL₃ cocycle manuscript](nonbruck-affine-cocycle-data.md). Every normalizer element preserves this scalar field and, after undoing a field automorphism, lies in GL_d(q); conversely ΓL_d(q) normalizes A. Therefore N_{GL_{df}(p)}(A)=ΓL_d(q), and C0004's folder-isomorphism theorem identifies the two orbit classes exactly with two isomorphism classes of loops in this restricted family, each of order q^d|SL_d(q)|. Neither these orbits nor the loop statement classify regular subgroups of other projection types.

The proof has the separate mathematical reviews below; no computation or fresh primary-source audit of the standard simplicity/perfectness facts was performed. For d=2 the orbit statement holds independently; the C0004 loop application requires the separate quasisimplicity range q≥4.

## Larger root projections: a restricted reduction

Let 1≤s≤f and let S≤Fq be an s-dimensional Fp-subspace. Put T_S={I+tE12:t∈S}. Within the subfamily with π(Y)=T_S and W=Fq e1≤N=Y∩V, all regular subgroups are parameterized by surjective Fp-linear maps λ:V/W→S: set N/W=ker λ, identify V/N with S via λ, and define ζ(u(t))=λ⁻¹(t). The action of T_S on V/N is trivial because (u(t)−1)V⊆W⊆N, and the cocycle condition is precisely the additivity of ζ. Regularity is bijectivity. Conversely, given λ, let N/W=ker λ, let ζ(u(t)) be the unique coset in V/N with λ-value t, and set Y={(v,u(t)):v+N=ζ(u(t))}. Additivity of ζ and triviality of the quotient action make Y a subgroup of order q^d; injectivity of ζ makes Y intersect the zero stabilizer trivially, so Y is regular. Every such λ therefore constructs one Y and determines it uniquely. This deliberately does not assert W≤N for s>1: a nontrivial p-group action on a quotient of dimension s>1 is possible.

For fixed S, the ΓL_d(q)-normalizer consists of g=Dσ, where σ∈Gal(Fq/Fp), D has the flag shape in the preceding proof, and k=a/d0 satisfies kσ(S)=S. Its action on the maps is

    (g·λ)(v+W)=k σ(λ(g⁻¹v+W)).

This follows because g u(t) g⁻¹=u(kσ(t)). Therefore the orbit problem in this restricted larger-root subfamily is exactly the orbit problem for surjective Fp-linear maps V/W→S under these explicit semilinear transformations. Different S are compared using the same formula with kσ(S)=S'. This gives a uniform finite linear-data reduction, not a solved closed orbit count and not an assertion that all Y with π(Y)≤T_S satisfy W≤N. For s=1 the preceding quotient argument forces W≤N and solves the full projected-order-p transvection subfamily.
