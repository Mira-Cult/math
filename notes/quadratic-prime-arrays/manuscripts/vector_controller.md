# A physical rank-two controller and the quadratic prime-array theorem

All labels are manuscript-local. This source is a conventional proof composed
with the exact analytic and local interfaces below. The [provenance record](../PROVENANCE.md)
distinguishes review of this argument from review of the ordinary proof. No explicit numerical threshold is asserted.

## 1. The result and its hypotheses

Use exactly the canonical pools in [PROBLEM.md](../PROBLEM.md). Write
$`O=\mathbb Z[i]`$ or $`\mathbb Z[\omega]`$, $`D=2`$ or $`3`$, and


```math
A=A(n,D),\quad M=n^D,\quad B=\max_{z\in A}N(z),\quad
 R=\sqrt B,\quad T=\sum_{z\in A}z,\quad m=T/M,\quad \mu=nm.
```


All supremum norms below are in the fixed coefficient basis $`(1,i)`$ or
$`(1,\omega)`$. The symbol $`B`$ is the norm cutoff and $`R`$ the geometric
radius; neither is the scalar rational-prime cutoff of the old proof.
The actual $`T`$ includes all inert entries and any cut conjugate norm tie.

**Theorem QPA.** There are constants $`n_G,n_E`$ such that:

1. For every odd $`n\ge n_G`$, the first $`n^2`$ canonical nonramified Gaussian
   prime representatives have a bijective square placement with all rows,
   columns and both long diagonals prime; the first $`n^3`$ representatives
   have a bijective cube placement with every full signed-coordinate line prime.
2. The same two assertions hold for the Eisenstein pool for **every integer**
   $`n\ge n_E`$, with no parity or modulo-three restriction.
3. Gaussian even orders $`n\ge2`$ are impossible. The singleton arrays are
   respectively $`1+2i`$ and $`2`$.

The constants are ineffective in this proof. The theorem is about prime
**elements**, not prime norms alone. The constructed large targets lie in
an interior subcone and hence are split prime elements, while all prescribed
inert entries are still present exactly once. Target sums may repeat.

The analytic inputs are Lemmas W,T,S,G,P,M,U of
[analytic_inputs.md](analytic_inputs.md): prime boxes with the enclosing-area
error and exceptional character paid; fixed three-form exact-total targets;
elementary disjoint gap families; a finite multiscale prefix; and centered
four-pair supply. Lemma T uses Kai, arXiv:2306.16983v5, Theorem12.1 with
$`t=3,d=4`$, not a growing affine system. The arithmetic and even-order
incidence statements are in [local_arithmetic.md](local_arithmetic.md).

The geometric imports are the public [signed trades](../../sudes-first-conjecture/cube/signed_trades.md), Sections 1, 2, 4;
[full cube composition](../../sudes-first-conjecture/prime-cube.md), Section 4;
[axis controller](../../sudes-first-conjecture/cube/cube_controller.md), Sections 3, 4;
and [incidence](../../sudes-first-conjecture/cube/incidence.md), the tensor-expander
coefficient theorem. Square geometry uses the public
[finite controller](../../sudes-first-conjecture/explicit-controller.md),
Section3. These are geometry and integer-flow imports only. None of their
rational-prime distribution or scalar subset-sum conclusions is transferred.

## 2. Compatibility and the fixed congruence obstruction

Let


```math
\mathcal D=\begin{cases}(1+i)O,&O=\mathbb Z[i],\\O,&O=\mathbb Z[\omega].\end{cases}
```


This is the additive group generated by all differences in sufficiently long
canonical pools. Gaussian entries all have residue1 modulo $`1+i`$; hence an
order-$`n`$ line has residue $`n`$. For even $`n\ge2`$ a line sum is divisible by
$`1+i`$ and has real part at least$`n`$, excluding all associates of $`1+i`$.
It is composite. For odd$`n`$, a large nonramified prime target has the same
residue1. In the Eisenstein ring no restriction is imposed by $`\mathcal D=O`$.
These claims, including actual small witnesses for the difference groups,
are proved in the local-arithmetic source.

For a square, compatible targets consist of row and column vectors with
both totals exactly$`T`$; the two diagonal values are free. For a cube write
$`U_d`$ for the axis tensor omitting coordinate$`d`$, and
$`F_{ij,\epsilon}(s)`$ for the slice-diagonal family with varying coordinates
$`i,j`$, relation $`x_j=x_i`$ or $`x_j=n-1-x_i`$, and remaining coordinate$`s`$.
The necessary equations are the pairwise axis plane marginals, common
axis directional total$`T`$, and the six equations


```math
\sum_s F_{ij,\epsilon}(s)
 =\sum_t U_k(t,\rho_\epsilon(t)),\qquad
 \rho_+(t)=t,\quad\rho_-(t)=n-1-t,                        \qquad\text{(1)}
```


where$`k`$ is the remaining axis. They express two partitions of the same
diagonal square. No primality of that square total is requested.
The four body targets have no further equation when$`n\ge10`$.
The local source proves the full integer image for every$`n\ge10`$, including
even$`n`$: five noncentral reflection orbits suffice. The same integer maps
operate on each coefficient in$`O`$, or indeed on any abelian group.

We prove the following stronger physical statement. Put $`\ell=\log R`$.

**Controller V.** For all sufficiently large$`n`$, every compatible target
vector in$`O`$ within


```math
H=\frac{nR}{\ell^{400}}                                  \qquad\text{(2)}
```


of$`\mu`$ is realized by a permutation of exactly$`A`$, provided in the
Gaussian case each target has residue$`n`$ modulo$`1+i`$. There is no
extra target congruence restriction for Eisenstein arrays.

Thus local admissibility is not defined circularly. The final eventual
classification is odd Gaussian orders and all Eisenstein orders. At finite
orders the additional obstructions in the local source remain real.

## 3. From independent increments to a complete finite reservoir

We supply the step missing from a mere rank-two spanning argument.
Take$`U=2R`$ and a fixed$`L=C\ell`$ large enough that every set left after
the total reservation below has cardinality at least$`U^2/L`$. This follows
from$`M\sim R^2/(2\log R)`$ and a reservation of$`o(M)`$ points.
Apply analytic LemmaG once at$`H_s=\lceil10^6L^2\rceil`$ (enlarging the
constant for this harmless rounding). Select two independent seed gaps
$`g_1,g_2`$, and set


```math
G=[g_1\ g_2],\quad \Gamma=G\mathbb Z^2,\quad
 d=|\det G|,\quad q=d\mathcal D.
```


Both$`g_j`$ are actual differences of pool entries, so$`\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal D`$.
The exact lattice index in$`O`$ is$`d`$, and


```math
d\mathcal D\subseteq dO\subseteq\Gamma,
 \quad d=O(\ell^4),\quad
 \|G\|_\infty=O(\ell^2),\quad
 \|G^{-1}\|_\infty=O(\ell^2),\quad
 \kappa_G:=\|G\|_\infty\|G^{-1}\|_\infty=O(\ell^4).       \qquad\text{(3)}
```


The adjugate formula proves the inclusions and inverse estimate. The ideal$`q`$
has norm$`O(\ell^8)`$, hence at most$`\ell^{10}`$ eventually. We never assume
that$`\Gamma`$ itself is an ideal or physically multiply an available pair.

Reserve all required seed copies first from the two disjoint matchings.
There are$`O(n^{D-1})`$ main reservoirs, each with a polynomial-logarithmic
seed demand. Their combined demand is$`o(M/\ell)`$ and fits the matchings
of size$`\gg M/\ell`$. Only the demanded submatchings are taken. All later
pair choices pay for these reserved endpoints in their deletion allowance.

For one reservoir use LemmaP to build an uncontrolled prefix that can make
every signed change in$`\Gamma\cap[-20R,20R]^2`$. At every dyadic scale
reserve the bounded number of identical-gap copies prescribed there.
A switch copy means one pair for an ordinary transfer, two equal-gap pairs
for a rectangle, or four equal-gap pairs for a cuboid. At each scale LemmaG
provides$`\gg M/\ell`$ disjoint pairs after arbitrary preceding deletions,
far more than the polynomial-logarithmic copies needed in one request.
Every copy uses new endpoints. If$`2b`$ copies of a gap are reserved, start
with$`b`$ switched and$`b`$ unswitched. This gives all signed coefficients
between$`-b`$ and$`b`$ by ordinary subset selection. The prefix center$`p_0`$
is half its total gap and lies in$`\Gamma`$ exactly. The number of stages
is$`O(\ell^4+\ell^{3/2}\log R)`$; we use the coarse eventual envelope


```math
P=\lceil\ell^{100}\rceil.                               \qquad\text{(4)}
```


Prefixes may use points anywhere in the pool. Their midpoint error is
charged at the full$`O(R)`$ per cell; it is not treated as centered.

### Complete two-dimensional capacity from centered macros

For an integer$`K\to\infty`$ let$`J=\lceil1000K\rceil`$. Append$`J`$ macro
stages from each of the two coefficient-direction cones supplied by LemmaM:


```math
\delta_{jt}\in dO\subseteq\Gamma,\qquad
 \|\delta_{jt}-(R/100)e_j\|_\infty\le2\eta R,
 \quad j=1,2,\quad1\le t\le J,\qquad\eta=\ell^{-140}.    \qquad\text{(5)}
```


For each stage obtain one, two, or four simultaneously disjoint equal-gap
pairs, according to its physical type. All their midpoints are within
$`\eta R`$ of the actual mean$`m`$. The matches for different stages and
reservoirs are also disjoint, by deletion before each next choice.

**Lemma Z (bounded vector subset completeness).** For all sufficiently
large$`R,K`$, the prefix and macros have a realizable subset$`q_0\in\Gamma`$
with distance$`O(\ell^2)`$ from half the total gap. Starting from this
subset or its complement, every vector in$`\Gamma\cap[-KR,KR]^2`$ is
an attainable signed native change. Both reference states give each pair
its actual midpoint in expectation when chosen with equal probability.

**Proof.** Let$`S`$ be the sum of macro gaps and consider their centered
zonotope


```math
Z=\left\{\sum_{j,t}(t_{jt}-1/2)\delta_{jt}:0\le t_{jt}\le1\right\}.
```


For every real linear functional$`v=(v_1,v_2)`$ its support function is
$`\frac12\sum|v\cdot\delta_{jt}|`$. Equation(5) bounds this below by


```math
JR(1/200-2\eta)(|v_1|+|v_2|).
```


Once$`\eta\le1/800`$, this is at least$`(JR/400)\|v\|_1`$.
The support-function separation criterion implies
$`[-2KR,2KR]^2\subseteq Z`$, since$`J\ge1000K`$.

Choose$`q\in\Gamma`$ nearest to$`S/2`$ by rounding its two$`G`$-coordinates.
Then$`\|q-S/2\|_\infty\le\|G\|_\infty/2=O(\ell^2)`$.
For every$`x\in\Gamma\cap[-KR,KR]^2`$, the vector$`q+x-S/2`$ lies
in$`Z`$ eventually. Therefore the bounded linear system
$`\sum t_{jt}\delta_{jt}=q+x`$, $`0\le t_{jt}\le1`$, has a solution.
At an extreme point at most two coordinates are strictly between0 and1:
three fractional columns would have a nontrivial linear dependence, allowing
both a small positive and negative perturbation. Round those at most two
coordinates to0 or1. The residual$`r=q+x-\sum\epsilon_{jt}\delta_{jt}`$
is in$`\Gamma`$ and has supremum norm at most$`4R`$. The prefix realizes
$`r`$ relative to$`p_0`$. Thus$`p_0+q+x`$ is a subset sum of the whole
reservoir. In particular$`q_0=p_0+q`$ is realizable. This proves the
claimed box around$`q_0`$.

The complement of any realizable subset is realizable. Hence the same
symmetric box occurs about the complementary center. The two centers
differ by$`S-2q`$, of size$`O(\ell^2)`$. Complementing every switch exchanges
the two endpoints of every pair, including all paired copies in a rectangle
or cuboid. A fair choice consequently gives the pair midpoint at each cell.
All coefficients have been decoded by actual finite subset choices. QED.

No assertion that arbitrary subset sums of two independent vectors contain
a box is being used. The prefix resolves the bounded rounding residue, and
the macro zonotope supplies the large capacity. The resulting reservoir has
$`O(P+K)`$ stages and full native capacity$`\Gamma\cap[-KR,KR]^2`$.

## 4. Modular routing into the actual correction lattice

Use the fixed$`\mathbb Z`$-basis of$`\mathcal D`$


```math
(v_1,v_2)=\begin{cases}((1,1),(1,-1)),&\text{Gaussian},\\
 ((1,0),(0,1)),&\text{Eisenstein}.
 \end{cases}
```


Each$`v_j`$ is a difference of actual ring units, hence a difference of two
unit residue classes modulo$`q=d\mathcal D`$. Analytic LemmaU applies to
this ideal as well as$`(d)`$: its proof uses only ideal-lattice counts and the
unit-difference criterion. It supplies four equal actual gaps in the
specified difference class after the same deletion allowance. Choose smaller
subsets for one- and two-pair switches. Reserve$`d-1`$ switches for each
basis direction in every path/rectangle/body routing type.

A vector error in$`\mathcal D`$ has unique integer$`v`$-coordinates. Reduce
both coordinates modulo$`d`$. Path prefix sums route any zero-total vector
of such errors; double path prefix sums route a zero-row/column-margin
tensor. Choosing representatives between0 and$`d-1`$ gives nonnegative
finite switch counts. The actual gap of a chosen switch differs from$`v_j`$
by an element of$`q`$, so these switches correct the errors modulo$`q`$.
After routing, all line residuals belong to$`q\subseteq\Gamma`$.

For a cube the order is plane totals, the two slice directions, the remaining
axis tensor, bodies, then six face paths. Compatibility ensures the zero
margins at each phase. Body switches preserve axes exactly; after them each
face family still has the prescribed total modulo$`q`$, by(1). Every face
switch preserves the other five families, all bodies and all axes. The
square uses separate row/column paths and isolated diagonal rectangles.
These are exactly the finite integer routing maps of the ordinary proof,
applied to both$`v`$-coordinates with distinct physical switches.

In the Gaussian case every initial line error lies in$`\mathcal D`$ under
ControllerV's residue hypothesis. Whole-plane errors and all induced
residuals do too. In the Eisenstein case this is automatic. Actual swaps
always change entries by differences in$`\mathcal D`$. No division by a
nonunit in$`O`$, or independent permutation of the two coordinates, occurs.

The routing uses$`O(n^{D-1}d)`$ entries, plus$`O(nd)`$ new signed-cube
entries, and$`O(d)`$ cells per required line under the placement below.
Every line's numerical change is$`O(dR)`$. It is paid as a real vector change,
not only a residue change. Main-reservoir supports are untouched.

## 5. Physical geometry and simultaneous disjoint placement

All gadget identities are identities in an abelian group, so the following
transfers apply to an actual element gap$`\delta\in O`$.

- A two-cell pair swap changes its two native sums by$`+\delta,-\delta`$.
- A rectangle made from two distinct pairs of the same gap uses the patterns
  $`[[p,p+\delta],[q+\delta,q]]`$ and
  $`[[p+\delta,p],[q,q+\delta]]`$. It preserves both row and column sums;
  its four cell changes are$`\delta[[1,-1],[-1,1]]`$.
- A cuboid uses four distinct pairs placed on parallel edges with alternating
  orientations. Flipping all four changes vertex$`(i,j,k)`$ by
  $`\delta(-1)^{i+j+k}`$, preserving every axis line.

The phrase same gap means equality of both integer coordinates. A scalar
incidence column alone is never substituted for the one/two/four physical
pairs paid above.

Fix on every relevant level a loopless multigraph of degree at most64 and
edge expansion at least8. The ordinary32-permutation counting proof gives
such a graph for every$`n\ge2`$; exhaustive finite graph search is a possible
construction. Integral cut-flow gives scalar edge coefficients of size at
most a fixed constant times the largest zero-total vertex demand. Apply this
to$`G`$-coordinates. In coefficient supremum norm the resulting vector edge
flow costs at most$`C\kappa_G`$ times the demand. Thus a native vector box
of radius$`KR`$ handles all zero-total vertex demands$`o(KR/\ell^4)`$.
This explicitly pays the change of lattice basis.

### Square placement

Use one ordinary reservoir on each edge of the row and column graphs,
at parameter$`K_2`$ below, and one rectangle reservoir for each long diagonal,
at parameter$`K_3`$. Place the two diagonal bundles first with mutually
distinct rows and columns across all their main and residue stages.
For a designated diagonal cell$`(i,f(i))`$ choose a second row$`j`$ and
column$`k`$ avoiding used coordinates and the at most finitely many reflected
coincidences. It is the only cell on its chosen diagonal and no cell lies
on the other diagonal. This works whenever the total stage count is$`o(n)`$,
for odd or even$`n`$. Every row/column has at most two of these cells.

Place row and column prefix, macro and residue passes separately, using
the degree and transverse-column caps of FC Section3. They avoid both
diagonals. The stage counts are respectively$`O(P),O(K_2),O(d)`$ per
bundle; all are$`o(n)`$. The cap proof excludes a fixed small fraction plus
$`o(n)`$ choices, so all supports are disjoint. It bounds every row/column
load in each pass by a constant times that pass's stage parameter. A diagonal
has only its own bundle. Each nonnative line meets an ordinary reservoir
in at most one pair or has a fixed pair sum. These facts control random
reference fluctuations. This is the FC geometric proof, with vector stages
and changed counts; it uses no order parity assumption.

### Cube placement and its even-order extension

Use the ordinary three axis-control levels:

1. For each edge between two$`z`$-planes, $`n`$ level0 reservoirs, with their
   transverse$`(x,y)`$ locations a partial matching within each reservoir.
2. In every$`z`$-slice, independent row and column graph reservoirs at level1;
   stages in a bundle have distinct transverse coordinates.
3. For every ordered pair of$`x`$- and$`y`$-graph edges, one level2 rectangle
   reservoir; its stages have distinct$`z`$ levels.

These give at most$`32n^2,64n^2,1024n^2`$ reservoirs respectively, with
constant changes from separately labeled modular paths. Their exact native
identities are those of the ordinary axis controller. The two-coordinate
arithmetic only changes stage payloads, not the supports.

Add level3 isolated cuboid reservoirs on the edges of the six face-family
graphs and one on each of the four bodies. For a plus$`xy`$ face transfer
between levels$`u,v`$, use
$`\{a,b\}\times\{a,c\}\times\{u,v\}`$, with$`a,b,c`$ in distinct
noncentral reflection orbits avoiding$`u,v`$. Its only nonaxis action is
$`\delta(e_{F(u)}-e_{F(v)})`$. Reflections give the other five families.
For a body use$`\{a,b\}\times\{a,c\}\times\{a,d'\}`$ with four
separate noncentral orbits. Only the intended body changes. Its six face
side effects occur at the corresponding four fixed levels.

Place these new cuboids first by the pinned signed-trade packing lemma,
with prefix/macro/residue passes kept separate. Different stages of a body
bundle use globally fresh reflection orbits within that bundle. Consequently
any face line meets at most one cell of each body bundle, across all stages.
Any face line has total drift$`O(R)`$ from each body's entire correction.
The packing proof counts forbidden values in reflection orbits of size two;
for even$`n`$ there is no central value to exclude, so each exclusion bound
only decreases. The finite requirement$`n\ge100H_0(J_{\rm stages}+1)`$
continues to hold eventually since$`J_{\rm stages}=o(n)`$. In particular
no odd-cardinality argument is imported at this step.

Keep each old macro level (level0, level1 and level2) in its own
separate pass, so its collateral line load is O(K0), O(K1) or O(K2),
respectively, rather than merely the largest global load.
Pack the old plane/slice/rectangle supports around the cuboids using the
old-support extension lemma in signed_cube.md Section4. Plane supports avoid
all diagonal sections; old slice and rectangle supports avoid all bodies.
They have$`O(P),O(K_2),O(d)`$ cells per face line in their separate passes.
A face line meets at most one stage of any one old slice or rectangle
reservoir, and therefore has range$`O(R)`$ under its complementary references.
The proof uses only bounded graph degrees, reflection exclusions, planned
plane totals and$`o(n)`$ occupied cells on a fiber; it also works for even$`n`$.
Its exceptional axes lying inside diagonal sections are retained and paid
by the same face caps. No generic-position assumption discards these axes.

The resulting support contracts are: all supports disjoint; no old numerical
axis stage touches a body; new face bundles preserve all nonnative required
sums exactly; body spill on a given face is$`O(R)`$; and per-line
prefix/macro/residue loads are$`O(P),O(K_3),O(d)`$. The constants are fixed
before$`n`$ grows. Stages reserved as upper-bound slots but not needed are
returned to filler assignment. Values are chosen after supports are planned.

## 6. A single joint quantitative budget

Take, with all floors positive on a sufficiently large tail,


```math
K_0=\lfloor n/\ell^{390}\rfloor,\quad
 K_1=\lfloor n/\ell^{350}\rfloor,\quad
 K_2=\lfloor n/\ell^{310}\rfloor,\quad
 K_3=\lfloor n/\ell^{270}\rfloor,\quad
 \eta=\ell^{-140},\quad P=\lceil\ell^{100}\rceil.          \qquad\text{(6)}
```


These intentionally generous exponents are a concrete, consistent asymptotic
schedule, not a numerical cutoff or an optimized bound. All fixed geometric
constants, the two macro cones, all prefix copies, and the four-pair cuboid
factor are included in the implied constants of the following invoices.

| Item | Square | Full signed cube |
|---|---:|---:|
| Main reservoirs | $`O(n)`$ | $`O(n^2)`$ old plus $`O(n)`$ new |
| Ordinary/rectangle/cuboid pairs per stage |1 or2|1,2 or4|
| Total reserved entries $`Q`$ | $`O(nK_2+K_3+nP+nd)`$ | $`O(n^2K_2+nK_3+n^2P+n^2d)`$ |
| Common eventual bound | $`O(M/\ell^{310})`$ | $`O(M/\ell^{310})`$ |
| Seed-pair demand per direction | $`O(nP)`$ | $`O(n^2P)`$ |
| Available seed matching | $`\gg M/\ell`$ | $`\gg M/\ell`$ |
| Largest per-line macro count | $`O(K_3)`$ | $`O(K_3)`$ |
| Prefix and residue counts per line | $`O(P+d)`$ | $`O(P+d)`$ |

In both dimensions$`Q=o(M)`$; in particular fewer than half the input entries
are reserved. Every supply invocation is made against this **entire** invoice,
including all seeds, both directions and all future supports. At every step
the actually deleted set has cardinality at most$`Q`$.

The smallest prime boxes have radius$`\eta R`$, area$`\asymp\eta^2R^2`$,
and modulus norm at most$`\ell^{10}`$. LemmaW gives before deletion at least


```math
\gg\frac{M}{\ell^{290}}.                               \qquad\text{(7)}
```


Since$`Q=O(M/\ell^{310})`$, at least half remains eventually in every
required class and box, uniformly in all adaptive prior choices. LemmasM/U
then give at least$`\gg M/\ell^{291}\to\infty`$ equal-gap disjoint pairs
for each new request. Four fresh pairs therefore exist. We do not reserve
an entire bank; each request removes only its actual one/two/four pairs.
The same reasoning applies to each dyadic prefix request, using global
density rather than short-window distribution. Norm ties are immaterial
because every macro/residue box is strictly below$`(1-\epsilon)R`$;
prefix and seed points come directly from the frozen pool.

Each stage is thus given distinct actual entries before the filler permutation.
The square and cube point scales are respectively


```math
R\asymp n\sqrt{\log n}\quad(D=2),\qquad
 R\asymp n^{3/2}\sqrt{\log n}\quad(D=3).                  \qquad\text{(8)}
```


All placement requirements hold since$`K_3+P+d=o(n)`$.

## 7. Centering the actual complete bijection

For every main reservoir independently choose one of the complementary
reference subsets of LemmaZ with probability one half. Leave modular gadgets
in fixed reference states. Every macro cell has expected value equal to its
pair midpoint, within$`\eta R`$ of$`m`$. Every prefix/residue cell costs
$`O(R)`$. If$`r_L`$ cells of a required line are reserved and their sum is$`C_L`$,
then


```math
\|\mathbb E C_L-r_Lm\|_\infty
       =O(\eta K_3R+PR+dR).                             \qquad\text{(9)}
```


A single independent reservoir choice changes a native sum by$`O(\ell^2)`$
by LemmaZ, a nonnative line meeting at most one/two stage vertices by$`O(R)`$,
and a preserved line by zero. The support contracts in Section5 cover every
axis, face and body line, including exceptional diagonal-section axes.
Thus$`O(R)`$ bounds the contribution range per reservoir per coordinate.
At most$`O(K_3+P+d)`$ reservoirs meet any line, by its reserved-cell count.
Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over the two coordinates and all
$`O(n^2)`$ required lines give a joint choice satisfying


```math
\|C_L-r_Lm\|_\infty\le E,
 \qquad E=O\left(\eta K_3R+PR+dR+
                  R\sqrt{(K_3+P+d)\log n}\right).       \qquad\text{(10)}
```


The independent variables are whole reservoir choices, not individual cells
or the lines. Every outcome retains the full signed capacities of LemmaZ.

There remain$`M_0\ge M/2`$ unused entries. Summing(10) over one partition
into axis lines and using the exact total$`T`$ gives, for their mean$`m_0`$,


```math
\|m_0-m\|_\infty\le2E/n.                              \qquad\text{(11)}
```


Uniformly permute **all** remaining entries into the unoccupied cells.
The expected completed line sum is within$`3E`$ of$`\mu`$. Each coordinate
of a pool entry lies in a fixed interval of length$`O(R)`$. The
sampling-without-replacement bounded-difference inequality therefore bounds
one line's tail at$`u`$ by$`2\exp(-u^2/(C nR^2))`$. Taking
$`u=C'R\sqrt{n\log n}`$ with a sufficiently large fixed$`C'`$ and applying
a union bound gives a completion with every required line error at most


```math
E_0=O\left(\eta K_3R+PR+dR+
 R\sqrt{(K_3+P+d)\log n}+R\sqrt{n\log n}\right)=o(H).    \qquad\text{(12)}
```


Indeed$`\eta K_3R=O(nR/\ell^{410})=o(H)`$, and every other displayed
ratio is a fixed logarithmic power divided by a positive power of$`n`$.
This uses$`\log R\asymp\log n`$ from(8). All pool entries now occur
exactly once. The modular routing adds$`O(dR)=o(H)`$, placing every residual
in$`\Gamma`$ while retaining the same centering estimate.

## 8. Consuming the vector capacities

A target in ControllerV's box initially has error at most$`(1+o(1))H`$
after centering and modular repair. The following operations use disjoint
main supports, all still at their reference states when consumed.

### Square

Rows and columns have zero-total residuals in$`\Gamma`$. Apply integral
flow separately to their$`G`$-coordinates. The actual vector edge flow is
$`O(\kappa_GH)=O(\ell^4H)=o(K_2R)`$. LemmaZ consumes it by a legal
subset of its reservoir. Row corrections preserve every column and both
diagonals; column corrections preserve every row and both diagonals. The
two diagonal errors remain$`O(H)`$ and are consumed by their isolated
