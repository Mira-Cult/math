## Exact ordinary result

The ordinary SC3 statement is a permutation of exactly the first n³ odd primes,
for every sufficiently large odd n, with all 3n²+6n+4 deduplicated signed-coordinate
lines prime. Repeated sums are allowed. Its stronger USC3 theorem realizes every
compatible odd target vector within K0 X of the mean, where K0=ceil(n^(3/5)).
Compatibility includes every axis plane marginal and six face-family totals;
bodies have no additional equation for odd n≥11. The source does not claim
prime square sections, shorter lines, arbitrary slopes, a numerical cutoff or
a growing dimension. It composes an actual permutation controller with a
separate prime-target theorem.

Its scale X is the largest rational prime, asymptotic to3n³log n. It uses
K0=n^(3/5), K1=n^(2/3), K2=n^(3/4), K3=n^(39/50), delta=n^(-1/5),
polylogarithmic prefixes and a growing common scalar gap. The target source
uses a repeatable exact-sum prime-list lemma plus the Thorner–Zaman short-interval
progression consequence, with an ineffective threshold. The proof therefore
cannot be transferred by substituting the word Gaussian for rational.

## Two changes that carry the new proof

First, an additive rank-two lattice is not a bounded physical correction
region. A few independent pairs only span their integer combinations formally.
The new prefix pays finitely many disjoint copies at every dyadic scale and
rounds a two-dimensional residual down through those scales. Centered large
gaps then form a zonotope; rounding its at-most-two fractional coordinates
leaves a small lattice residual that the prefix actually realizes. The same
subset choices operate both coordinates of each prime element.

Second, prime density in separate target boxes does not preserve an exact pool
total. The new target construction reserves three weight-one positions and
uses the forms x,y,T−x−y, whose restrictions to each other's kernels have
finite cokernel. Every other orbit is greedily balanced in both coordinates.
This reduces the growing list to a fixed three-form theorem with uniform
polylogarithmic coefficients. A two-prime completion would fail that source's
independence hypothesis.

The windows are Y/(log Y)^A with Y~nR. They are wide enough to dominate an
enclosing-scale error, and fit inside nR/(log R)^400 by choosing exponent401.
The controller consequently uses logarithmic stage scales instead of the
old fractional powers. Its full deletion and rounding invoice is in the
canonical [controller proof](vector_controller.md), Sections6–8.
