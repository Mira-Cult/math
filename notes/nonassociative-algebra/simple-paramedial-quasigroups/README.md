# Every simple paramedial quasigroup is finite

*Mira Cult · Problem 0338*

A quasigroup has unique solutions to each of the equations `a*x=b` and `y*a=b`. We consider its multiplication and both division operations. Simplicity means that equality and the universal relation are its only congruences for this signature. A quasigroup is **paramedial** if `(x*y)*(u*v)=(v*y)*(u*x)` for all four inputs.

**Theorem.** There is no infinite simple paramedial quasigroup.

The negative answer was already publicly claimed in [adrunkhuman's formalization project](https://github.com/adrunkhuman/infinite-simple-paramedial-quasigroups-proof). This note gives a separate mathematical proof, using a commutative ring normalized by the coefficient automorphisms, rather than the central trace ring in that project's account. We make no priority claim and have not certified its Lean build.

## From a congruence to a module

The unrestricted affine representation theorem of Kepka and Němec expresses a paramedial quasigroup on an abelian group $A$ as

```math
x*y=\phi(x)+\psi(y)+g,\qquad \phi^2=\psi^2,
```

where $g\in A$ and $\phi,\psi$ are automorphisms of $A$. No finiteness assumption is needed for the representation. A precise statement in this convention is [Shcherbacov and Pushkashu, Theorem 1.2, p. 358](https://cmuc.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/cmuc1002/shcherb.pdf), citing P. Němec and T. Kepka, *T-quasigroups, I*, Acta Univ. Carolin. Math. Phys. **12** (1971), no. 1.

Let $R$ be the unital subring of $\operatorname{End}(A)$ generated by $\phi,\psi$ and their inverses. An $R$-submodule $U$ gives a congruence by taking cosets modulo $U$. Multiplication on $A/U$ uses the induced automorphisms and the image of $g$. The divisions are also induced, since solving either affine equation applies an inverse coefficient automorphism. This is a quotient in the full quasigroup signature. In particular it is cancellative, so the argument also excludes an infinite quasigroup with no nontrivial cancellative congruences. We do not need to identify that condition with any definition using multiplication alone.

If $A$ is nonzero and the quasigroup is simple, $A$ is therefore a simple left $R$-module.

## Two translates of a maximal submodule

Set $t=\phi\psi^{-1}$ and $s=\phi^2=\psi^2$. The element $s$ commutes with both generators, and

```math
\phi t\phi^{-1}=\phi^2\psi^{-1}\phi^{-1}=\psi\phi^{-1}=t^{-1}.
```

Consequently $B=\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1},s,s^{-1}]$ is a commutative subring normalized by $\phi$. In fact $R=B+B\phi$ as an additive sum: the right side is a subring by normalization and $\phi^2\in B$, and contains $\phi^{-1}=s^{-1}\phi$ and $\psi=t^{-1}\phi$ together with $\psi^{-1}$. We do not require $B$ to be central or the sum to be direct. If $v$ is any nonzero element of $A$, then $A=Rv$, so $v$ and $\phi(v)$ generate $A$ over $B$.

Choose a maximal proper $B$-submodule $U$. Finite generation ensures that a union of a chain of proper submodules is still proper, so such a maximal submodule exists. Its quotient is a simple module over the commutative ring $B$, hence is isomorphic to $B/\mathfrak m$ for a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. This is a finite field, by the argument below.

The subgroup $\phi(U)$ is again a $B$-submodule. Since $s$ and $s^{-1}$ belong to $B$, $\phi^2(U)=U$. Thus $U\cap\phi(U)$ is stable under $B$ and both $\phi$ and $\phi^{-1}$, and is a proper $R$-submodule. Simplicity makes this intersection zero. The diagonal quotient map now embeds the additive group $A$ in $A/U\times A/\phi(U)$. These two factors have the same finite order, since $\phi$ exchanges them. In particular $|A|\le |B/\mathfrak m|^2$. The one-element quasigroup is finite as well.

## Why the residue field is finite

A field $k$ finitely generated as a ring over $\mathbb Z$ is finite. In positive characteristic, Zariski's lemma over the prime field gives a finite extension of a finite field. If the characteristic were zero, the field would also be finitely generated as an algebra over $\mathbb Q$, so Zariski's lemma would make it a number field. There is a positive integer $N$ such that every one of the finitely many ring generators is integral after multiplication by $N$; this follows by clearing denominators in their monic rational equations. Their ring is contained in $\mathcal O_k[1/N]$. For any rational prime $\ell$ not dividing $N$, that ring does not contain $1/\ell$, since this would make $N^j/\ell$ a rational algebraic integer for some $j\ge0$. This contradicts $k$ being a field of characteristic zero. Applying this to $B/\mathfrak m$ completes the proof.

The proof settles existence, not a new classification of finite simple paramedial quasigroups. For the finite structure results, see §2.2 of the cited paper.
