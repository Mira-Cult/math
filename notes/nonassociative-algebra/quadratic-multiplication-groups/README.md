# A nonassociative Moufang variety with quadratic multiplication groups

**Mira Cult**

There are loop varieties with nonassociative finite members of unbounded order in which every finite multiplication group has order at most the square of the loop order. Here is an explicit family with an exact counting formula. The exponent two is necessary for the varieties constructed here, even when restricted to the variety generated by a single finite example.

## Statement

For a loop $`Q`$, let $`\mathrm{Mlt}(Q)`$ be the permutation group generated by all left and right translations. Fix an odd prime $`p`$. Let $`A`$ be an algebra over $`\mathbb F_p`$, not necessarily with a multiplicative unit, with bilinear multiplication satisfying
```math
xy=-yx,\qquad x(yz)=-(xy)z
```
and in which all products of four factors vanish, in every bracketing. On the set $`A`$, define
```math
x*y=x+y+xy.
```
These loops form a variety in the ordinary loop signature. They are Moufang, and for every finite such loop
```math
|\mathrm{Mlt}(Q)|=|A|\cdot|A/\mathrm{Ann}(A)|\le |Q|^2,
```
where $`\mathrm{Ann}(A)`$ is the two-sided annihilator. For every $`m\ge 1`$ the variety contains a nonassociative example of order $`n=p^{6m+1}`$ whose multiplication group has order $`n^2/p`$. In particular no bound $`Cn^\delta`$ with fixed $`\delta<2`$ holds throughout this variety.

## 1. Recovering the algebra from the loop

Write $`\ell_x(z)=xz`$ and let $`T_x`$ be additive translation by $`x`$. Anti-commutativity gives $`xx=0`$, because the characteristic is odd. Anti-associativity then gives $`\ell_x\ell_y=-\ell_{xy}`$ and $`\ell_x^2=0`$. The loop translations are
```math
L_x=T_x(I+\ell_x),\qquad R_x=T_x(I-\ell_x).
```
Both are invertible, and $`0`$ is a two-sided unit. In particular both divisions in the loop are algebra terms:
```math
x\backslash y=(I-\ell_x)(y-x),\qquad y/x=(I+\ell_x)(y-x).
```

Conversely, an iterated loop power of $`x`$ is the corresponding integer multiple of $`x`$: products between scalar multiples of $`x`$ vanish. Write $`r`$ for the fixed positive representative of $`1/2`$ modulo $`p`$. If $`s=x+y`$ and $`b=xy`$, then $`x*y=s+b`$ and $`y*x=s-b`$. The identities give $`x(xy)=0`$ and $`y(xy)=0`$, so $`sb=bs=0`$, while $`ss=bb=0`$. Consequently the loop term $`(x*y)^r*(y*x)^r`$ equals $`s`$. Powers also recover the other scalars, including additive negation. The original algebra product $`xy`$ is now recovered by subtracting $`x+y`$ from $`x*y`$ in the recovered addition.

These two translations by terms show that the class is a variety rather than merely a family of examples. Explicitly, translate the algebra axioms into loop terms and also impose the identities equating the original loop operations with their algebra-term definitions. A loop satisfying these identities yields an algebra of the stated class under the recovered terms, and its loop operations are recovered back. Thus the theorem applies to every member of the variety, including subloops and homomorphic images.

## 2. The exact multiplication group

The family of affine permutations $`T_a(I+\ell_b)`$ is a group: the linear parts multiply by the rule
```math
(I+\ell_b)(I+\ell_c)=I+\ell_{b+c-bc},
```
and $`I-\ell_b`$ is the inverse of $`I+\ell_b`$. The family contains all loop translations. Conversely $`R_x^{-1}L_x=(I+\ell_x)^2=I+2\ell_x`$ is in the multiplication group. As $`x`$ ranges over $`A`$, this gives every linear part, since multiplication by two is invertible. Then $`T_x=L_x(I+\ell_x)^{-1}`$ is also in the multiplication group. Thus the family of affine permutations is exactly $`\mathrm{Mlt}(Q)`$.

The translation part is determined by the image of $`0`$, and $`\ell_b=\ell_c`$ exactly when $`b-c\in\mathrm{Ann}(A)`$, by anti-commutativity. This proves the order formula.

## 3. Nonassociative examples and sharpness

Let $`V=\mathbb F_p^3`$ and take the graded vector space
```math
A_0=\Lambda^1 V\oplus\Lambda^2 V\oplus\Lambda^3 V.
```
On homogeneous inputs of degrees $`(1,1)`$ or $`(2,1)`$, use the exterior product. For degrees $`(1,2)`$, use minus the exterior product. All other products are zero. The signs make the multiplication anti-commutative. The only possibly nonzero triple products have three degree-one inputs, for which the two bracketings differ by a sign. Fourfold products vanish. For a basis $`x,y,z`$ of $`V`$, the additive difference between the two bracketings of their loop product is
```math
(x*y)*z-x*(y*z)=(xy)z-x(yz)=2x\wedge y\wedge z\ne0.
```
This is an actual finite nonassociative loop.

For sharpness, start with the direct sum of $`m`$ copies of $`A_0`$, fix an orientation in each copy, and quotient by the top-degree hyperplane of vectors whose coefficients sum to zero. This hyperplane is an ideal because all top-degree vectors annihilate the algebra. The quotient has dimension $`6m+1`$ and one-dimensional annihilator. Indeed a nonzero degree-one component can be multiplied by a vector of degree one in its own summand to give a nonzero degree-two component. If all degree-one components vanish, any remaining nonzero degree-two component can be tested against a degree-one vector in its summand to give a nonzero top-degree contribution supported only there; such a contribution survives the sum-zero relation. Only the residual top-degree line annihilates everything. A basis triple in a single summand still witnesses nonassociativity. The order formula now gives $`n^2/p`$ as claimed. These examples are homomorphic images of direct powers of the loop associated to $`A_0`$, so they also belong to the subvariety generated by that single finite loop.

## 4. The Moufang identity

The trilinear map $`T(x,y,z)=(xy)z`$ is alternating. Interchanging the first two inputs gives a sign by anti-commutativity; for the last two, use anti-associativity and anti-commutativity on the inner pair. In the expansion of $`(x*y)*(z*x)`$ all fourfold products and repeated-variable triple products vanish. The two triple terms that remain are $`(xy)z+y(zx)=0`$ by the alternating rule. In $`x*((y*z)*x)`$ they are $`(yz)x+x(yz)=0`$ by anti-associativity. In both expansions the degree-two terms sum to $`yz`$, so both results equal $`2x+y+z+yz`$. This proves the middle Moufang identity throughout the variety.

## Context and reproducibility

The [conference problem as indexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_problems_in_loop_theory_and_quasigroup_theory#Bound_on_the_size_of_multiplication_groups) asks for a polynomial bound in some variety. Literally, groups suffice: left and right translations commute across sides, and in a group every element of the multiplication group is the product of one left and one right translation. The condition here that nonassociative finite members exist in unbounded orders is a strengthening. No claim of priority for this family or this strengthened statement is made.

For background on anti-associative algebras see Elisabeth Remm’s [Anti-associative algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10812) and [Anti-commutative anti-associative algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04092). The finite-field construction and group-order argument here do not rely on a characteristic-zero structural theorem. [Three concepts of nilpotence in loops](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13490), by Žaneta Semanišinová and David Stanovský, concerns structural nilpotence of multiplication groups, which is distinct from the size bound here.
