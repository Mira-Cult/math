# The Frattini subloop of a finite Moufang loop

*Mira Cult · Problem 0325*

**Theorem.** In every finite Moufang loop $L$, the intersection $\Phi(L)$ of its maximal proper subloops is normal and centrally nilpotent. For the trivial loop we take $\Phi(L)=L$.

Here normal means the kernel of a loop homomorphism, and central nilpotence means that the upper central series of the subloop itself reaches the whole subloop. The statement answers [Grishkov's Loops ’11 conjecture, §1.12](https://www.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~loops11/problem_session.pdf) and includes the nilpotence assertion in [Grishkov and Zavarnitsine, *Groups with triality*, Conjecture 5](https://www.ime.usp.br/~grishkov/papers/grtrial.pdf). We make no priority claim. The proof first establishes nilpotence for normal subloops already contained in $\Phi(L)$, then proves normality of $\Phi(L)$ by induction. This separation matters: nilpotence of the Frattini subloop of a smaller quotient need not, by itself, give its normality.

## 1. Triality and exact kernels

We use the group-with-triality construction of Grishkov and Zavarnitsine, [*Groups with triality*, Lemmas 1–4 and the extended inner pseudoautomorphism group after Corollary 1](https://www.ime.usp.br/~grishkov/papers/grtrial.pdf). Let $S=\langle\sigma,\rho\rangle\cong S_3$ act on a group $G$, where $\sigma$ is a transposition and $\rho$ a 3-cycle. We write the operators on the left and put $[g,\sigma]=g^{-1}\sigma(g)$. For every such label $m$, triality says $m\rho(m)\rho^2(m)=1$; the three factors commute, and $\sigma(m)=m^{-1}$. The labels form the Moufang loop $\mathcal M(G)$ with label product $(\rho(m))^{-1} n(\rho^2(m))^{-1}$.

In particular $[\rho^2(m),\sigma]=m$, so for every $S$-invariant subgroup $B\le G$ we have $\mathcal M(B)=B\cap\mathcal M(G)$. If $B$ is also normal, the map of label loops onto $\mathcal M(G/B)$ has kernel $\mathcal M(B)$. For any subloop $A$ of $\mathcal M(G)$ the $S$-subgroup $K_A$ generated by its labels has label loop $A$. More generally $[B,S]$ is generated as an $S$-subgroup by $\mathcal M(B)$. See also Lemma 7 of the Sylow paper linked below. These facts apply also to quotient groups with a nonfaithful operator action.

For a finite loop $L$, take its finite adjoint group $G=\mathcal E(L)$, with $\mathcal M(G)=L$, $[G,S]=G$, and no nontrivial normal $S$-subgroup fixed pointwise by $S$. Hence a normal $S$-subgroup of $G$ with trivial label loop is trivial: $\sigma$ and all its $S$-conjugates fix that subgroup. If $C$ is normal in $L$, it lifts to a normal $S$-subgroup $W$ of $G$ with $\mathcal M(W)=C$. To see the lift, use the map to $\mathcal E(L/C)$ in the extended inner pseudoautomorphism model. The coordinate permutations are generated by the translations and composite translations defining the inner pseudoautomorphisms; they act on the quotient fibres, and both these permutations and their companions descend. The multiplication and the two triality operators in the model descend with them. The kernel has label loop $C$. If $C$ is a minimal nontrivial normal subloop, choose a minimal lift; any proper normal $S$-subgroup of that lift has label loop $1$ by minimality, and so is trivial. Thus $W$ is a minimal normal subgroup under $G$ and the operators together.

We use two established structure inputs from [Grishkov and Zavarnitsine, *Sylow's theorem for Moufang loops*, arXiv:0709.2696](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.2696). By Lemmas 4 and 6, an $S$-simple factor with nonabelian simple group components has an $S$-invariant ordinary Sylow 2-subgroup. Its label loop is trivial (a single factor fixed by $S$), a nonabelian simple group (a triple of factors with $S$ permuting sites), or a nonassociative Paige loop. In the third case its label loop has even order and 2 is a Sylow prime in the terminology of that paper. Theorem 3 gives the minimal normal subloop classification and describes the group-type-radical-free loops used below. We also use the central nilpotence of finite Moufang $p$-loops; see [Drápal, *A short proof for the central nilpotency of Moufang loops of prime power order*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2023.07.025).

## 2. The normal part of the Frattini subloop

We prove a group statement first. Let $G=[G,S]$ be a finite group with triality, and $U\triangleleft G$ be $S$-invariant. Assume that $G=UK$ with $K$ an $S$-invariant subgroup forces $K=G$. Then the normal closure $D_G(U)$ of $[U,S]$ in $G$ is nilpotent. We do not assert the same of $U$.

The nonsupplement condition passes to quotients by normal $S$-subgroups inside $U$, and to those subgroups themselves. In a chief series for $G$ and $S$ inside $U$, suppose there is a nonabelian factor $W$. Work in the corresponding quotient and take an $S$-invariant ordinary Sylow 2-subgroup $J$ of $W$, orbit by orbit on its simple factors, by §1. It is not normal in $W$. The ordinary group Frattini argument gives $G=W N_G(J)$; the normalizer is proper and $S$-invariant, contrary to the condition. Thus $U$ is solvable.

Induct on $|U|$, the trivial case being immediate. A minimal nontrivial normal $S$-subgroup $W$ inside $U$ is an elementary abelian $p$-group irreducible under the joint quotient and operator action. Set $K=G/W$. By induction $D=D_K(U/W)$ is nilpotent. Its Hall $p'$-subgroup $V$ is characteristic in $D$, hence normal in $K$ and $S$-invariant. Moreover $V$ is generated by the $K$-conjugates of $[V,S]$. Indeed $D$ is normally generated by the $S$-translates of the labels of $U/W$; these lie in $\mathcal M(D)$ by exact intersection. Projection along the Sylow $p$-factor sends them to actual labels of $V$ and is equivariant for $K$ and $S$.

We claim that $V$ acts trivially on $W$. If it did not, irreducibility would give $W^V=0$. Choose an ordinary Hall complement $J$ to $W$ in the preimage of $V$. Schur–Zassenhaus conjugacy makes $G\rtimes S=W N_{G\rtimes S}(J)$, and the intersection equals $C_W(J)=0$. Comparing the external $S$ with the lift of $S$ in this normalizer gives a cocycle in $H^1(S,W)$. For $p>3$ it is a coboundary by averaging. The next two paragraphs treat the bad characteristics. Once it is a coboundary, a $W$-conjugate of $J$ is $S$-invariant. Its normalizer in $G$ is a proper $S$-invariant supplement to $W$, contradicting the hypothesis.

### Characteristic two

Conjugate the complements so that the two lifts of $\rho$ agree, by coprime conjugacy. Write $\sigma=w\widetilde\sigma$ with $w\in W$, where $\widetilde\sigma$ normalizes $J$. The $S_3$ relations give $\rho(w)=w$. In characteristic two $P=1+\rho+\rho^2$ projects onto $W^\rho$, and linearized triality $(\sigma-1)P=0$ makes $\sigma$ the identity there; also $P(\sigma-1)=0$. Let $Z$ be the center of the nontrivial nilpotent image of $J$ on $W$, and $J_Z$ its preimage in $J$. The center is invariant under $G,S$, and $W^Z=0$ by faithfulness of the image and irreducibility. For $j\in J_Z$ fixed by $\widetilde\sigma$, the actual $\sigma$-label of $j$ belongs to $\mathcal M(W)=(\sigma-1)W$. Calculating this label with $\sigma=w\widetilde\sigma$ and using also $j^{-1}$ gives $P(jw)=w$, where $jw$ denotes the action.

Over an algebraic closure decompose $w$ into $Z$-weights. Each nonzero sum over a $\rho$-orbit is fixed by $\rho$, hence by $\sigma$, so its orbit is $\sigma$-stable. It has size one or three, with a $\sigma$-fixed weight $\chi$. Its $\chi$-component is nonzero, and $\chi$ is nontrivial because $W^Z=0$. The fibre of a nonidentity value of $\chi$ in $J_Z$ has odd cardinality and is invariant under $\widetilde\sigma$, so contains a fixed $j$. In the $\chi$-component of $P(jw)$, the size-one orbit contributes $\chi(j)w_\chi$; for size three the other two eigenvalues coincide since their weights are $\sigma$-paired, and cancel in characteristic two. Thus again the value is $\chi(j)w_\chi\ne w_\chi$, impossible. Therefore $w=0$.

### Characteristic three

We show $H^1(S,W)=0$ in the active situation above. We may pass to the faithful action image of $K$ on $W$, and replace $V$ by its image; normality, nilpotence, coprimality and the active generation property are preserved. Extend scalars to an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_3$; testing cohomology after scalar extension is valid since the finite linear equations are over $\mathbb F_3$.

We first give a weight-transport observation. Take an abelian $3'$, $S$-invariant subgroup $A$ of the image, normalized by an $S$-invariant subgroup $K_0$. Decompose $W$ by $A$-characters. An orbit of $\rho$-moving characters makes no contribution to $H^1$, since the corresponding orbit sum is induced from a stabilizer of order at most two and Shapiro's lemma applies. Every $\rho$-fixed character is $\sigma$-fixed: apply triality to $[a,\sigma]$, $a\in A$, and evaluate its three rotations at the character, giving $\chi([a,\sigma])^3=1$, hence $\chi([a,\sigma])=1$. Thus a nonzero obstruction supplies an $S$-fixed weight $\chi$ whose space has nontrivial $\sigma$-action. Indeed, if $\sigma$ were trivial there, the $S_3$ relations force $\rho$ trivial too, and $H^1$ of a trivial module vanishes because $S_3^{\rm ab}$ has order two.

For $k\in K_0$, lift $k$ and apply genuine group triality to the label of $wk$, where $w$ lies in this weight space and $(\sigma-1)w\ne0$. Writing $m=[k,\sigma]$ in $K$, the coefficient of $w$ in the triality identity is
```math
(1+m\rho+m\rho(m)\rho^2)\,k^{-1}(\sigma-1)w=0.
```
Here group elements denote their conjugation actions on $W$. This follows by expanding the $W$-part of the label, $k^{-1}(\sigma-1)w$ plus the baseline part independent of $w$, and then expanding the three rotated labels. The cocycle components of the lifts alter only the constant term because $W$ is abelian. The three nonzero terms have weights obtained from $\chi$ by $k^{-1}$, $m\rho(k)^{-1}$, and $m\rho(m)\rho^2(k)^{-1}=(\rho^2\sigma(k))^{-1}$. They must have the same weight, since otherwise one of the nonzero summands is alone in a direct summand. The first and third imply that $k^{-1}\chi$ is fixed by $\rho^2\sigma$. Use the two rotated triality identities as well, choosing a vector on which the relevant conjugate transposition is nontrivial. Then every weight in the $K_0$-orbit is fixed by all of $S$.

Apply this with $K_0=K$ to each abelian characteristic subgroup $A$ of a Sylow subgroup $P$ of $V$, including $Z(P)$. The whole $K$-orbit just found is $S$-fixed, so the generators $[K,S]$ act trivially on that orbit, and $K=[K,S]$. The Frobenius conjugates of the fixed weight are also fixed and exhaust the occurring weights by rational irreducibility. Their common kernel on $A$ is trivial by faithfulness, and each has the same kernel. Thus $A$ is cyclic, central in the action image, and fixed pointwise by $S$.

For a nonabelian $P$, this forces class two: if the class is $c\ge3$, the characteristic subgroup $\gamma_{c-1}(P)$ is abelian since its self-commutator lies in $\gamma_{2c-2}(P)=1$, yet is not central. Write $q\ne3$ for the prime of $P$. If the derived exponent were $q^a$ with $a\ge2$, the $q^{\lceil a/2\rceil}$-th powers would generate an abelian characteristic subgroup by the class-two commutator formula, and therefore be central. Their commutators with arbitrary elements would force a smaller derived exponent, a contradiction. Hence $Z=Z(P)$ is cyclic, $P'$ has order $q$, and $X=P/Z$ is elementary abelian. The commutator gives a nondegenerate alternating $S$-invariant form on $X$. An abelian Sylow factor of $V$ is $S$-fixed, and must be trivial by projection of the active generation condition. Likewise, if $\rho$ acts trivially on $X$, its action on $P$ is a central twist in the $q$-group $\operatorname{Hom}(X,Z)$, so is trivial. Then triality makes every $\sigma$-label in $P$ have cube one, hence trivial. Active generation again rules out that $P$. Some nontrivial Sylow factor thus has a moving part for $\rho$.

That moving part is orthogonal to the fixed part (average over $\rho$) and is $T\otimes Y$ for the two-dimensional standard $S_3$-module $T$. For odd $q$, this follows from Maschke's theorem and the standard representation. For $q=2$, regard the moving part as a vector space over $\mathbb F_4$ where $\rho$ is multiplication by a primitive cube root and $\sigma$ is the semilinear Frobenius; descent gives the same decomposition. The invariant form factors as the unique invariant pairing on $T$ tensored with a form on $Y$. For odd $q$ the first pairing is symmetric, so the form on $Y$ is alternating. For $q=2$, take $u\ne0$ in the image of $\sigma-1$ on $T$. For each $y$ the vector $u\otimes y$ is the projection of a genuine label in $P$, which commutes with its $\rho$-rotate. Since $u$ and $\rho(u)$ are independent, their pairing on $T$ is nonzero, and the equality forces the multiplicity form to vanish on $(y,y)$; it is again alternating.

Thus $T\otimes\langle y\rangle$, for any nonzero $y$, is an $S$-active isotropic space. Its preimage $A$ in $P$ is abelian, $S$-invariant and normal in $P$. Apply weight transport with $K_0=P$. Any obstruction gives an $A$-weight whose whole $P$-orbit is $S$-fixed. The weight's restriction to $Z$ is faithful, as already established. Nondegeneracy of the commutator form makes its $P$-orbit consist of all extensions of this restriction to $A$: the ratios of the translates run through the full character group of $A/Z$. Thus $S$ acts trivially on that dual group and on $A/Z$, contradicting activity. This completes the Hall argument in characteristic three.

### The conclusion for normal subloops

Now $V$ acts trivially on $W$. Its preimage is a direct product of $W$ and a unique Hall $p'$-subgroup $J$, which is normal and $S$-invariant. The preimage of the Sylow $p$-subgroup of $D$ is a normal $p$-subgroup, and together these direct factors give the entire preimage of $D$. It is nilpotent, hence so is its subgroup $D_G(U)$. This completes the induction.

If $F\triangleleft L$ lies in $\Phi(L)$, take $U$ to be the lifted kernel of $L\to L/F$. If $G=UK$ for an $S$-subgroup $K$, its label loop surjects onto $L/F$. If $\mathcal M(K)$ were proper, a maximal subloop containing it would also contain $F$, yet still cover $L/F$, impossible. Thus $K=G$ by $S$-generation. The preceding theorem applies, and $\mathcal M(D_G(U))=\mathcal M(U)=F$. Characteristic Sylow factors split the nilpotent group $D_G(U)$ as a direct product of $S$-groups, so $F$ is a product of centrally nilpotent finite Moufang $p$-loops. In particular $F$ is centrally nilpotent.


## 3. Detecting the action of a Frattini label

We now prove normality by induction on $|L|$. Let $C$ be a minimal nontrivial normal subloop, with a minimal lift $W$ as in §1, and put $Q=L/C$. For any maximal subloop $M$ not containing $C$, the image of $M$ in $Q$ is all of $Q$ by maximality. Its $S$-label group $K_M$ therefore satisfies $G=W K_M$, since the quotient is $S$-generated. Put $J=C_G(W)$, a normal $S$-invariant subgroup.

It suffices to prove that all labels in $\Phi(L)$ centralize $W$. For then $\Phi(L)\subseteq\mathcal M(J)$. For each $M$ not containing $C$, the group $J\cap K_M$ is normalized by $K_M$ and centralized by $W$; hence it is normal in $G=W K_M$ and $S$-invariant. Its label loop is the actual intersection $M\cap\mathcal M(J)$. The maximals that contain $C$ correspond exactly to the maximals of $Q$. Consequently, for the quotient map $\pi$,
```math
\Phi(L)=\pi^{-1}(\Phi(Q))\cap\mathcal M(J)
       \cap\bigcap_{M\not\supseteq C}\mathcal M(J\cap K_M).
```
All factors are normal by induction and the kernel identities. If every maximal contains $C$, we can instead conclude immediately from the quotient correspondence.

### Abelian minimal normal subloops

If $C$ is abelian, $W$ is elementary abelian, say of characteristic $p$. Indeed, a nonabelian characteristically simple $W$ would decompose into $S$-orbits of simple group factors. An active orbit contributes a nonabelian simple group or a Paige loop, not an abelian loop; at least one orbit must be active. In the elementary abelian case the extension by $C$ has an affine product on its fibres. For representatives $m_x$ of $x\in Q$, write the labels over $x$ as $m_x\cdot c=\lambda_x(c)m_x$, where $\lambda_x$ is conjugation on $W$ by $(\rho(m_x))^{-1}$ and $c\in C\subseteq W$. The label law is jointly affine in the two $W$-variables: moving $W$-terms to the left only applies linear conjugations, the operator $\rho$ and its powers, and addition. The two coefficient maps take values in the output fibre's linear coordinate space, by testing each variable separately. Each is bijective by loop division and equal fibre sizes. In the resulting coordinates the product is
```math
(x,a)(y,b)=(xy,\phi_{x,y}(a)+\psi_{x,y}(b)+c_{x,y}),
```
with each coefficient map an automorphism of the additive fibre. This is the affine extension in [Drápal and Vojtěchovský, *Abelian congruences and solvability in Moufang loops*, Theorem 2.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03646), rather than an assumption that any abelian subloop induces an abelian congruence.

An actual supplement $M$ of $C$ has $M\cap C$ normal in $L$. To see this, choose an element in each $M$-fibre and right-multiply by it to identify the unit fibre $D=M\cap C$ with that fibre. Each fibre is a coset of a subgroup $V_x$ obtained by an affine coefficient map from $D$. Multiplying elements of $M$ and varying one coordinate at a time gives
$\phi_{x,y}(V_x)\subseteq V_{xy}$ and $\psi_{x,y}(V_y)\subseteq V_{xy}$; equal orders give equality. Thus equality in the first coordinate and difference in $V_x$ in the second defines a congruence. Both divisions are compatible by applying inverses of these coefficient maps. Its unit class is $D$. Minimality of $C$ now makes each maximal $M$ omitting $C$ a genuine complement.

Assume one exists, and choose its label group $K$. Then $G=WK$ and $\mathcal M(W\cap K)=1$. The intersection is normal in $K$, centralized by the abelian $W$, and $S$-invariant, hence trivial by reduction. Thus $K\cong G/W$. A Frattini label $m$ is in $K$ and also in $wKw^{-1}$ for each $w\in W^S$, since these are proper $S$-subgroups with proper label loops. Injectivity of $K\to G/W$ shows $wmw^{-1}=m$: its $w$-conjugate and $m$ belong to $K$ and have the same projection. Hence $m$ fixes $W^S$.

The image of $m$ in $Q$ lies in $\Phi(Q)$, by pullback of maximals. By induction $\Phi(Q)$ is normal. The $S$-generated group $K$ has a normal $S$-kernel $U$ lifting $\Phi(Q)$, using its map to the reduced adjoint group of $Q$ and then to that of $Q/\Phi(Q)$. The nonsupplement argument of §2 applies in $K$, so $D_K(U)$ is nilpotent and contains $m$. Its Sylow $p$-subgroup is characteristic, thus normal under $K$ and $S$, and acts trivially on $W$: a $p$-group acting on a nonzero space in characteristic $p$ has a nonzero fixed space, and here it is jointly invariant. Therefore $m$ has action on $W$ of order prime to $p$.

We show that this action must be trivial. The action group $A$ generated by $m$ and $\rho(m)$ is abelian of order prime to $p$, and $S$-invariant. Over an algebraic closure take an occurring character $\chi$. If $\rho$ moves it, for each $w$ in its space the sum $w+\rho(w)+\rho^2(w)$ is $S$-fixed by linearized triality, with $\chi$-component $w$. Thus $\chi(m)=1$. If $\rho$ fixes $\chi$, triality gives $\zeta^3=1$ for $\zeta=\chi(m)$. If $\zeta\ne1$, then $p\ne3$ and $\sigma$ sends $\chi$ to a different character. Choose a nonzero $\rho$-eigenvector $w$ in the $\chi$-space with eigenvalue $\eta$. For $k=m^j$ one can choose $j$ with $\zeta^{-2j}\eta=1$. Put $a=[k,\sigma]=m^{-2j}$. Applying the coefficient identity of §2 to $wk$, with $a$ in place of its label, and projecting to the $\chi$-space gives $-3k^{-1}w=0$: the $\sigma(w)$ term belongs to the distinct $\sigma(\chi)$-space, and $a\rho$ acts as one on $k^{-1}w$. This is impossible. All weights of $m$ are therefore trivial. The criterion above proves normality.

### Nonabelian associative minimal normal subloops

Here $W$ is a product of simple groups. The active $S$-orbits are triples permuted by $S$, each contributing a nonabelian simple direct factor to $C$; the other factors are individually fixed by $S$. The diagonal group on each triple centralizes $S$, so its conjugations induce actual automorphisms of $L$ acting on the corresponding factor of $C$ by inner group automorphisms. As $\Phi(L)$ is characteristic, $\Phi(L)\cap C$ is normal in the group $C$ and thus is a product of some whole simple factors.

In fact this intersection is trivial. Choose an $S$-invariant ordinary Sylow 2-subgroup $D$ of $W$, using identical Sylows on each permuted triple and arbitrary Sylows on inactive factors. It is not normal in $W$. Its normalizer $N=N_G(D)$ is a proper $S$-subgroup with $G=WN$ by the ordinary group Frattini argument, hence $G=W[N,S]$ by $S$-generation in the quotient. Let a maximal subloop $M$ contain the proper $\mathcal M(N)$. Then $K_M$ contains $[N,S]$. If $\Phi(L)\cap C$ contained a simple factor, $K_M$ would contain its entire $S$-generated triple. The action of $[N,S]$ together with $S$ on the factors of $W$ is transitive, by minimality of $W$ and the fact that $W$ acts without permuting its own factors. Thus $K_M$ would contain all of $W$ and equal $G$, impossible.

The subgroup $W^S$ acts by loop automorphisms trivially on $Q$. Since $\Phi(L)\to Q$ is injective, $m\in\Phi(L)$ commutes with $W^S$. Its conjugation fixes the inactive factors pointwise and preserves each active triple, fixing its full diagonal pointwise. Every automorphism of a direct product of nonabelian simple groups permutes the factors and then applies automorphisms within them. The diagonal condition forces the internal automorphisms in a triple to be trivial in the common labelling, leaving only a site permutation.

On a triple the permutation cannot be a transposition, because $\sigma(m)=m^{-1}$ would force it to be the designated transposition of $S$, whose $\rho$-rotates do not commute. If it is a three-cycle, take $k=m^j$ so that $a=[k,\sigma]=m^{-2j}$ acts on that triple by $\rho^{-1}$. Choose $w$ in one of the two factors swapped by $\sigma$, of prime order different from three; such a prime occurs since a nonabelian finite simple group is not a 3-group. For $u=k^{-1}[w,\sigma]k$, the label of $wk$ is $ua$. The actual triality identity for $ua$, using that for $a$, gives
```math
u\,\operatorname{Ad}(a)\rho(u)\,\operatorname{Ad}(a\rho(a))\rho^2(u)=1.
```
Since $a$ acts as $\rho^{-1}$ on this triple, the product is $u^3$. But $u$ has the same non-three prime order as $w$, a contradiction. Thus $m$ centralizes $W$, and the criterion proves normality in this case too.

## 4. Exhausting the minimal normal cases

The group-type radical is the largest normal subloop all of whose composition factors are groups. By [the Sylow paper, Theorem 3(ii)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.2696), a finite Moufang loop with trivial group-type radical is a subdirect product of finite Paige loops and enlarged Paige loops. We show below that they are Frattini-free. Pulling back maximals through each coordinate surjection forces the Frattini subloop into the intersection of the coordinate kernels, hence makes it trivial. If the radical is nontrivial, take $C$ minimal nontrivial normal in $L$ inside it. The minimal-normal classification in Theorem 3(i) makes $C$ an ordinary group, either elementary abelian or a product of nonabelian simple groups. These are exactly the cases of §3. This completes the induction on normality, and then §2 with $F=\Phi(L)$ proves central nilpotence.

### Why the Paige factors are Frattini-free

Use the norm-one units in the split octonion algebra $O$ over $\mathbb F_q$ modulo its norm-one scalars, the usual Paige model. Every nonscalar unit lies in a nondegenerate quaternion subalgebra. If the quadratic algebra generated by it is étale, take a nonzero-norm perpendicular vector and use the Cayley–Dickson formula. Otherwise subtract its scalar part to obtain $e\ne0$ with trace and norm zero and $e^2=0$. Choose a trace-zero $f_0$ with polar norm pairing $B(e,f_0)=-1$, possible by nondegeneracy and independence of $1,e$. Adding a multiple of $e$ makes its norm zero, giving $f^2=0$ and $ef+fe=1$. Artin's two-generator theorem and the matrix-unit relations now give a split quaternion algebra. All finite-field nondegenerate quaternion algebras are split.

The split quaternion subalgebras are conjugate under $O$-automorphisms, and every automorphism of one extends to $O$. Indeed, take an isomorphism of quaternion algebras and add perpendicular vectors of equal nonzero norm. The perpendicular space in a Cayley–Dickson decomposition has norm a nonzero scalar times the split quaternion norm, which represents every nonzero value. Extending the isomorphism by this choice respects the Cayley–Dickson products. This also extends the conjugations inside the projective norm-one subgroup $\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)$ of a quaternion algebra. Therefore a nontrivial characteristic Paige subloop contains a nontrivial normal subgroup of such a $\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)$. For $q\ge4$, simplicity and quaternion conjugacy give the entire Paige loop.

For completeness, the two small fields use the following elementary calculation in the Zorn vector-matrix model; a tuple $(a,u,v,b)$ has trace $a+b$ and norm $ab-u\cdot v$. Every nonidentity element has projective order two or three by quaternion membership. For $q=2$, an involution normally generates $\operatorname{PSL}_2(2)=S_3$. If a characteristic subloop starts with order three, the unique order-three conjugacy class in $\operatorname{PSL}_2(2)$, together with quaternion conjugacy, gives it $(1,e_1,e_1,0)$ and $(0,e_2,e_2,1)$ for distinct coordinate vectors. Their product has trace zero, top-left entry zero and norm one, so is a nonscalar involution. For $q=3$, an order-three element normally generates $\operatorname{PSL}_2(3)=A_4$. Starting instead with an involution, the unique class of involutions and quaternion conjugacy supply $(0,e_1,-e_1,0)$ and $(1,e_2,e_1+e_2,-1)$. Their product has trace one and norm one; it is nonscalar since the second matrix is not a scalar multiple of the inverse of the first. Its quadratic polynomial has repeated root $-1$, so its projective order is three. Thus both small fields give the entire loop as well. As the Frattini subloop is proper and characteristic, it is trivial.

For odd $q$, the enlarged Paige loop is the invertible octonion units modulo all nonzero scalars. The norm square-class map has the Paige loop as kernel and quotient $C_2$, so the Frattini is in the kernel. Invariance under octonion automorphisms and the preceding quaternion argument make it either trivial or the full kernel. But the projective unit group $\operatorname{PGL}_2(q)$ of a split quaternion subalgebra is a proper subloop surjecting onto $C_2$ by determinant. A maximal containing it cannot contain the whole kernel, which excludes the second alternative. For even $q$, the enlarged loop equals the Paige loop because every nonzero norm is a square.
